Wichita Storm History

What has Wichita weather done to roofs?

Wichita sits in one of the most active hail corridors in the country. These are a few of the storms the National Weather Service in Wichita still points to when it talks about roof damage in the city.

01

September 15, 2010: the Kansas record hailstone

On the evening of September 15, 2010, a supercell crossed the west side of the metro and dropped a hailstone near Pawnee and 119th Street West that measured 7.75 inches across. That stone still stands as the Kansas state record, according to the National Weather Service in Wichita.

The same storm laid a swath of large hail from the Goddard area across south Wichita toward Udall. Federal storm records put the damage around Goddard alone near $150 million. Roofs built before that night were often replaced in 2010 and 2011, which means many west-side shingle roofs are now reaching the age where wear starts to show.

02

April 24, 2006: the costliest hailstorm in years

In April 2006, hail of two to three inches fell across west Wichita. The Wichita NWS office estimated about $70 million in damage and called it the city's costliest hail event since 1992.

Storms like that one are why insurance claim paperwork, adjuster meetings and color-matching questions come up so often in Wichita roof conversations. A neighborhood can go from normal wear to dozens of claims in a single afternoon.

03

July 8, 2009: hail downtown and in College Hill

The July 2009 storm is remembered because it hit the older core of the city. Baseball-size hail was reported downtown and in College Hill, as documented in the NWS event summary.

Older homes in these neighborhoods often have steeper roofs, multiple layers in their history, wood decking with gaps and chimneys that need careful flashing work. Damage on those roofs is not only about shingles. It shows up at valleys, step flashing, gutters and soft metal vents.

04

April 29, 2022: the same evening as the Andover tornado

The supercell that produced the Andover tornado also dropped hail on Wichita. Federal storm records list 1.5-inch hail at the Wichita airport and 1.25-inch hail in Eastborough that evening.

Hail around an inch and a half is the size where many standard three-tab and architectural shingles start to bruise. Bruises are not always visible from the ground, which is why we walk the roof when it is safe and photograph what we find.

Permits in Wichita

Who issues roofing permits in Wichita?

Roofing in Wichita is regulated at two levels: the State of Kansas registers roofing contractors, and the Metropolitan Area Building and Construction Department (MABCD) issues the local permit and contractor license.

01

MABCD handles the city and the unincorporated county

MABCD is the joint building department for the City of Wichita and unincorporated Sedgwick County. Its office is at 271 W 3rd St N, and its main line is (316) 660-1840. MABCD issues roofing permits, licenses roofing contractors and schedules inspections.

A contractor needs an MABCD roofing license before it can pull a Wichita roofing permit, and MABCD requires the Kansas roofing registration as part of that license. After major storms MABCD reminds homeowners to check both, as explained on its contractor licensing page.

02

When a permit is not required

Wichita does not require a permit for roofing repair or replacement work that totals 400 square feet or less in a 12-month period, according to the City of Wichita's permit exemption list. Four hundred square feet is four roofing squares, which covers most spot repairs, small sections and many leak repairs.

A full replacement on a typical house is far larger than that, so it needs a permit. We confirm the permit path in writing before work starts, so you are not guessing whether the job is covered.

03

What the permit costs

Under the Wichita-Sedgwick County Unified Building and Trade Code, the roofing permit fee is based on the footprint of the structure: five cents per square foot, with a $50 minimum and a $1,500 maximum.

For a house with a 1,600-square-foot footprint, that works out to $80. The permit fee is a small part of a roof project, but it is the step that brings a city inspector to look at the work.

04

Historic districts and the Delano overlay

Wichita has local historic districts, including Park Place-Fairview, Topeka-Emporia, Bitting, North Topeka and 10th Street, plus the Delano overlay district. Roofing permits in these areas are routed through the city's Historic Preservation Office.

The design review guidelines treat an in-kind reroof, meaning the same type of material, as a minor project. Changing the material type or the roofline is a bigger review. If your home is in one of these districts, tell us during the inspection and we will plan the material choice with that review in mind.

Wichita Homes

What kinds of roofs do Wichita homes have?

Wichita grew in waves, and each wave left a different kind of roof behind. Knowing which wave your house came from tells us a lot before we ever set up a ladder.

01

Older core neighborhoods

Homes in College Hill, Riverside, Delano and the neighborhoods around downtown often date from the early 1900s through the 1940s. Many have steep gables, dormers, brick chimneys and plank decking instead of plywood.

On these roofs we pay close attention to the deck. Wide gaps between boards can need new sheathing before shingles go on, and chimneys need step and counter flashing that is built correctly rather than sealed with caulk.

02

Post-war ranch homes

Wichita's aviation industry earned it the name Air Capital of the World, and the workforce it drew after World War II filled large neighborhoods with ranch homes. These houses usually have low to moderate pitches, long eaves and simple hip or gable roofs.

Low-slope sections, porch tie-ins and attic ventilation are common trouble spots. Many of these attics were built with too little intake ventilation, which shortens shingle life in Kansas summers.

03

Newer subdivisions on the edges

Growth on the northwest, northeast and far west sides brought larger homes with complex roofs: multiple valleys, high walls, skylights and several roof planes that meet at one point.

Complex roofs have more places for water to collect. Valleys, kick-out flashing at wall ends and the transitions between roof planes are where we focus, along with gutter capacity on the large roof areas these homes have.

04

Commercial and multifamily buildings

Strip centers, warehouses, churches and apartment buildings across Wichita commonly use low-slope roofs: TPO, modified bitumen or older built-up systems.

These roofs fail differently from shingle roofs. Ponding water, open seams, clogged drains and leaks around rooftop units are the issues we check first. See our commercial roofing page for how we handle them.

Our Process in Wichita

How do we handle a Wichita roof from first call to final inspection?

We are based on South Hoover Road in west Wichita, so most Wichita inspections can be scheduled quickly. This is how a typical project moves.

01

Free inspection and photos

We start with the problem that made you call, then inspect the rest of the roof: shingles, flashing, vents, valleys, gutters and the attic when it is accessible. When it is safe to walk the roof, we take photos so you can see what we saw.

You get a plain explanation of what needs attention now, what can wait and what is normal wear. Our team speaks English and Spanish.

02

Written scope and price

If the roof needs work, we write down the scope and price before anything starts. For replacements, our basic planning price is $505 per roofing square, and the final number is confirmed in writing after the inspection. Use the roof cost calculator for a rough estimate first.

If the work follows a storm, we can meet your insurance adjuster. Your insurer decides what the policy covers.

03

Permit, registration and scheduling

For work above the 400-square-foot exemption, the permit is pulled through MABCD with the contractor's state registration number on file, as Kansas law requires. You can look up any roofer in the Kansas Attorney General's registration directory.

We schedule around weather. Most residential replacements take one to a few days once materials are on site.

04

Clean-up and warranty

Every new roof includes the Wichita Roofing Pros lifetime transferable workmanship warranty. Manufacturer system upgrades are available for longer full-start coverage.

At the end of the job we clean the property, run magnets for nails, and walk you through the finished roof. If MABCD schedules a final inspection, we coordinate it.
