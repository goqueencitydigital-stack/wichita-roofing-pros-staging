Sedgwick County Storms

Which storms shaped Sedgwick County roofs?

Sedgwick County has seen some of the most significant hail and tornado events in Kansas history. Many of today's roofs were installed after one of them.

01

September 15, 2010: the state record hailstone

A supercell crossing west Sedgwick County dropped a 7.75-inch hailstone near Pawnee and 119th Street West, still the Kansas state record, according to the NWS in Wichita. Federal records put damage around Goddard near $150 million.

02

April 24, 2006: the west Wichita hailstorm

An early-morning hailstorm hit primarily west Wichita with hail up to baseball size and an estimated $70 million in property damage, according to the NWS Wichita office.

03

May 3, 1999: Haysville and south Wichita

An F4 tornado tracked about 24 miles from north of Wellington through Haysville and south Wichita, killing six people and damaging 150 homes and 27 businesses, as documented by the NWS.

04

April 26, 1991: McConnell and Andover

The 1991 tornado crossed south and east Wichita and McConnell Air Force Base before reaching F5 strength in Andover, just across the Butler County line.

Sedgwick County Permits

Who issues roofing permits across Sedgwick County?

Sedgwick County uses a mix of permit offices. Which one applies depends on where your home sits.

01

MABCD for Wichita and the unincorporated county

The Metropolitan Area Building and Construction Department (MABCD) handles building and housing code enforcement for the City of Wichita and unincorporated Sedgwick County, according to the MABCD jurisdictions page. Its office is at 271 W. 3rd St. N., (316) 660-1840.

The roofing permit fee is five cents per square foot of structure footprint, with a $50 minimum and a $1,500 maximum, as listed on the MABCD permits page.

02

Small cities that start at city hall

For a group of small cities, the permitting process always starts with the city, and contractors must hold an active MABCD license. Inspections for those cities are requested through the MABCD portal or by phone at (316) 660-9550.

Valley Center adds its own city license on top of the MABCD license. See our Valley Center page.

03

Cities with their own rules

Other Sedgwick County cities run their own permit and inspection processes. Derby, Haysville and Park City each have their own requirements, and MABCD may or may not perform services for those cities.

See our pages for Derby, Haysville and Park City.

04

MABCD license and state registration

MABCD requires roofing contractors to hold both an MABCD license and a Kansas roofing registration, as described on its contractor licensing page. After big storms, MABCD reminds homeowners to verify both before signing.

You can look up any roofer in the Kansas roofing registration directory.

Sedgwick County Planning

How should Sedgwick County homeowners plan around storm season?

Most hail in Sedgwick County falls between April and June, with more storms possible through late summer.

01

Take baseline photos in winter

Photos of your roof, gutters and siding taken before storm season give you a clear before-and-after record if hail hits.

02

Keep your policy details handy

Know your deductible, whether your policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value and any time limits for reporting damage.

03

Clear gutters before spring

Clean gutters drain storm rain properly and make hail dents easier to spot afterward.

04

Book an inspection after any big storm

Hail damage is not always visible from the ground. A free inspection after a big storm gives you photos and a clear answer.

Sedgwick County Housing Eras

How old is your Sedgwick County home, and what does that mean for the roof?

The decade your home was built tells us a lot about what we will find under the shingles.

01

Before 1950

Homes in College Hill, Riverside, Delano and the older parts of Mulvane, Valley Center and Derby often have board decking with gaps, steep pitches and brick chimneys. Expect new sheathing, new chimney flashing and careful work around plaster ceilings during tear-off.

02

1950s through 1970s

Ranch homes across south Wichita, Haysville, Park City and Planeview often have low slopes, long eaves and attached carports. Low-slope sections may need a membrane instead of shingles, and older plank decking may need repair.

03

1980s through 2000s

Split-levels and two-story homes in east and west Wichita, Derby and Maize usually have plywood or OSB decking in good shape. Many are on their second or third roof after the 2006 and 2010 hailstorms.

04

2010 and newer

Newer subdivisions in Goddard, Maize, Andover-adjacent east Wichita and Derby have complex rooflines and builder-grade shingles. Pipe boots, ridge vents and wall flashing are the first things we check.

Sedgwick County Weather

What does a typical Sedgwick County storm year look like?

Hail and wind are not evenly spread across the year.

Spring is the busiest season. From April through June, supercells regularly cross Sedgwick County, bringing hail, high wind and the occasional tornado warning.

Summer storms tend to bring straight-line wind and heavy rain, which test flashing and gutters more than shingles. Late-summer hail is less common but not rare; the record hailstone fell in mid-September.

Fall is the best time to replace a roof for many homeowners. Temperatures are mild, shingles seal well, and the roof is ready before winter.

Winter brings ice and the occasional heavy snow. Ice dams at the eaves are the main risk, which is why ice and water shield at the eaves is part of every roof we install in the county.

What roofing work do homeowners in Sedgwick County usually need?

From there, we help you choose between roof repair, roof replacement, commercial roofing, gutters, and siding and trim. The recommendation should follow the roof condition, not the other way around.

  • Gutters, siding, soffit, and fascia should be reviewed when water is affecting roof edges or walls.

What roofing problems are common in Sedgwick County?

Common roofing problems around Sedgwick County include storm-lifted shingles, hail marks, worn pipe boots, flashing leaks, clogged gutters, and water moving behind fascia or siding. In our experience, small stains inside the home often start outside at a detail that looks minor from the ground. That is why we check the roof, drainage, and nearby exterior components together.

  • Hail marks, lifted shingles, cracked pipe boots, and loose flashing are common reasons to schedule an inspection.
  • Gutter overflow can make roof-edge leaks worse, especially when water runs behind fascia or siding.
  • A small ceiling stain may come from a roof detail several feet away from the visible interior mark.
  • In Sedgwick County, wind exposure and storm direction can change which roof slopes show damage first.
