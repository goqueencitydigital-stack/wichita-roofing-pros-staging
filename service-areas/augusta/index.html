Permits in Augusta

What does the Augusta city code say about roofing?

Augusta is in Butler County, so it does not use Wichita's MABCD office. Its building code spells out who can pull a roofing permit and what insurance they must carry.

01

Permits through the Inspection Department

The Augusta building code says roofing contractors must obtain the proper permits from the city Inspection Department. Permits are issued to authorized licensed contractors and to qualifying homeowners doing their own work.

Permit fees are set by resolution of the city governing body, and building valuation can be based on the contractor's bid price.

02

What counts as a roofing contractor

Augusta defines a roofing contractor as someone qualified, licensed and equipped to install, repair and replace roof coverings. That work can include roof-deck insulation, coatings and the sheet metal that goes with the roof.

General, building and residential contractors are not required to get a separate roofing license, because roofing is included in their license.

03

Insurance and testing

Augusta requires minimum liability insurance of $300,000 for anyone holding a contractor license or obtaining a permit. Roofing contractors are among the license categories with an exam requirement, with a minimum score of 75 percent.

Trade licenses are valid for one year. Those numbers give Augusta homeowners a clear standard to check against.

04

Homeowners doing their own work

An owner who lives in a single-family home can do some work on that home without a contractor license, under the conditions in the code. Most homeowners still hire a roofer for a full replacement because of the safety risk and the warranty.

If you hire a contractor, ask to see their Augusta license, their insurance certificate and their Kansas roofing registration.

Augusta Weather

What weather wears down Augusta roofs?

Augusta sits on the Walnut River in southwest Butler County, about 20 miles east of Wichita. It shares the same spring storm season as the metro.

01

Large hail in southern Butler County

On May 31, 2008, a predawn hail event dropped hail of one to 2.25 inches across parts of southern Butler County and neighboring counties, according to the National Weather Service in Wichita. The event caused nearly $20 million in damage across south-central and southeast Kansas.

Storms like that one arrive at night, when homeowners do not see the hail fall. Checking gutters and vehicles the next morning is the easiest way to know whether a roof inspection is needed.

02

Wind on the open hills

East of Augusta the Flint Hills begin, and the terrain opens up. Wind runs hard across that country during spring and summer storms.

Augusta roofs on the east and south edges of town take the most wind. We check edges, rakes and ridge caps on every inspection.

03

Older homes near downtown

Augusta has a historic downtown and older neighborhoods with homes dating back to the oil boom of the early twentieth century. Many have steep roofs, plank decking and brick chimneys.

Those roofs need careful flashing work and often new sheathing over gapped planks before a modern shingle can be installed.

04

Newer homes near the edges of town

Augusta has added new subdivisions as more families move out from Wichita. Those homes have newer roofs but often builder-grade ventilation.

We check attic ventilation and pipe boots on newer homes, because those are usually the first parts of a young roof to fail.

Augusta History

How does Augusta's history show up on its roofs?

Augusta grew quickly during the Butler County oil boom, and the housing from that era is still a big part of town.

Many Augusta homes near downtown were built in the 1910s and 1920s, when oil money brought new families and new construction to the Walnut River valley. Houses from that period were framed with solid lumber and covered with board decking, often with gaps between the planks that were meant for wood shingles.

When we replace a roof on an Augusta home from that era, we usually find more than one layer of old roofing. Removing every layer lets us see the original planks, repair any rot around chimneys and valleys, and add plywood or OSB sheathing so a modern shingle can be fastened to the manufacturer's standard.

Older Augusta homes also tend to have limited attic ventilation. Adding soffit intake and ridge exhaust during a replacement keeps the attic cooler and drier, which helps the new roof last.

Brick chimneys deserve special attention. We replace step and counter flashing rather than relying on caulk, and we look for loose mortar or cracked crowns that let water into the chimney itself.

Newer Augusta homes on the west side of town face a different set of issues: builder-grade vents, short starter strips and pipe boots that crack after ten summers. Either way, the free inspection starts with photos so you can see exactly what we see.
