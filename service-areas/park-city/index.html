Permits in Park City

What does Park City require from a roofer?

Park City writes its own contractor rules into city code, including a specialty license for roofing and siding and a minimum insurance amount.

01

A roofing and siding permit

The Park City licensing and permits page lists a roofing and/or siding permit application among its permit forms, along with the city fee schedule.

We file that permit before tear-off on every Park City roof. If you are planning siding work too, the same permit type covers both trades.

02

Class S-1 roofing and siding license

Park City code lists a Class S-1 specialty license for roofing and siding. A separate specialty license is required for each subclass, as described in the Park City contractor license code.

The code recognizes a valid Sedgwick County or City of Wichita license as qualifying a contractor to apply for a comparable Park City license.

03

Insurance the city requires

Park City requires proof of comprehensive general liability insurance of at least $300,000 per occurrence and aggregate for bodily injury and property damage combined. It also requires proof of workers compensation insurance for all employees working in the city.

Those requirements protect you if someone is hurt on your property or a crew damages something during the job. Ask any roofer for current certificates before you sign.

04

Annual license and where to apply

The Park City code sets a $100 annual license fee. Licenses are not prorated, and unless renewed they expire on December 31.

Applications go to the Office of Planning and Code Enforcement at 6110 N. Hydraulic. The Planning Department can be reached at (316) 744-2026, extension 208.

Park City Homes

What roof issues come up most in Park City?

Park City sits along I-135 north of Wichita, with a mix of 1960s and 1970s ranch homes, newer subdivisions and commercial buildings along the highway.

01

Ranch homes from the town's early years

Park City was incorporated in 1980, but many of its neighborhoods date back to the 1950s through the 1970s. Ranch homes from that era often have low-slope roofs and older plank decking.

We plan for a full tear-off and deck inspection on these homes. Gaps between old planks may need new sheathing before modern shingles can be installed to manufacturer specs.

02

Low slopes and ponding at the edges

Low-slope sections over porches and additions can hold water if the slope is too shallow for shingles. Over time, water backs up under the shingle edges.

On slopes that are too low for shingles, we talk with you about a low-slope membrane instead. That decision is part of the free inspection.

03

Trees and gutters on mature lots

Older Park City neighborhoods have mature trees that drop leaves and seeds into gutters. Clogged gutters overflow and rot the fascia behind them.

We check gutters, fascia and soffit during the roof visit. Read our gutter guards page if cleaning is becoming a chore.

04

Commercial buildings along the highway

Park City has warehouses, shops and offices along I-135 and 61st Street North. Those buildings often have flat or low-slope roofs with TPO or modified bitumen membranes.

We also serve those buildings. See our commercial roofing page for inspections, repairs and replacements.

Park City Maintenance

What maintenance keeps a Park City roof in shape?

A little seasonal attention helps a Park City roof reach its full life. We wrote a full guide on roof maintenance in Park City, and here are the highlights.

01

Spring: look after the first big storm

Check gutters for granules and dents, look for shingles in the yard and walk the perimeter with binoculars if you can. Note anything that looks lifted or missing.

If you see damage, call for an inspection before the next storm adds more.

02

Summer: watch attic heat

Kansas summer heat bakes shingles from both sides. A hot attic shortens shingle life and can make upstairs rooms uncomfortable.

Make sure soffit vents are not blocked by insulation and that roof vents are clear.

03

Fall: clear gutters before leaves drop

Clean gutters before the leaves come down and again after. Clear downspouts so winter melt can drain.

Trim branches that touch or hang over the roof so they cannot scrape shingles in the wind.

04

Winter: watch for ice at the eaves

Ice can build at the eaves after a snow and a cold snap. Ice dams push water under shingles at the lowest part of the roof.

Ice and water shield at the eaves, installed during a replacement, is the best defense. We include it in our roof systems.

What does the Park City replacement process include?

Every Park City project follows the same clear steps.

Free inspection. We document the roof with photos and explain options in plain terms. Schedule an inspection.

Written estimate. We use a planning price of $505 per roofing square, so a 25-square Park City roof starts at about $12,625. The final price is confirmed in writing after the inspection.

City permit. We pull the Park City roofing permit and keep a copy on site.

Install. Full tear-off, deck inspection, board replacement where needed, then a complete roof system with ice and water shield, underlayment, ventilation and ridge cap.

Final. Cleanup, magnet sweep and a walkthrough. Our lifetime transferable workmanship warranty covers the installation.

Qualifying projects can use Wisetack financing. All financing is subject to credit approval.

What roofing service should I choose first in Park City, KS?
Start with roof repair if you have an active leak, a few missing shingles, or isolated flashing damage. Look at roof replacement when the roof is aging, leaking repeatedly, or showing damage across several slopes. An inspection should make that choice clearer.
Can storm damage change the roofing recommendation?
