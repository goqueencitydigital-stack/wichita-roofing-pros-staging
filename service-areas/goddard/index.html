Goddard Storm History

What did the 2010 hailstorm do to Goddard roofs?

Goddard homeowners who were here on September 15, 2010 still talk about the sound of that storm. It is the reason so many roofs in town share the same age.

01

The night the record hailstone fell

The supercell that produced the Kansas state record hailstone on September 15, 2010 formed west of Wichita and tracked right across the Goddard area. The record stone, 7.75 inches across, came down near Pawnee and 119th Street West, only a few miles from Goddard city limits, according to the National Weather Service in Wichita.

Federal storm records for that event put damage in and around Goddard near $150 million. That figure covers roofs, siding, windows, vehicles and gutters across a town that was much smaller then than it is today.

02

Why that matters fifteen years later

Most Goddard homes that were standing in 2010 got a new roof in 2010 or 2011. Those roofs are now fifteen years old or more, and the architectural shingles installed during that rush are reaching the middle and late stage of their service life.

A roof that was installed quickly during a storm rush also deserves a closer look. Crews were stretched thin across west Sedgwick County that fall, and we still find Goddard roofs from that period with short nails, reused pipe boots and drip edge that was never replaced.

03

Hail is a regular visitor, not a one-time event

Goddard sits on the open western edge of the metro, and storms that build over Kingman and Reno counties often reach Goddard first. Hail of one inch or more is a normal part of a Goddard spring.

That is why we tell Goddard homeowners to check gutters, downspouts and window screens after every storm with hail. Dents in soft metal are a quick clue that the shingles above them deserve an inspection.

04

West wind on the windward slopes

Straight-line wind out of the south and west hits Goddard homes without much in the way to slow it down. In newer subdivisions with young trees, the west and south slopes take the full force.

Lifted, creased or missing tabs show up on those slopes first. When we inspect a Goddard roof, we spend extra time on the windward side, along the rakes and at the ridge cap.

Permits in Goddard

How do roofing permits work inside Goddard city limits?

Goddard handles its own permit counter, but it relies on the Metropolitan Area Building and Construction Department (MABCD) for contractor licensing and inspections.

01

The City of Goddard processes the permit

The City of Goddard permits page says the city processes all permits for work done inside Goddard city limits. If your address is in town, the permit process starts at Goddard City Hall rather than at the MABCD office downtown.

City Hall is at 118 N Main Street, and the main line is (316) 794-2441. If your home is outside city limits in unincorporated Sedgwick County, MABCD handles the permit directly.

02

Contractors need an MABCD license

Goddard requires contractors to be licensed with MABCD, and the city does not issue a separate business license for work in Goddard. That keeps things simpler for homeowners: one MABCD license check tells you whether the roofer is eligible.

MABCD also requires a current Kansas roofing registration from the Attorney General before it licenses a roofing contractor. You can look up any roofer in the Kansas roofing registration directory.

03

Inspections are scheduled through MABCD

Once the Goddard permit is paid for, the contractor or homeowner schedules the inspection with MABCD. According to the MABCD small-cities page, inspections for cities in that group are requested through the MABCD portal or by phone at (316) 660-9550.

We handle the scheduling on every Goddard roof we permit, and we make sure the job site is ready for the inspector before the request goes in.

04

How we keep the paperwork straight

Before we start a Goddard roof, we confirm whether the address is inside city limits, pull the right permit and post it where the inspector expects it.

Some Goddard addresses near 167th Street West or along Kellogg sit right on a boundary line, so we check the parcel instead of assuming. Getting that detail right avoids delays on tear-off day.

Goddard Homes

What roof problems are common on Goddard homes?

Goddard has grown fast, with large subdivisions on former farmland around the school district campuses. That growth shapes what we see on the roof.

01

Builder-grade roofs on new construction

Many Goddard subdivisions were roofed by the home builder with an entry-level architectural shingle. Those roofs are fine on day one, but they often go in with the minimum number of roof vents and basic pipe boots.

When those homes reach ten or twelve years old, we start to see cracked boots, granule loss on the south slopes and attic heat buildup. A free inspection can show whether you are looking at a repair or the start of a replacement plan.

02

Open lots and exposed rooflines

Homes backing up to fields or retention ponds in Goddard have nothing between them and the wind. The wide-open lots that make Goddard feel spacious also leave rooflines exposed.

On these homes we pay attention to the starter course and the first row of shingles along the eaves and rakes. If the starter strip was skipped or installed without adhesive, wind gets under the edge.

03

Gutters sized for the roof, not the lot

Goddard homes with long, single-slope runs at the back of the house can dump a lot of water at one corner. Undersized gutters and too few downspouts send that water over the edge and toward the foundation.

We can look at gutter size and downspout placement during the same visit as your roof check. See our seamless gutter installation page for what we offer.

04

Older homes near downtown Goddard

The older homes near Main Street and the original town grid are different. Some have low-slope porch roofs, older decking or more than one layer of shingles from earlier decades.

On those houses we plan for a full tear-off and a deck inspection, and we price any board replacement clearly before the job begins.
