Sumner County Storms

What storms have crossed Sumner County?

Sumner County lies directly south of Wichita, between the metro and the Oklahoma line. Storms that later hit Wichita often pass through Sumner County first.

01

May 3, 1999: a tornado that began near Wellington

On the evening of May 3, 1999, a tornado touched down about four miles north of Wellington around 7:15 p.m. and tracked north for about 24 miles, producing F4 damage in Haysville, according to the National Weather Service in Wichita.

The NWS timeline shows a tornado warning for northern Sumner County at 7:38 p.m., a spotter report near Mayfield at 7:44 p.m. and another report two miles northwest of Wellington at 7:58 p.m.

02

May 31, 2008: hail in northern Sumner

The predawn hail event of May 31, 2008 dropped hail of one to 2.25 inches across parts of northern Sumner County, as documented by the NWS in Wichita.

Homes around Belle Plaine, Mulvane and Conway Springs sit in that northern band of the county, close to the Sedgwick County line.

03

Hail from the southwest

Many Sumner County hail days begin with storms forming in north-central Oklahoma and moving northeast. Caldwell, Argonia and Wellington are often first in line.

Because storms move quickly across open farmland, hail can hit one town and miss the next. We inspect each roof on its own merits.

04

Wind on wheat country

Sumner County is one of the leading wheat counties in Kansas, and its open fields offer little protection from wind. Straight-line gusts can lift shingles on homes and peel panels on barns.

We pay close attention to rake edges, ridge caps and metal fastening on rural Sumner County properties.

Sumner County Permits

How do roofing permits work in Sumner County?

Sumner County cities set their own permit and license rules, and the county handles zoning in the unincorporated areas.

01

Wellington runs its own building office

The City of Wellington has its own building, codes and zoning staff. We confirm current Wellington permit and contractor license requirements with the city before every Wellington roof.

If you are unsure what your project needs, call Wellington City Hall before work starts. We are happy to make that call for you.

02

Mulvane straddles two counties

Mulvane sits on the line between Sedgwick and Sumner counties. Its city office handles permits for homes inside city limits on both sides of the line.

Homes just outside Mulvane may be in unincorporated Sedgwick County, where MABCD handles permits, or in unincorporated Sumner County. We check the parcel first.

03

Unincorporated Sumner County

Sumner County adopted its current zoning regulations on July 1, 2006, with later amendments. Zoning rules affect new structures and land use, and the county planning and zoning office can explain whether a project needs review.

A like-for-like reroof on an existing home is usually straightforward, but we confirm with the county when a project adds space or changes a structure.

04

Smaller cities

Belle Plaine, Conway Springs, Oxford, Caldwell and Argonia each handle permits through their city offices. Rules vary from town to town.

In every Sumner County town, roofers working for pay must be registered with the Kansas Attorney General. That registration is the one requirement that applies everywhere.

Wellington Spotlight

What should Wellington homeowners know about their roofs?

Wellington is the largest city in Sumner County and the one we visit most often.

Wellington's downtown and the streets around it are lined with homes built in the late 1800s and early 1900s, when the railroad and wheat trade drove growth. Many of those houses have tall gables, wraparound porches and dormers.

On these homes the porch roof is often a separate low-slope section tied into the main roof. That transition is one of the most common leak points we see in Wellington, along with chimneys and old valley metal.

Newer Wellington neighborhoods on the edges of town have simpler ranch and two-story homes. Their roofs are easier to replace, but they still need good ventilation and proper flashing at walls and pipes.

The 1999 tornado that devastated Haysville started just north of Wellington, and spring storms continue to track across the area. Whether your home is a century old or ten years old, a free inspection after a big storm is the best way to know where you stand.

Mulvane Spotlight

What makes Mulvane roofs different?

Mulvane sits on the Sedgwick and Sumner county line near Kansas Star Casino and the Arkansas River.

Mulvane has grown quickly, with new subdivisions on the north side and older homes near the original downtown. That mix means we see everything from 1940s plank decking to brand-new builder-grade roofs.

Because the city straddles two counties, Mulvane homeowners sometimes get conflicting advice about where to pull a permit. Inside city limits, the City of Mulvane handles it. Outside, the county line decides.

Open ground around Mulvane leaves many homes exposed to south and west wind. We check windward slopes and edges closely on every Mulvane inspection.

What roofing work do homeowners in Sumner County usually need?

Most calls in Sumner County start with one visible problem: a stain on the ceiling, missing shingles after wind, hail concerns, or water spilling where it should not. From there, we help you choose between roof repair, roof replacement, commercial roofing, gutters, and siding and trim. The recommendation should follow the roof condition, not the other way around.

  • Roof repair is the right starting point when damage appears isolated and the rest of the roof still has life.
  • Roof replacement should be discussed when age, leaks, or storm damage show up across several roof areas.
  • Commercial roofs need a different inspection because drains, seams, membranes, and rooftop equipment change the leak path.
  • Gutters, siding, soffit, and fascia should be reviewed when water is affecting roof edges or walls.

What roofing problems are common in Sumner County?

Common roofing problems around Sumner County include storm-lifted shingles, hail marks, worn pipe boots, flashing leaks, clogged gutters, and water moving behind fascia or siding. In our experience, small stains inside the home often start outside at a detail that looks minor from the ground. That is why we check the roof, drainage, and nearby exterior components together.

  • Hail marks, lifted shingles, cracked pipe boots, and loose flashing are common reasons to schedule an inspection.
  • Gutter overflow can make roof-edge leaks worse, especially when water runs behind fascia or siding.
  • A small ceiling stain may come from a roof detail several feet away from the visible interior mark.
  • In Sumner County, wind exposure and storm direction can change which roof slopes show damage first.

What roofing service should I choose first in Sumner County?
Start with roof repair if you have an active leak, a few missing shingles, or isolated flashing damage. Look at roof replacement when the roof is aging, leaking repeatedly, or showing damage across several slopes. An inspection should make that choice clearer.
Can storm damage change the roofing recommendation?
Yes. Hail and high wind can turn a simple-looking problem into a larger roof issue. We check shingles, vents, flashing, gutters, and soft metals before recommending repair or replacement. Photos matter because storm damage is not always obvious from the ground.
