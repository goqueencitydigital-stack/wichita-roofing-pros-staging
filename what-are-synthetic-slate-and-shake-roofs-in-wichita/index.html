What Are Synthetic Slate and Shake Roofing Products?

Synthetic slate and synthetic shake are manufactured roofing tiles designed to replicate the visual character of natural slate stone or split cedar shake wood. Most products are made from recycled rubber, recycled plastics, polymer composites, or a blend of these materials. Manufacturers compress and mold the raw material under heat to create individual tiles that mimic the texture, shadow lines, and color variation found in natural materials. Unlike natural slate, which is quarried stone, or real cedar shake, which is split or sawn wood, synthetic versions are engineered for consistency, lighter weight, and resistance to the impact, moisture cycling, and UV exposure that real materials struggle with over time. In Wichita, Synthetic Slate / Shake in Wichita is one of the more specialized residential roofing services offered because the installation technique differs from standard asphalt shingle work.

How Does Synthetic Roofing Hold Up Against Wichita Hail?

Hail performance is one of the main reasons Wichita homeowners investigate synthetic roofing. Many synthetic slate and shake products achieve a Class 4 impact resistance rating under UL 2218 testing, the highest classification available, meaning a 2-inch steel ball dropped from 20 feet does not crack the tile. According to IBHS hail and high-wind roof research, impact-resistant materials can meaningfully reduce storm-related damage compared to standard products when installed correctly. In Wichita, where hail of 1 inch or larger is not uncommon during spring and early summer storm seasons tracked by the NOAA National Weather Service Wichita, a Class 4 rating on your roofing material provides genuine added protection. Some Kansas insurers also offer premium discounts for Class 4 rated roofs, so it is worth asking your carrier about your specific policy before installation.

Not every synthetic product on the market carries a Class 4 rating, so verifying the specific UL certification of the product your contractor proposes is important. Lower-end polymer shingles may use similar marketing language without meeting the full standard.

How Much Do Synthetic Slate and Shake Roofs Cost in Wichita?

Synthetic slate and shake products sit above asphalt shingles but generally below natural slate in total installed cost. Material costs vary widely by brand, profile, and tile thickness, but residential projects in Wichita typically range from roughly $7 to $14 per square foot installed, depending on roof complexity, pitch, and which product line is selected. That compares to $4 to $7 per square foot for architectural asphalt shingles and $20 or more per square foot for genuine quarried slate. The higher upfront investment in synthetic materials is often offset by a longer manufacturer warranty, typically 30 to 50 years on leading products, and reduced maintenance compared to real cedar shake, which requires periodic cleaning, retreatment, and eventual wood replacement as boards split and decay. When evaluating total cost of ownership over a 30-year horizon, synthetic options can be competitive even against premium asphalt products. A Wichita Roofing Pros roof inspection can help you assess whether your existing structure and decking are compatible with the added weight of some synthetic tile profiles before you commit to a material.

Top 5 Factors Wichita Homeowners Should Evaluate Before Choosing Synthetic Roofing

  1. Impact Rating – Confirm the product carries UL 2218 Class 4 certification. Ask your contractor for the specific test report, not just a marketing claim.
  2. Weight per Square – Most synthetic products weigh 150 to 250 lbs per roofing square (100 sq ft), significantly less than natural slate at 700 to 1,500 lbs per square. Verify your decking and rafter system can handle the load without engineering changes.
  3. Manufacturer Warranty Terms – Read what the warranty actually covers. Some warranties are prorated after year 10 and exclude wind or hail damage that is already addressed by insurance. Look for non-prorated lifetime or 50-year transferable warranties.
  4. Contractor Installation Certification – Several synthetic roofing brands require or recommend installer certification. Improper fastening or flashing integration voids many warranties and creates leak points, especially at valleys and wall intersections common in Wichita two-story homes.
  5. HOA and Neighborhood Compatibility – Some Wichita neighborhood associations have guidelines on roofing materials and colors. Confirm that the profile and color options available in your chosen product line are permitted before purchasing.

Is Synthetic Slate or Shake Better Than Asphalt for Wichita Homes?

The comparison between synthetic slate or shake and premium architectural asphalt shingles comes down to priorities. Asphalt shingles in a Class 4 rated product line, such as certain impact-resistant 3-tab or architectural profiles, provide solid storm protection at a lower price point and are the most widely installed roofing material across the Wichita roofing service area. Synthetic slate and shake products offer a distinctly different visual profile, often with deeper shadow lines and a more custom appearance that stands out in neighborhoods where standard asphalt is the norm. They also tend to have longer product warranties and can add resale appeal. Where asphalt wins is in cost, availability of local contractors experienced in the material, and ease of repair when individual shingles are damaged. Synthetic tile repairs are more specialized because matching a specific tile profile and color after years of weathering can be difficult if the original product line has been discontinued or reformulated.

What Does Installation Involve for Synthetic Roofing in Wichita?

Installing synthetic slate or shake in Wichita follows a similar sequence to other premium steep-slope roofing products. The process starts with a full decking inspection and repair of any soft spots, which is especially relevant after storm seasons that can leave hidden moisture damage. Ice and water shield underlayment is applied at eaves and in valleys before a synthetic or felt underlayment covers the field. Individual synthetic tiles are fastened according to the manufacturer’s nailing pattern, and starter courses are set at the eaves with cut tiles or manufacturer-supplied starter pieces. Flashing at chimneys, skylights, and roof-to-wall intersections requires careful integration because synthetic tile profiles are thicker than asphalt shingles and must be accounted for in the flashing step dimension. Any roofing work on Wichita homes requires a building permit through the city permits office, and properties in unincorporated areas of Sedgwick County fall under requirements administered through the county. Scheduling an inspection before installation helps identify decking concerns and ensures the selected product is appropriate for your roof’s pitch and structure.

How Do You Maintain a Synthetic Slate or Shake Roof?

One of the selling points of synthetic roofing materials is reduced maintenance compared to natural alternatives. Real cedar shake requires periodic cleaning to remove moss and mildew, retreatment with preservatives, and eventual replacement of split or deteriorated boards, sometimes as frequently as every 10 to 15 years in humid climates. Synthetic products do not rot, do not absorb moisture the way wood does, and are not susceptible to insect damage. Routine maintenance for a synthetic roof in Wichita involves annual visual inspections to check for any cracked or dislodged tiles after major storms, keeping gutters clear so water drains properly, and removing accumulated debris from valleys where leaves can trap moisture. If a tile is cracked by an unusually large hailstone or impact, individual tile replacement is possible, though sourcing a color match on older installations can be challenging. Connecting with Wichita Roofing Pros for a roof inspection after any significant storm event lets you document any impact points before filing an insurance claim if the damage warrants it.

Choosing a roof material for Wichita weather

Hail resistance, wind ratings and summer heat should drive the choice as much as looks. Impact-rated products, proper ventilation and a correct installation matter more in Kansas than in milder climates.

Frequently Asked Questions About Synthetic Slate Roofing in Wichita

Can synthetic slate be installed over existing shingles in Wichita?

Most synthetic slate and shake manufacturers do not recommend or warranty installation over existing shingles. The added weight of synthetic tiles combined with the irregular surface of old shingles underneath can compromise fastening, drainage, and long-term performance. A full tear-off is the standard approach, which also allows inspection of the decking for any hidden moisture damage before new materials go down.

Do synthetic roofs qualify for insurance discounts in Kansas?

Some Kansas insurers offer premium discounts for Class 4 impact-resistant roofing materials, but the discount availability and amount varies by carrier and policy. Contact your homeowner’s insurance provider directly and provide the UL 2218 Class 4 certification documentation for the specific product installed. Not all synthetic products achieve Class 4, so verify before purchasing the material.

How long do synthetic slate roofs last in Wichita’s climate?

Leading synthetic slate and shake products carry manufacturer warranties of 30 to 50 years, and many are rated for a service life exceeding that range under normal conditions. Wichita’s combination of hail, high wind, temperature cycling, and UV exposure is within the design parameters of Class 4 rated synthetic products. Actual longevity depends on installation quality, maintenance, and whether any individual tiles sustain damage that goes unrepaired.

Is synthetic slate heavier than standard asphalt shingles?

Synthetic slate and shake products typically weigh 150 to 250 lbs per roofing square, which is moderately heavier than standard 3-tab asphalt shingles at around 200 to 250 lbs per square and comparable to architectural asphalt shingles. This weight is far below natural slate, which can exceed 1,000 lbs per square. Most homes built in Wichita over the past several decades can accommodate synthetic products without structural modification, but a contractor inspection should confirm your decking and framing condition.

What colors and styles are available for synthetic shake roofs in Wichita?

Synthetic shake and slate products come in a range of earthy tones, wood-grain simulations, and slate-gray palettes. Color variety depends on the specific product line. Some brands offer 10 to 20 color options ranging from cedar tan and harvest brown to charcoal and weathered gray. Colors are typically molded through the material rather than surface-coated, which reduces fading compared to painted or granule-coated products. Reviewing manufacturer samples in Wichita’s direct sunlight is worthwhile since colors can appear different under show-room lighting.

Synthetic roofing materials represent a meaningful upgrade path for Wichita homeowners who want premium aesthetics combined with documented storm resistance. The key is verifying Class 4 impact ratings, confirming installer experience with the specific product, and understanding the warranty terms fully before signing a contract.
