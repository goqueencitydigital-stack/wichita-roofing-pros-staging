Why Tarp

Why is tarping an active leak so important?

Once water gets inside, damage spreads quickly. It soaks insulation, stains drywall, swells wood and can lead to mold. A properly installed tarp stops more water from getting in while you wait for an insurance decision, materials or a scheduled replacement.

Many homeowners insurance policies also expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a loss. Keeping receipts and photos of emergency tarping helps show that you did. The Kansas Insurance Department’s storm guidance encourages homeowners to make temporary repairs to prevent additional damage and keep records.

How It Is Done

How is a roof tarp installed correctly?

01

Find the source

The crew traces the leak to the damaged area, which is not always directly above the interior stain.

02

Cover beyond the damage

The tarp extends past the damaged area, ideally over the ridge, so water runs over it, not under it.

03

Fasten with cap nails

Plastic-cap roofing nails hold the material and keep water from running down the nail head.

04

Keep edges down

Edges are secured so wind cannot catch and lift the tarp.

05

Document it

Photos of the damage and tarp help your insurance claim.

06

Recheck and re-tarp

If a tarp starts to degrade, the crew comes back and replaces it.

Wood Shake

What should owners of wood shake roofs know?

Wood shake roofs have a natural, textured look that many homeowners love, but they need more care than asphalt shingles. Shakes can split, cup, grow moss or rot over time, especially on shaded slopes. Hail can split brittle shakes, and wind can lift loose pieces.

When a wood shake roof reaches the end of its life, many homeowners replace it with synthetic shake, which gives a similar look with less upkeep, or with architectural asphalt shingles. See synthetic slate and shake or What are synthetic slate and shake roofs in Wichita? for options.

Claims

How does tarping fit into an insurance claim?

When storm damage causes a leak, the order of steps matters. First, protect the home with a tarp so damage does not get worse. Second, document the damage with photos before and after tarping. Third, open the claim with your insurer and keep the claim number. Fourth, meet the adjuster, ideally with your roofer, so the damage can be shown clearly. Finally, complete repairs or replacement once the claim is decided.

Tarping does not mean you have committed to a contractor for the full job. It is a temporary step to protect your home. Keep your tarping invoice with your claim records. Our insurance claim roof replacement page walks through the full process.

Materials

Why use synthetic underlayment and cap nails for tarping?

Not every tarp is equal. Thin blue tarps can tear, flap and let water under them within days, especially in Kansas wind. The project on this page uses synthetic underlayment as the tarp material, held with plastic-cap roofing nails. Synthetic underlayment is made for roofs, so it holds up better to sun and wind than many general-purpose tarps, and it lies flatter against the roof surface.

The plastic caps spread the holding force over a wider area and help keep water from running down the nail shank into the roof. Edges are secured so wind cannot get underneath. Even so, any tarp is temporary, and it is checked and replaced if it starts to degrade while the claim moves forward.

Inside the Home

What should you do inside while the roof is tarped?

Once the tarp stops new water, focus on drying the inside. Move furniture and belongings out of affected rooms, run fans and a dehumidifier, and pull back wet carpet if you can. Check the attic for soaked insulation, which may need to be removed. Photograph everything before you clean or throw it away, and keep receipts for any drying work for your insurance claim.

Shake Roofs

Why do wood shake roofs leak?

Wood shakes split, cup and wear thin with age. In Kansas, sun, hail, freeze-thaw cycles and wind all speed that process up. Once shakes crack or slip, water can find a path to the underlayment and decking. Moss and debris hold moisture against the wood and shorten its life further. A tarp buys time; a lasting fix usually means repair or replacement.

A tarp is temporary. It can protect the home while a claim or replacement is scheduled, but it should be checked and replaced if it starts to wear.
