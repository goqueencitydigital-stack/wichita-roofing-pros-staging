Roofing Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the words you will see on an estimate or inspection report.

3-tab shingles
Flat, single-layer asphalt shingles with three cutouts per strip; an older, lighter style.
Adjuster
The insurance company’s representative who inspects damage and recommends what the policy covers.
Architectural shingles
Laminated, dimensional asphalt shingles with a thicker profile and better wind performance than 3-tab shingles.
Class 4 shingle
A shingle that passes the UL 2218 impact test with a 2-inch steel ball, the highest impact rating.
Decking (roof deck)
The plywood or OSB boards the roof is nailed to. It is inspected after tear-off for rot and soft spots.
Deductible
The part of an insurance claim the homeowner pays, set by the policy.
Drip edge
Metal flashing along roof edges that directs water into the gutter and protects the fascia.
Fascia
The board along the roof edge where gutters attach.
Flashing
Metal pieces that seal joints where the roof meets walls, chimneys, skylights and valleys.
Granule loss
Shedding of the mineral granules on asphalt shingles; often visible in gutters after hail or with age.
Hail bruise
A soft spot in a shingle where hail fractured the mat under the granules.
Ice and water shield
Self-adhering waterproof underlayment used at eaves, valleys and penetrations.
Kansas roofing registration
A certificate every roofing contractor providing roofing for a fee in Kansas must hold from the Kansas Attorney General.
MABCD
Metropolitan Area Building and Construction Department, which issues permits in Wichita and Sedgwick County.
Manufacturer warranty
The shingle maker’s coverage for product defects; enhanced tiers can add tear-off and labor coverage.
Modified bitumen
A multi-ply, asphalt-based low-slope roofing system with polymer modifiers.
Pipe boot
A rubber or metal collar that seals around plumbing vent pipes; a frequent leak point.
Pitch
Roof steepness, written as rise per 12 inches of run (for example 6/12).
Ponding water
Water that stays on a low-slope roof more than 48 hours after rain.
Ridge cap
Shingles that cover the peak where two roof planes meet.
Roofing square
100 square feet of roof surface; the unit roofs are measured and priced in.
Soffit
The underside of the roof overhang, often vented to bring air into the attic.
Synthetic underlayment
A woven polymer underlayment installed over the deck, replacing traditional felt paper.
Tear-off
Removing existing roofing down to the deck before installing the new roof.
TPO
Thermoplastic polyolefin, a single-ply white membrane with heat-welded seams used on low-slope roofs.
Valley
The inside angle where two roof planes meet and channel water.
Ventilation (intake and exhaust)
Airflow through soffit intake and ridge or roof exhaust vents that keeps the attic cooler and drier.
Workmanship warranty
The contractor’s coverage for problems caused by installation, separate from the manufacturer’s product warranty.

Roof Parts

What are the main parts of a roof called?

These are the parts of a roof you will hear named during an inspection, from the peak down to the gutter.

01

Eave

The lower edge of the roof that overhangs the wall. Gutters hang along the eaves, and ice and water shield is usually installed here.

02

Rake

The sloped edge of a gable roof, running from the eave up to the ridge. Rake edges are especially exposed to wind.

03

Ridge

The horizontal peak where two roof slopes meet. Ridge vents and ridge cap shingles are installed here.

04

Hip

The sloped external angle where two roof planes meet, running from the ridge down to a corner. Hip roofs slope on all four sides.

05

Gable

The triangular section of wall at the end of a pitched roof. A gable roof has two slopes meeting at a ridge.

06

Dormer

A structure that projects from a sloped roof, usually with a window. Dormers add valleys and flashing points that need careful detailing.

07

Roof plane

Any single flat section of roof. Complex roofs have many planes, which adds valleys, hips and labor.

08

Overhang

The part of the roof that extends past the exterior wall, protecting siding and windows from rain.

09

Rafters and trusses

The wood framing that supports the roof deck. Trusses are prebuilt triangular frames; rafters are individual sloped boards.

Layers

What layers make up a shingle roof system?

A shingle roof is a system of layers, each with a job. From the framing up:

01

Roof deck

Plywood or OSB sheathing nailed to the rafters or trusses. Everything else is fastened to it.

02

Ice and water shield

A self-adhering waterproof membrane installed at eaves, valleys and around penetrations where water collects or ice dams form.

03

Underlayment

A layer of synthetic material or felt over the rest of the deck, providing a second line of defense under the shingles.

04

Starter strip

A row of adhesive-backed shingles along the eaves and rakes that helps the first course seal and resist wind uplift.

05

Field shingles

The main shingles covering the roof surface, installed in overlapping courses.

06

Ridge cap

Specially shaped shingles that cover the ridge and hips, often over a ridge vent.

Flashing and Water

What do flashing and drainage terms mean?

01

Step flashing

Individual L-shaped metal pieces woven with each shingle course where a roof meets a side wall.

02

Counter-flashing

Metal set into or over a wall or chimney that covers the top of the step or base flashing.

03

Chimney cricket

A small peaked structure built behind a chimney to divert water around it instead of letting it pool.

04

Kick-out flashing

A bent piece of flashing at the bottom of a roof-to-wall joint that directs water into the gutter instead of behind siding.

05

Open valley

A valley lined with exposed metal, with shingles cut back on both sides.

06

Closed-cut valley

A valley where shingles from one side run through, and shingles from the other side are cut in a clean line over them.

07

Downspout

The vertical pipe that carries water from the gutter to the ground.

08

Scupper

An opening in a parapet wall on a low-slope roof that lets water drain off the edge.

09

Ice dam

A ridge of ice that forms at the eaves when snow melts higher on the roof and refreezes at the cold edge, backing water under shingles.

Ventilation

What do attic ventilation terms mean?

01

Intake vents

Vents low on the roof, usually in the soffit, that let outside air into the attic.

02

Exhaust vents

Vents high on the roof, such as ridge vents or box vents, that let warm, moist air out.

03

Ridge vent

A continuous vent along the ridge, covered by ridge cap shingles, that exhausts attic air.

04

Box vent

A square, low-profile vent installed near the ridge. Also called a static or turtle vent.

05

Net free area

The actual open area of a vent through which air can move, used to size a ventilation system.

06

Balanced ventilation

Roughly equal intake and exhaust, so air flows steadily through the attic.

Storm Damage

What do hail and wind damage terms mean?

01

Functional damage

Damage that affects how well the roof keeps water out or how long it will last, such as fractured shingle mats or torn tabs.

02

Cosmetic damage

Damage that changes appearance without affecting performance, such as minor dents in metal. Some policies treat cosmetic damage differently.

03

Creased shingle

A shingle bent back by wind and folded, which can break the seal and weaken the shingle.

04

Wind uplift

Wind pressure that lifts shingles at edges, ridges and corners. Starter strips and correct nailing help resist it.

05

Collateral damage

Dents on gutters, downspouts, siding, screens or air conditioners that help show the size and direction of a hailstorm.

06

Date of loss

The date a storm caused damage, used by insurers when processing a claim.

Insurance

What do roofing insurance terms mean?

01

ACV (actual cash value)

A payment based on the depreciated value of the damaged roof. Policies vary, so check your declarations page.

02

RCV (replacement cost value)

A payment based on the cost to replace the roof with similar materials, often paid in two parts.

03

Depreciation

The reduction in value for age and wear. Under replacement-cost policies, some depreciation may be recoverable after work is complete.

04

Supplement

A request to the insurer to add items that were missed or discovered once work began, such as rotted decking.

05

Scope of loss

The insurer’s list of damaged items and the repairs it approves.

06

Code upgrade coverage

Optional coverage that pays for work required by current building codes during a repair or replacement.

Materials

What do roofing material terms mean?

01

Standing seam metal

Metal panels joined by raised seams that hide the fasteners, used on homes and commercial buildings.

02

Exposed-fastener metal

Metal panels screwed through the face, common on barns and outbuildings.

03

Synthetic slate and shake

Polymer or composite products shaped to look like slate or cedar shake, lighter than the natural materials.

04

Concrete tile

Heavy molded concrete roof tiles. The roof structure must be able to support the weight.

05

EPDM

A black rubber single-ply membrane used on low-slope roofs.

06

Algae resistance

Shingles with copper or other granules designed to slow the dark streaks caused by algae.

07

UL 2218

The impact test for roofing products. Products are rated Class 1 to Class 4 based on the size of steel ball they withstand.

08

Bundle

A package of shingles. Many architectural shingles come three bundles to a square.

09

Exposure

The part of each shingle that is visible after installation; the rest is covered by the course above.

Process

What do estimate and project terms mean?

01

Scope of work

The written list of everything the roofer will do, from tear-off to cleanup.

02

Line item

A single entry on an estimate, such as drip edge, ridge vent or decking replacement.

03

Per-sheet decking price

The price to replace each sheet of damaged decking found after tear-off, listed in advance on a good estimate.

04

Overlay (layover)

Installing new shingles over an existing layer instead of tearing off. Codes limit how many layers a roof can have.

05

Dry-in

Covering the deck with underlayment so the home is protected from rain before shingles go on.

06

Final walkthrough

A review of the finished job with the homeowner, including cleanup and paperwork.

07

Planning price

A per-square price used to budget before inspection. Ours is $505 per square, confirmed in writing after inspection.

Glossary Questions, Answered

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900. Hablamos Español.

What is the difference between a hip roof and a gable roof?

A gable roof has two slopes that meet at a ridge, with triangular walls at each end. A hip roof slopes on all four sides.

What is the difference between underlayment and ice and water shield?

Underlayment covers the whole deck as a secondary barrier. Ice and water shield is a self-adhering waterproof membrane used in the most vulnerable areas, such as eaves and valleys.

What does Class 4 mean on shingles?

Class 4 is the highest impact rating under UL 2218, meaning a sample withstood a 2-inch steel ball dropped from a set height without cracking.

What is a roofing supplement?

A request to the insurer to add items that were missed in the original claim or discovered during work.
