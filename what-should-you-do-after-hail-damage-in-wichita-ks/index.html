What should you do in the first 72 hours after hail?

The first few days set up the claim and protect the house. Work through these in order.

  1. Stay safe. Stay off the roof and watch for downed lines. The National Weather Service advises staying indoors until storms pass.
  2. Photograph from the ground. Gutters, downspouts, window screens, siding, vehicles and any granules at downspouts — with dates.
  3. Stop active leaks. Catch water, move belongings and call for emergency tarping if needed.
  4. Review your policy. Note your deductible and claim deadlines.
  5. Report the claim. Contact your insurer or agent as soon as practical; the Kansas Insurance Department publishes storm-claim guidance.
  6. Get a roof inspection. A professional inspection documents roof damage you cannot safely see.
  7. Be careful with door-knockers. Verify Kansas AG registration before signing.

What size hail damages a roof?

The National Weather Service treats hail one inch (quarter size) or larger as severe. Shingle damage depends on hail size, density, wind and the roof’s age and impact rating.

What Should You Do Immediately After a Wichita Hailstorm?

Safety comes first. Do not climb onto your roof during or right after a storm. Wet shingles, debris, and structural unknowns make immediate access dangerous. Instead, do a ground-level visual survey from multiple angles of your property. Look for visible missing shingles, displaced ridge cap, or obvious impact craters. Check soft-metal surfaces you can safely reach: downspout elbows, window screens, aluminum fascia, and AC condenser fins all show hail impact clearly and give you early evidence of storm intensity before any contractor arrives. Document everything with time-stamped photos on your phone. Contact the Storm & Hail Damage in Wichita team as early as possible to get an inspection scheduled while storm evidence is fresh.

How Do You Document Hail Damage for an Insurance Claim in Wichita?

Insurance adjusters evaluate two types of evidence: physical damage on the roof and corroborating records showing that a qualifying weather event occurred at your address. Photograph damage from ground level first, then add close-up images of any hail spatter marks, granule displacement in gutters, and dented metal. Create a written log with the storm date and time, the approximate hail size if you observed it or kept a sample, and any immediate interior effects such as ceiling stains or attic moisture. The NOAA National Weather Service Wichita storm reports archive verifies storm events by date and location, providing third-party confirmation that supports your claim. Keep all receipts for any emergency protective work such as tarping. Submit documentation promptly because most Kansas policies have claim reporting deadlines.

Should You Use a Door-Knocker Contractor After Wichita Hail?

This is one of the most important judgment calls Wichita homeowners face after a large hail event. Storm-chasing contractors arrive within hours of major events, often offering fast inspections and immediate claim filing assistance. Some are legitimate regional contractors with the crews and materials to handle high volume. Others are out-of-state operations that may produce inflated estimates, use substandard materials, or disappear when workmanship issues arise months later. What Does an Independent Hail Damage Inspection Cover?

A professional hail damage inspection from a Wichita-based contractor goes beyond what you can observe from the ground. The inspector walks the roof surface and checks for bruising on shingles, which appears as soft spots where hail impact has fractured the mat beneath the granule layer. They examine all penetrations including pipe boots, vent covers, and skylights for impact marks. Ridge cap and hip shingles receive separate evaluation because they take impact from multiple angles. The inspector also checks the attic for evidence of water intrusion, examines decking for moisture staining, and reviews flashing condition. A written report with photographs becomes a central document for your insurance adjuster meeting. Top 6 Hail Damage Claim Mistakes Wichita Homeowners Make

  1. Waiting too long to file: Kansas policies have reporting deadlines; delaying claim submission after a storm event risks denial on procedural grounds.
  2. Not documenting evidence before cleanup: Once granules are swept from gutters and dented screens are replaced, the visual record weakens. Document first, clean second.
  3. Accepting the first adjuster estimate without review: Initial estimates sometimes miss supplemental damage such as soft-metal items, fascia, or gutters. A contractor-prepared scope can identify omissions.
  4. Signing contracts before the claim is approved: Commit to a contractor scope only after the insurance scope is confirmed so you understand what will and will not be covered.
  5. Choosing a contractor based solely on price: The lowest bid after a hailstorm often reflects materials or workmanship shortcuts that appear as premature failures within two to three years.
  6. Ignoring interior follow-up: Even a successfully repaired roof can leave attic moisture that causes mold if not addressed. An attic check after any hail-related roof repair confirms full resolution.

How Does Kansas Hail Season Affect Wichita Roof Damage Risk?

Wichita sits in a region of Kansas where severe convective storms are a documented annual occurrence from approximately March through September. Hail of one inch diameter or larger is significant enough to cause functional shingle damage, and storms producing hail of 1.5 inches or larger cause damage that typically supports a full replacement claim. Roof age and shingle quality affect vulnerability: a 15-year-old standard three-tab roof will show more functional damage from a given hail event than a recently installed Class 4 impact-resistant shingle system. What a Sedgwick County storm season does to a roof

Repeated storms add up. A roof that came through one hail event with light bruising can start shedding granules, lifting tabs or leaking around flashing after the next round of wind. Documenting condition after each major storm gives you a clear timeline if you ever need to file a claim.

Storm damage checklist: what to look for from the ground for Wichita homeowners

You do not need to climb a ladder to spot the first clues. Walk the property in daylight and note anything on this list.

  • Dents or dings on gutters, downspouts, vents and window wraps
  • Granules collecting at downspout outlets or on driveways
  • Missing, creased or lifted shingles, especially on the windward side
  • Dented AC condenser fins or damaged patio furniture
  • Cracked or split siding and damaged window screens
  • New water stains on ceilings or walls after the storm
  • Debris or tree limbs resting on the roof
  • Shingle pieces in the yard

Frequently Asked Questions About Hail Damage in Wichita

How long do I have to file a hail damage claim in Kansas?

Kansas homeowner policies vary, but most require prompt reporting of known damage. Many policies have explicit filing deadlines ranging from one to two years from the date of loss. Filing earlier is always better because storm evidence on roofs weathers and degrades over time, making later assessments less definitive. Contact your insurer as soon as you confirm damage to protect your claim timeline.

Can I tell if my roof has hail damage without climbing up?

Ground-level indicators are reliable early signals. Check your aluminum window screens for round impact dimples, examine AC condenser fins for scattered dents, and look at your downspouts for spatter marks at the elbow joints. Granule accumulation in gutters significantly above baseline is another strong indicator. None of these replace a professional rooftop inspection, but they help you determine whether a claim is worth pursuing before calling a contractor.

What is functional hail damage versus cosmetic damage?

Functional hail damage compromises the roof’s ability to protect the home from water intrusion. It includes shingle bruising where the mat is fractured, granule loss deep enough to expose the asphalt layer, and impact damage to flashing or seals. Cosmetic damage alters appearance but does not affect waterproofing performance, such as minor surface scuffs on metal trim. Insurance policies differ in how they cover cosmetic-only damage; review your policy language or ask your insurer for clarification specific to your coverage.

Do I need a contractor present during the adjuster’s visit?

Having a contractor or roofing professional present during the adjuster inspection is generally beneficial. They can point out damage that might be overlooked from a quick walk, explain findings using standard roofing terminology the adjuster uses, and ensure the inspection covers all vulnerable areas including gutters, skylights, and soft metals. Wichita Roofing Pros regularly assists homeowners through the adjuster process as part of their inspection service.

What happens if my insurance claim is denied for hail damage?

Denial is not the final word. Kansas homeowners have the right to request a re-inspection, hire a public adjuster at their own cost to prepare an independent estimate, or invoke the appraisal clause in their policy if the dispute involves damage scope or value. Document all communications with your insurer in writing. If the dispute remains unresolved, the Kansas Department of Insurance has a consumer assistance process available to policyholders facing claim disputes.

How Do You Evaluate a Roofing Contractor After a Wichita Hailstorm?

Following a major hail event in Wichita, the number of active roofing contractors in the market spikes significantly. Some are established local businesses with years of Sedgwick County experience and permanent crews. Others are traveling storm-chasing operations with limited local accountability after the job is finished. Evaluating contractors quickly but carefully protects you from both poor workmanship and consumer protection issues. Start with three questions: Does the contractor have a permanent Kansas business address? Do they carry current general liability and workers’ compensation insurance with certificates naming your property? Can they provide references from recent Wichita-area customers whose roofs you can inspect in person or via photos?

Beyond those initial filters, review the proposed contract before signing anything. The scope of work should identify the specific shingle product, color, and manufacturer by name. Disposal and permitting should be explicitly included. The workmanship warranty period should be stated in the contract, not just referenced verbally. Payment terms should tie to project milestones rather than requiring a large deposit before material delivery. If a contractor requires more than 30 to 40 percent upfront, that is a signal to proceed with caution. The Storm & Hail Damage in Wichita team provides written estimates with full scope detail so homeowners have a benchmark to compare against when reviewing other bids. Well-documented storm work also protects you if the insurance carrier needs to review supplemental items during the claim settlement process.
