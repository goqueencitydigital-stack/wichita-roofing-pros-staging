When Does Sedgwick County Code Require a Full Tear-Off?

Kansas building codes adopted by Sedgwick County and the City of Wichita generally follow the International Residential Code, which limits most residential structures to two layers of roofing material. If a home already has two layers of shingles on the roof, a third layer is not permitted by code regardless of the condition of the existing material. A full tear-off is required by rule, not just by preference.

Verifying the layer count is a standard part of any pre-replacement inspection. Contractors can typically determine the number of existing layers by examining the roof edge, checking tab thickness at the eaves, or looking at exposed sections at chimneys or vents. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s that have never had a full tear-off often carry two layers and trigger the code requirement automatically on the next replacement.

Permit requirements apply to full tear-off replacements in the Wichita and Sedgwick County area. The Sedgwick County MABCD permits office handles permit applications for properties in unincorporated Sedgwick County, while City of Wichita properties use the city permit system. A licensed contractor should pull the required permit before work begins, and a final inspection closes the permit upon completion.

What Deck Conditions Make Tear-Off Necessary Regardless of Layer Count?

Even when a roof has only one existing layer, deck conditions may make tear-off the responsible choice. Installing new shingles over a deck that has soft spots, delaminated plywood, water-damaged OSB, or inadequate fastener penetration creates a system that will fail prematurely because the new roofing has nothing solid to hold it down.

Soft or spongy areas underfoot when walking a roof are the most common field sign of deck deterioration. These areas typically result from long-term moisture intrusion that has caused the OSB or plywood decking to swell, delaminate, or rot. A competent inspection distinguishes between minor surface moisture absorption that will dry out versus structural deterioration that requires panel replacement before new roofing can be properly fastened.

Inadequate nail penetration is a less visible but equally serious concern. Current code requires roofing nails to penetrate through the deck a minimum distance to achieve required wind resistance. If the existing deck material is too thin, has areas of deterioration, or has been compromised by previous work, new fasteners may not achieve proper withdrawal resistance. In Wichita and Sedgwick County, where wind events regularly test roof fastening systems, this matters significantly.

How Does Storm Damage Influence the Tear-Off Decision?

Major hail events in Sedgwick County frequently trigger insurance-funded roof replacements. The scope decision in these cases is particularly important because the insurance settlement is calculated on a specific replacement scope, and an improper re-cover when conditions require tear-off creates both a performance problem and a potential coverage gap on the next claim.

When a hailstorm causes sufficient damage to warrant replacement, most insurance adjusters will include tear-off and disposal in the replacement scope for a single-layer roof that otherwise qualifies for re-cover. This is because storm-damaged material that remains under new shingles creates unpredictable drainage paths, compressed areas, and potential warranty voidance issues with the new shingle manufacturer. The Kansas Department of Insurance storm claim data reflects the volume of replacement claims processed across Sedgwick County following significant storm seasons.

Wind damage that has lifted or removed shingles exposes deck areas to weather between the storm event and repairs. When water has penetrated through damaged areas, a thorough deck inspection during tear-off is essential to identify and replace compromised panels before new material is installed. Replacing one or two sheets of decking costs far less during a planned replacement than water damage repairs discovered after new shingles are already down.

What Is the Full Tear-Off Replacement Process in Sedgwick County?

A properly executed full tear-off follows a defined sequence that protects the building during the process and sets up the new roofing system for maximum service life.

The crew begins by protecting landscaping, HVAC equipment, and other ground-level features with tarps and magnetic nail collection tools. All existing shingles, underlayment, and flashings are removed down to the bare deck. The exposed deck is inspected for soft spots, delamination, fastener issues, and any areas that require panel replacement before proceeding. Deck repairs are documented and completed before the new system begins.

New underlayment, ice and water shield at eaves and penetrations, and drip edge are installed per manufacturer specifications and local code requirements. New flashings are fabricated and installed at all penetrations, chimneys, skylights, walls, and valleys. New shingles are applied with manufacturer-specified fastener patterns and fastened into solid deck material. All penetration boots, pipe jacks, and vent flashings are replaced as part of a complete system installation.

Top 6 Triggers That Mean a Full Tear-Off Is Required

  1. Two existing shingle layers: Code in the Wichita and Sedgwick County area prohibits a third layer on most residential structures. If two layers are present, tear-off is required before any new roofing can be installed.
  2. Soft or deteriorated decking: Wet, spongy, or delaminated deck panels cannot be safely covered with new shingles. The deck must be repaired or replaced, which requires removal of all existing material to access the problem areas.
  3. Active moisture in existing layers: If existing shingles or underlayment are wet or saturated, installing new material over them traps moisture against the deck and accelerates further deterioration.
  4. Insurance scope requiring tear-off: When a storm damage claim is approved for replacement, the insurance scope may specifically include tear-off and disposal. Substituting a re-cover when the scope calls for tear-off creates a gap between what was paid and what was delivered.
  5. Manufacturer warranty requirements: Some premium shingle manufacturer warranties require installation over a single layer on a sound deck as a condition of full warranty coverage. A re-cover on an existing layer may void the extended warranty entirely.
  6. Uneven substrate from previous repairs: Multiple generations of patch repairs, added layers over specific sections, and irregular surface conditions create an unlevel substrate that compromises new shingle installation. Tear-off creates a fresh, even starting surface.

How Do You Choose the Right Contractor for a Sedgwick County Tear-Off?

Full tear-off replacements involve more complexity and risk than simple re-cover jobs, and contractor selection matters accordingly. A contractor who does not pull permits, does not perform a detailed deck inspection, or skips replacing worn flashings is cutting steps that affect both code compliance and system longevity.

Verify that the contractor you hire carries current Kansas contractor licensing, holds general liability insurance and workers compensation coverage, and pulls the required permit for your property. A permit creates an inspection record that can be valuable at resale and confirms that the work was completed to code.

Homeowners in Sedgwick County who take the time to understand the tear-off decision before accepting a contractor’s scope recommendation are better positioned to evaluate bids accurately and avoid both overpaying for unnecessary work and accepting a re-cover that leaves problems in place. A knowledgeable second opinion from a contractor who inspects the deck, counts the existing layers, and probes for moisture before presenting a scope is always worth the time investment. Signs it may be time to replace, not repair

One sign alone does not always mean replacement. Several together usually do.

  • Roof age near or past 20 years
  • Widespread granule loss or bald shingles
  • Curling, cupping or cracked shingles across slopes
  • Repeated leaks in different spots
  • Soft or sagging decking
  • Storm damage on multiple slopes
  • Daylight through attic boards
  • Neighbors replacing roofs after the same storm

Frequently Asked Questions About Full Tear-Off Replacement in Sedgwick County

How much does a full tear-off replacement cost in Sedgwick County?

Full tear-off replacement costs in Sedgwick County vary by roof size, pitch, number of penetrations, material selection, and deck condition found during the project. Tear-off and disposal labor adds to the baseline material and installation cost versus a re-cover. Requesting a detailed written estimate that separates tear-off, deck repairs, materials, accessories, and flashings helps homeowners compare bids accurately and understand exactly what each proposal includes.

How long does a full tear-off replacement take in Wichita?

Most standard residential tear-off replacements in the Wichita area are completed within one to two days for average-sized homes, weather permitting. Larger homes, complex roof geometry with multiple hips and valleys, or significant deck repair needs extend the timeline. Contractors typically schedule tear-off and replacement consecutively so the home is not left with exposed decking overnight if conditions allow, though weather delays can create gaps.

What happens to the old shingles after tear-off?

Removed shingles and underlayment are loaded into dumpsters or trailers and transported to a licensed disposal facility. Asphalt shingles are often recycled into aggregate for road base and paving projects in Kansas. A reputable contractor includes debris removal and disposal in the project scope. Homeowners should confirm that disposal fees are included in the written contract to avoid unexpected charges after project completion.

Can a tear-off replacement be covered by insurance in Kansas?

Yes. When storm damage has caused sufficient damage to warrant full replacement, the homeowner’s insurance policy typically covers tear-off and disposal costs as part of the replacement scope. The specific coverage depends on policy terms, the deductible, and whether the policy pays actual cash value or replacement cost value. A professional damage inspection report from a licensed contractor strengthens the claim file and supports the adjuster’s scope decision.

How do you verify how many shingle layers are on a Sedgwick County roof?

A contractor can assess layer count by examining the roof edge profile, measuring total shingle thickness at the eave, or checking exposed areas near penetrations and chimneys. In some cases, probing an edge with a flat bar reveals the layer structure. An accurate layer count is determined before any proposal is presented so that the scope reflects actual code requirements and conditions on the specific property.
