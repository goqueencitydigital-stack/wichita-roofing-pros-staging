Why Gutters Matter

What does a gutter system actually protect on a Wichita home?

Gutters are not decoration. They collect the water your roof sheds and move it to a place where it cannot hurt the house.

01

The roof edge and fascia

Without a working gutter, water runs off the drip edge and soaks the fascia board behind it. Over a few seasons, wet fascia softens, paint peels and the wood can rot. Rotted fascia also loosens the gutter hangers, which makes the problem worse.

A gutter that holds water against the board because it is clogged or pitched the wrong way does the same damage, only faster.

02

Siding, windows and doors

When rain pours over the front of a gutter, it splashes against siding, window trim and door thresholds. Repeated soaking stains siding, breaks down caulk joints and can push water behind trim.

On homes with wood or engineered wood siding, the bottom courses near downspouts often show the first signs of swelling.

03

Foundations and basements

A roof sheds a lot of water. A 1,500 square foot roof collects roughly 900 gallons from a single inch of rain. When that water lands next to the foundation, it saturates the soil and looks for a way in.

Many Wichita basement leaks start with a downspout that empties right at the wall or a gutter that overflows in the same spot every storm.

04

Landscaping, walks and driveways

Overflow cuts trenches in mulch beds, washes out plants and leaves dirt lines across patios. In winter, water released onto walkways and driveways can refreeze into slick patches.

Downspout extensions and splash blocks carry water away from those areas, so landscaping and concrete stay where they belong.

Wichita Weather

Why do gutters work harder in south-central Kansas?

Our weather swings from dry spells to intense downpours, and gutters have to handle both.

Short, heavy thunderstorms. Spring and early-summer storms in the Wichita area often drop a lot of rain in a short time. A gutter that seems fine in a light shower can overflow badly when an inch falls in less than an hour. Sizing and downspout placement matter most in those storms.

Hail and wind. Hail dents gutters and downspouts, and strong wind pulls loose sections away from the fascia. Dented gutters can still carry water, but crushed downspouts and separated seams usually need repair. Gutter damage is also a useful clue when we document a roof for an insurance claim.

Tree cover in older neighborhoods. Mature trees in neighborhoods like College Hill, Riverside and parts of east Wichita drop leaves, seeds and twigs into gutters several times a year. Those homes are the most likely to benefit from gutter guards.

Freeze and thaw. When clogged gutters hold water in winter, the ice adds weight and can pull hangers loose. It can also back water up under the first course of shingles. Clean, free-draining gutters reduce that risk.

Choosing the Right Work

Do you need gutter repair, new gutters or gutter guards?

Most homeowners call about one symptom. The right answer depends on the condition of the whole system.

01

Repair makes sense when

  • The gutters are structurally sound and mostly straight
  • Problems are limited to a few leaking corners, loose hangers or a damaged downspout
  • The fascia behind the gutter is still solid

See gutter repair for the details.

02

New seamless gutters make sense when

  • Sections sag in several places or have been patched many times
  • The system is undersized for the roof and overflows in normal storms
  • Hail damage, rust or separated seams run along most of the house
  • You are replacing the roof and want the edge done correctly at the same time

See seamless gutter installation.

03

Gutter guards make sense when

  • Trees overhang the roof and you clean gutters more than twice a year
  • Downspouts clog and overflow even though the gutters are in good shape
  • Climbing a ladder to clean gutters is no longer practical or safe for you

See gutter guards.

Roof and Gutters Together

How do gutters connect to the roof above them?

Gutters and roofs are installed by different trades on many homes. The problems often show up where the two meet.

01

Drip edge

Drip edge is the metal strip along the roof edge that directs water into the gutter instead of behind it. A missing or short drip edge lets water run down the fascia even when the gutter is perfect.

02

Valleys that overload one spot

Where two roof planes meet in a valley, water concentrates and can shoot past the gutter during heavy rain. A diverter, a larger gutter or a downspout near the valley often solves it.

03

Gutter height and slope

A gutter hung too low misses water from the roof. A gutter hung too high can catch the shingle edge or trap water under it. Slope toward the downspouts has to be consistent along the full run.

04

Timing with a new roof

When you replace the roof, it is the best time to fix drip edge, rehang or replace gutters and repair fascia. Coordinating both at once avoids tearing into a new roof edge later.

Our Process

What happens when we look at your gutters?

Every gutter visit follows the same order so the recommendation fits what the house actually needs.

We look from the ground first. We walk the house and note where water has stained siding, cut into mulch beds or pooled near the foundation. Those marks show where the system fails during real storms.

Then we check the gutters up close. We look at the slope, hangers, seams, end caps and outlets. We check the fascia behind the gutter for soft spots and look at the drip edge and first course of shingles.

We run the downspouts. We check that each downspout is clear, connected and discharging far enough from the house. Underground drain lines are noted if they back up.

You get clear options. We explain whether repair, new gutters, guards or roof-edge work will solve the problem, and we provide the price in writing. If the roof itself needs attention, we tell you that too, and if it does not, we say so.

Signs of Failure

What are the warning signs that gutters are failing?

You can spot most gutter problems from the yard.

01

Water spilling over during rain

Overflow means the gutter is clogged, undersized or pitched wrong.

02

Sagging sections

Sagging means loose hangers or soft fascia.

03

Peeling fascia paint

Water is getting behind the gutter or under the drip edge.

04

Puddles near the foundation

Downspouts are not carrying water far enough away.

05

Plants growing in the gutter

Debris has built up enough to hold soil and water.

06

Rust or holes

Steel gutters are corroding and may need replacement.

Gutter Budget

What affects the price of gutter work?

Every gutter quote depends on the house. These are the factors that move the number most.

Total length. Gutters are priced largely by the length of roof edge they cover, plus the number of downspouts.

Height and access. Two-story runs, steep slopes and tight side yards take more time and equipment than a single-story ranch.

Corners and complexity. Homes with many inside and outside corners, bump-outs and short runs require more cutting, sealing and fastening.

Fascia condition. Soft fascia must be replaced before new gutters go on. That cost is listed separately in the written price.

Guards and extras. Gutter guards, larger downspouts, underground drain connections and extensions add to the scope.

We give you the price in writing after we see the house, with every item spelled out.

Gutters & Gutter Guards FAQ

When do I need gutters and gutter guards?
You may need gutters and gutter guards when you see leaks, missing materials, stains, loose flashing, storm damage, or repeated problems in the same roof area. A roof inspection helps separate a small repair from a larger issue before water reaches decking, insulation, or interior finishes.
Can gutters and gutter guards be repaired instead of replaced?
Sometimes, yes. A newer roof with isolated damage may only need a focused repair. An older roof with widespread wear, repeated leaks, soft decking, or storm damage across several slopes may need replacement planning instead. The inspection should answer that question before you spend money.
Does Wichita weather affect gutters and gutter guards?
Yes. Wichita roofs deal with hail, strong wind, sudden rain, heat, and fast temperature changes. Those conditions can lift shingles, stress flashing, loosen gutters, and open small leak paths. That is why we check the full area around the visible problem.
What other roof work might be connected?
This depends on what we find. Some gutters and gutter guards calls connect to seamless gutters, gutter repair, gutter guards. Others connect to gutters, siding, flashing, or ventilation. We keep those recommendations tied to the actual roof condition, not a pre-set package.

More Answers

More Questions About Gutters & Gutter Guards

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

How often should gutters be cleaned in Wichita?
Most homes do well with a cleaning in late spring after seeds drop and again in late fall after leaves fall. Homes under large trees may need more frequent cleaning or gutter guards.
Where should downspouts discharge?
Downspouts should release water several feet away from the foundation onto ground that slopes away from the house. Extensions, splash blocks or connected drain lines all help. The residential building code calls for the ground next to a foundation to slope away from the house.
Can hail-damaged gutters be part of an insurance claim?
Often, yes. Dented gutters and downspouts are commonly documented with roof damage after a hailstorm. Coverage depends on your policy, so we photograph the damage and you can share it with your insurer.
Do you install gutters when you replace a roof?
Yes. We can coordinate gutter work with a roof replacement so the drip edge, fascia and gutters are handled together.
Can you fix gutters without replacing the roof?
Yes. Most gutter repairs and many gutter replacements happen on roofs that are still in good shape.
Do you clean gutters?
Gutter cleaning can be part of a repair, guard installation or roof visit. Ask us about your gutters when you schedule.
Can gutters be installed in winter?
Gutters can be installed in most weather, as long as conditions are safe for crews to work on ladders. Very cold days can slow sealant curing, which we account for.
Should I replace gutters before or after a new roof?
Ideally together or right after. The roof’s drip edge and the gutters work as a system, and coordinating both avoids disturbing new work.
How many downspouts does a house need?
It depends on roof area, pitch and gutter size. Long runs and steep roofs usually need more downspouts so water does not overflow at the far end.
What is the difference between K-style and half-round gutters?
K-style gutters have a flat back and decorative front and hold more water for their width. Half-round gutters have a rounded shape that suits some older or traditional homes.
