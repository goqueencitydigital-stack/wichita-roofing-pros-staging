Overview

If you’re looking at seamless gutter installation in Wichita, the first question is usually simple: can the roof be repaired safely, or is the problem part of a bigger pattern? Wichita Roofing Pros checks roof runoff, fascia condition, gutter length, downspout locations, drainage exits, and any roof valleys dumping heavy water before recommending work. Around Wichita, hail, high wind, sudden downpours, and hot summers can all shorten the life of roofing materials. We explain what we see, show you the areas that matter, and help you choose the next step without turning one roof concern into a bigger project than it needs to be.

Quick answerSeamless gutters are formed on site to the exact length of each roof edge, so there are no seams along the run to leak. Properly sized and pitched seamless gutters move water off the roof edge and away from the foundation, which protects fascia, siding and basements.

Symptoms

What signs mean you should call?

These are the signs homeowners most often notice first. Any one of them is worth a free inspection.

  • Water spilling over gutters in heavy rain
  • Leaking seams or corners
  • Sagging or pulling gutters
  • Rotting fascia behind gutters
  • Erosion or pooling near the foundation

Seamless vs. Sectional

How are seamless gutters different from sectional gutters?

The difference is how the gutter is made and how many joints it has along each run.

01

Formed on site to the exact length

Seamless gutters are formed from a coil of metal at the house, so each run is one continuous piece cut to the length of that roof edge. The only joints are at corners and end caps.

02

Sectional gutters have joints every few feet

Sectional gutters come in fixed lengths and are joined together along the run. Every one of those joints relies on sealant, and sealant ages in Kansas heat and cold. That is where most leaks start.

03

Cleaner look

With no seams along the run, seamless gutters look cleaner from the street and come in many colors to match trim, fascia or roof edge metal.

04

Fewer places for debris to catch

Seams can create small ridges inside the gutter that catch debris. A continuous run lets water and fine debris move toward the outlet more freely.

Sizing

How do we size gutters and downspouts for your roof?

Sizing is where many gutter systems go wrong. A good-looking gutter that is too small still overflows.

Roof area and pitch. A larger, steeper roof sends more water to the gutter faster. We measure the roof area draining to each run and account for pitch.

Gutter size. Many homes use 5-inch K-style gutters. Larger roofs, steep pitches and runs below valleys may benefit from 6-inch gutters, which carry noticeably more water. We recommend the size that fits each part of the house rather than one size everywhere.

Downspout size and count. Downspouts are often the real bottleneck. Larger downspouts and more of them move water out of the gutter before it overflows. Long runs usually need a downspout at each end or one in the middle.

Valleys and concentrated flow. Where two roof planes meet, water concentrates. We place downspouts near valleys or add diverters so that one section does not overflow every storm.

Where the water goes. A properly sized system still fails if downspouts empty next to the foundation. We plan extensions, splash blocks or connections to existing drain lines so water ends up away from the house.

Installation Details

What separates a good gutter installation from a poor one?

The details below are what keep gutters straight, dry and attached for years.

01

Consistent slope

Each run needs a steady slope toward its downspout. Too little slope leaves standing water. Too much looks crooked from the street. We snap lines and check slope before hanging.

02

Hidden hangers at close spacing

Hidden hangers fasten into the fascia and rafter tails with screws. Close spacing keeps the gutter from sagging under water, ice or debris.

03

Correct position under the roof edge

The back of the gutter sits under the drip edge so roof water lands inside the gutter, not behind it. We correct drip edge where it is missing or short.

04

Sealed corners and outlets

Corners, end caps and outlets are the only joints on a seamless run. We seal and fasten them carefully because they carry the leak risk for the whole system.

05

Sound fascia first

New gutters need solid wood behind them. Soft fascia is replaced before installation, and the cost is included in your written price.

06

Clean finish

We remove old gutters and debris, clean up the site and check that every downspout discharges where it should.

Planning

When is the best time to install new gutters?

Timing can save money and keep the roof edge in better shape.

01

With a roof replacement

Replacing the roof and gutters together lets us correct drip edge, fascia and gutters as one system. It avoids removing new gutters later to fix a roof edge, or disturbing a new roof edge to hang new gutters.

02

After a hailstorm

If hail damaged both the roof and the gutters, the repairs are often documented together. We photograph the gutter damage alongside the roof damage. See storm and hail damage.

03

Before a sale

Overflowing gutters and stained fascia stand out during a home inspection. Replacing worn gutters before listing removes an easy objection.

04

When repairs keep repeating

If the same gutters have been resealed and rehung several times, a new seamless system is usually the more economical choice over the next few years.

Gutter Styles

What gutter styles are available?

Most homes use K-style gutters, but other styles fit certain designs.

01

K-style

K-style gutters have a flat back and a decorative front that looks like crown molding. They carry a lot of water for their size and are the most common style on Wichita homes.

02

Half-round

Half-round gutters have a rounded shape that suits historic and traditional homes. They are mounted with brackets and pair with round downspouts.

03

Fascia-style

Fascia-style gutters have a smooth front that covers the fascia board for a clean, modern look.

04

Downspout shapes

Rectangular downspouts are the most common. Larger downspouts carry more water and clog less often.

Common Mistakes

What gutter installation mistakes cause problems later?

We see these mistakes on homes where gutters were installed without enough planning.

Hanging the gutter flat. A gutter with no slope holds water, which adds weight, breeds mosquitoes and rots the fascia.

Too few downspouts. One downspout for a long run of roof is a common cause of overflow.

Missing drip edge. Without drip edge, water runs behind the gutter even when the gutter is new.

Mounting into soft wood. Hangers fastened into soft fascia pull loose within a few seasons.

Ignoring valleys. Valleys that dump water at one point need a downspout nearby or a diverter.

Downspouts that end at the wall. Water still reaches the foundation if downspouts do not discharge away from the house.

Color and Finish

How do you pick a gutter color?

Gutters frame the roofline, so color makes a visible difference.

01

Match the trim

Matching gutters to the fascia and trim makes them blend into the roofline. This is the most common choice.

02

Match the roof

Darker gutters that match the shingles or roof edge can make the roofline look sharper on homes with light siding.

03

Contrast on purpose

A contrasting color, such as dark gutters on a white house, gives a modern, defined look.

04

Think about downspouts

Downspouts run down the walls. Many homeowners match downspouts to the siding so they disappear, even if gutters match the trim.

When do I need seamless gutter installation?
You may need seamless gutter installation when you see leaks, missing materials, stains, loose flashing, storm damage, or repeated problems in the same roof area. A roof inspection helps separate a small repair from a larger issue before water reaches decking, insulation, or interior finishes.
Can seamless gutter installation be repaired instead of replaced?
Sometimes, yes. A newer roof with isolated damage may only need a focused repair. An older roof with widespread wear, repeated leaks, soft decking, or storm damage across several slopes may need replacement planning instead. The inspection should answer that question before you spend money.
Does Wichita weather affect seamless gutter installation?
Yes. Wichita roofs deal with hail, strong wind, sudden rain, heat, and fast temperature changes. Those conditions can lift shingles, stress flashing, loosen gutters, and open small leak paths. That is why we check the full area around the visible problem.
What other roof work might be connected?
This depends on what we find. Some seamless gutter installation calls connect to gutters and gutter guards, gutter repair, gutter guards. Others connect to gutters, siding, flashing, or ventilation. We keep those recommendations tied to the actual roof condition, not a pre-set package.

More Answers

More Questions About Seamless Gutter Installation

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

What size gutters do Wichita homes need?
Many homes use 5-inch gutters, but larger roofs and heavy downpours can call for 6-inch. Read our gutter size guide.
Should I replace fascia before new gutters?
If the fascia is soft or rotted, yes. New gutters need solid fascia to hold them.
What material are seamless gutters made from?
Most residential seamless gutters are made from painted aluminum, which does not rust and can be formed on site in long runs. We confirm the material and color in your written estimate.
Can seamless gutters match my trim color?
Yes. Seamless gutters are available in a range of colors, so they can match your trim, fascia or roof edge.
How long does installation take?
It depends on the length of gutter, the height of the home and whether fascia repair is needed. We give you the expected timeline in advance.
Do I need 6-inch gutters?
Not always. Six-inch gutters are useful for larger or steeper roofs and below valleys. Many homes work well with 5-inch gutters and properly sized downspouts. We recommend sizes after measuring.
Will new gutters fix water in my basement?
They can help a great deal when the water comes from overflow or downspouts emptying near the foundation. Grading, soil and foundation cracks can also play a role, so we point out anything we notice.
Do new gutters come with a warranty?
Gutter work is backed by our workmanship. We explain warranty coverage for your specific project in the written estimate.
How are seamless gutters made on site?
A roll-forming machine shapes coil stock into continuous gutter lengths cut to fit each run of your home, so the only joints are at corners and ends.
Do seamless gutters need maintenance?
They still need cleaning. Fewer joints mean fewer places to leak, but leaves and debris will collect without guards.
How long do aluminum seamless gutters last?
Well-installed aluminum gutters often last for decades. Secure hangers, correct slope and regular cleaning help them reach that lifespan.
