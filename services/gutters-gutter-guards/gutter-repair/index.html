Common Problems

What are the most common gutter problems we repair in Wichita?

Most gutter calls come down to a short list of failures. Each one has a clear cause and a practical fix.

01

Leaking corners and seams

Inside and outside corners are joined pieces, and the sealant that holds them can crack after years of heat and cold. Water drips from the corner during every rain. We clean the joint, reseal or rivet it and check that the corner is not being stressed by a sagging run.

02

Sagging or pulling away

Gutters sag when hangers loosen, rust or are spaced too far apart, or when the fascia behind them softens. A sagging gutter holds water, which adds weight and makes it sag more. We reset or add hangers and repair the fascia where it will not hold.

03

Water behind the gutter

If water runs down between the gutter and the fascia, the drip edge is usually missing, short or installed behind the gutter instead of inside it. Correcting the roof edge stops the staining and rot.

04

Overflow in the same spot

Repeated overflow at one point usually means a clog at the outlet, a low spot in the run or a valley dumping more water than that section can carry. The fix may be cleaning, re-pitching the run or adding a downspout.

05

Crushed or disconnected downspouts

Lawn mowers, ladders and hail all damage downspouts. A separated elbow or a crushed section can send water straight to the foundation. We reconnect, replace damaged pieces and add extensions so water discharges away from the house.

06

Hail dents and storm damage

Hail leaves round dents in gutters and downspouts. Light dents may be cosmetic, but split seams and crushed sections need repair. We photograph storm damage for homeowners who are documenting a claim.

Repair or Replace

How do we decide whether to repair or replace a gutter?

We repair when repair will last. We recommend replacement only when patching would cost you more over time.

Repair is usually the right call when:

  • Problems are limited to a few corners, seams or hangers
  • The gutter is straight and holds its slope after it is reset
  • The metal is not rusted through or split in several places
  • The fascia behind it is solid or needs only small repairs

Replacement usually makes more sense when:

  • The same runs have been patched several times and still leak
  • Long sections sag even after new hangers are added
  • The gutters are undersized and overflow in normal storms
  • Hail or wind damaged most of the system
  • Older sectional gutters have a seam every 10 feet that keeps failing

If replacement is the better value, we explain why and give you both options in writing. See seamless gutter installation for what a new system involves.

Hidden Damage

What damage can a failing gutter cause that you cannot see from the yard?

Gutter problems rarely stay in the gutter. These are the areas we check when we find a long-running leak.

01

Fascia and soffit rot

Water that sits against fascia for months softens the wood and can travel into the soffit. Soft fascia is easy to miss behind a gutter. We probe it during every repair. See soffit, fascia and trim.

02

Roof deck at the eave

When water backs up over the back edge of a gutter, it can soak the first few feet of roof decking. That shows up as soft spots at the edge or stains in the attic near the eaves.

03

Siding and window trim

Overflow splashing onto siding stains it and can work behind trim and window casing. Engineered wood siding near the ground swells when it stays wet.

04

Foundation and basement walls

Water released next to the house saturates the soil. Over time, that can show up as damp basement walls, efflorescence or cracks in slabs and walks near the downspout.

Homeowner Tips

How can you spot gutter problems early?

You do not need a ladder to catch most gutter problems. Watch your home during and after a heavy rain.

During a storm, look for sheets of water pouring over the front of the gutter, drips at corners and water running down the face of the fascia.

After a storm, look for wet soil or puddles next to the foundation, splash marks on siding, new trenches in mulch beds and water lines on walkways.

On a dry day, look along the roofline for gutters that dip, hangers that have pulled out and gaps between the gutter and fascia. Peeling paint on fascia boards is often the first visible sign of water behind the gutter.

Inside the home, watch for damp spots on basement walls near downspouts and stains on ceilings close to exterior walls.

If you notice any of these, call (316) 742-1900. We can usually look at the gutters during a free roof inspection.

Downspouts and Drainage

How do we fix downspout and drainage problems?

Many gutter problems are really downspout problems. Water has to leave the gutter and the house quickly.

01

Clogged outlets and elbows

Debris collects where the gutter drops into the downspout and at each bend. We clear clogs and may add a larger outlet where clogs keep returning.

02

Short or missing extensions

A downspout that empties at the base of the wall puts water where it can reach the foundation. Extensions or splash blocks carry it several feet away.

03

Underground drain lines

Buried drain pipes can clog or collapse. If water backs up out of the downspout, the underground line may need cleaning or a new outlet.

04

Too few downspouts

Long runs with one downspout overflow in heavy rain. Adding a downspout cuts the distance water has to travel.

Seasonal Checks

What should Wichita homeowners check each season?

A short seasonal routine keeps gutters working and catches problems before they become repairs.

Spring. Clear seeds and debris from winter and early spring. Check that downspouts drain and extensions are in place before storm season.

Summer. After major storms, look for overflow spots, dented sections and loose hangers. Watch for cottonwood fluff mats in early summer.

Fall. Clean gutters after most leaves have dropped. Check that water drains before freezing weather arrives.

Winter. Watch for icicles along one section of gutter, which can point to a clog or a low spot. Do not chip ice off the gutter, which can damage it.

Fascia Repairs

What happens when the fascia behind a gutter is rotted?

Rotted fascia is the most common surprise during gutter repair.

We find it before we start. We probe the fascia along each run during the inspection, so rot is identified and priced before work begins.

The gutter comes down. To replace fascia, we carefully remove the gutter, set it aside and inspect the rafter tails and roof deck behind the board.

New board goes up. We install new fascia, prime and paint it or wrap it in aluminum, and correct the drip edge at the same time.

The gutter goes back on with fresh hangers. If the gutter is in good condition, we reinstall it with new hidden hangers and reset the slope. If not, we replace it.

Handling the fascia and gutter together means you are not paying for two separate visits, and the roof edge is sealed properly.

Gutter Repair FAQ

When do I need gutter repair?
You may need gutter repair when you see leaks, missing materials, stains, loose flashing, storm damage, or repeated problems in the same roof area. A roof inspection helps separate a small repair from a larger issue before water reaches decking, insulation, or interior finishes.
Can gutter repair be repaired instead of replaced?
Sometimes, yes. A newer roof with isolated damage may only need a focused repair. An older roof with widespread wear, repeated leaks, soft decking, or storm damage across several slopes may need replacement planning instead. The inspection should answer that question before you spend money.
Does Wichita weather affect gutter repair?
Yes. Wichita roofs deal with hail, strong wind, sudden rain, heat, and fast temperature changes. Those conditions can lift shingles, stress flashing, loosen gutters, and open small leak paths. That is why we check the full area around the visible problem.
What other roof work might be connected?
This depends on what we find. Some gutter repair calls connect to gutters and gutter guards, seamless gutters, gutter guards. Others connect to gutters, siding, flashing, or ventilation. We keep those recommendations tied to the actual roof condition, not a pre-set package.

More Answers

More Questions About Gutter Repair

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

Can a sagging gutter be fixed without replacing it?
Usually, yes. Most sagging comes from loose or widely spaced hangers. Resetting the run with new hangers fixes it, as long as the fascia behind it is solid.
Why do my gutters overflow even when they are clean?
The gutter may be pitched the wrong way, too small for the roof area, or receiving concentrated water from a valley. A larger gutter, another downspout or a diverter often solves it.
Is gutter repair covered by homeowners insurance?
Sudden storm damage such as hail or wind may be covered, depending on your policy. Normal wear and clogs usually are not. We document storm damage with photos so you can share them with your insurer.
How long does a gutter repair take?
Many repairs can be handled in one visit. Larger repairs that include fascia replacement may take longer, and we tell you the timeline in advance.
Should I fix the gutters before or after a roof replacement?
If the roof will be replaced soon, it is usually best to handle gutters at the same time so the drip edge, fascia and gutters are corrected together.
Can you repair copper or steel gutters?
Tell us what material your gutters are made of when you call. We will look at them and tell you what repair options make sense.
Why do my gutters drip at the seams?
Seams and corners rely on sealant that ages over time. Resealing or riveting the joint usually stops the drip.
Why does my gutter overflow even when it is clean?
An overflowing clean gutter usually points to poor slope, an undersized downspout or too few downspouts for the roof area draining into it.
Can a sagging gutter be fixed without replacing it?
Often. Resetting the slope and adding new hidden hangers fixes most sagging, as long as the gutter and fascia are still in good shape.
Are gutter leaks at the corners common?
Yes. Corners and end caps are sealed joints, and the sealant ages over time. Resealing these spots is one of the most common gutter repairs.
