Overview

If you’re looking at commercial roof inspection in Wichita, the first question is usually simple: can the roof be repaired safely, or is the problem part of a bigger pattern? Wichita Roofing Pros checks drains, seams, flashing, membrane surface, roof penetrations, edge metal, and areas around HVAC curbs before recommending work. Around Wichita, hail, high wind, sudden downpours, and hot summers can all shorten the life of roofing materials. We explain what we see, show you the areas that matter, and help you choose the next step without turning one roof concern into a bigger project than it needs to be.

Why Inspect

Why do commercial roofs need scheduled inspections?

Flat and low-slope roofs hide problems until water comes through the ceiling. By then, insulation and decking may already be wet.

01

Small issues stay small

A split seam, a loose coping joint or a clogged drain is a minor repair when it is caught early. Left alone for a season, the same issue can soak insulation, rust deck panels and damage inventory below.

02

Storm documentation

A dated inspection report before storm season gives you a record of the roof's condition. If hail hits, you can show what changed, which makes insurance conversations clearer.

03

Warranty compliance

Many manufacturer warranties for commercial membranes expect the owner to maintain the roof, keep drains clear and repair damage promptly. Scheduled inspections help you keep that record.

04

Budget planning

A series of inspection reports shows how the roof is aging over time. That history helps owners plan repairs and replacement years in advance instead of reacting to a leak.

What We Check

What does a commercial roof inspection cover?

A thorough commercial inspection looks at the whole roof assembly, from the edges to the drains.

01

Field membrane

We walk the field of the roof looking for blisters, punctures, cracks, exposed scrim, surface wear and areas of ponding water.

02

Seams and laps

We probe seams on single-ply roofs and check laps on modified bitumen and built-up roofs for separation.

03

Flashing and edges

We check base flashing at walls and curbs, metal edge, copings and termination bars for gaps, loose fasteners and failed sealant.

04

Drains and scuppers

We clear debris, check strainers and clamping rings, and look for signs of ponding near drains.

05

Rooftop equipment

We inspect curbs, condensate lines and service paths around HVAC units and exhaust fans.

06

Interior signs

When we can, we look at the underside of the deck and ceilings below for stains, rust and wet insulation.

Maintenance Tasks

What maintenance keeps a commercial roof performing?

Routine maintenance is mostly simple work done consistently.

01

Keep drains clear

Leaves, trash and debris build up around drains and scuppers. Clearing them twice a year and after major storms prevents ponding.

02

Reseal penetrations

Pitch pans and sealant at pipes and supports shrink and crack in Kansas heat. Resealing them before they open up is inexpensive and prevents leaks.

03

Repair seams and punctures

Small seam separations and punctures from foot traffic or dropped tools can be patched with compatible materials before water gets in.

04

Control foot traffic

Walkway pads around rooftop units protect the membrane from technicians and contractors. We can recommend where they are needed.

Inspection Schedule

How often should a Wichita commercial roof be inspected?

Our general recommendation for Wichita buildings follows the seasons.

Spring, before storm season. A spring inspection documents the roof before hail season and catches winter damage from freeze-thaw cycles and ice.

Fall, after summer heat. A fall inspection looks at the effects of summer heat on seams and sealants and clears drains before leaves and winter weather arrive.

After major storms. Any time significant hail or high wind crosses your building, a quick inspection confirms whether there is new damage. Storm damage is easier to document and claim when it is found promptly.

After rooftop work. When HVAC units are replaced or new equipment is installed, an inspection makes sure the new curbs and penetrations were flashed correctly and that the membrane was not damaged during the work.

Older roofs, roofs with known issues and buildings with sensitive operations below may benefit from more frequent checks.

Common Findings

What do we find most often on Wichita commercial roof inspections?

After years of walking commercial roofs in Kansas, a few findings come up again and again.

01

Clogged drains after fall

Leaves, plastic bags and debris collect at drains and scuppers every fall. A clogged drain after a heavy rain can put thousands of pounds of water on a roof.

02

Open seams near rooftop units

Areas around HVAC units see the most foot traffic and the most heat, so seams there open first.

03

Cracked sealant at penetrations

Sealant around pipes and pitch pans shrinks in summer heat and cracks in winter cold. It is a small fix that prevents a lot of leaks.

04

Loose metal edge and coping

Wind loosens edge metal and coping caps over time. Loose metal can let water in and can be torn off in the next storm.

05

Ponding in low areas

Deflection in the deck or settled insulation creates low spots that hold water for days after a rain.

06

Previous repairs that did not hold

Mismatched patches, roof cement over membranes and caulk over flashings often fail within a season. We note them so they can be repaired correctly.

For Property Managers

How can property managers use inspection reports?

A good inspection report is a management tool, not just a list of problems.

Share with owners and boards. Clear photos and priority levels make it easier to explain why roof work is needed and what it will cost.

Plan capital budgets. Reports over several years show how quickly the roof is aging, which helps you forecast replacement and avoid surprise expenses.

Hold contractors accountable. When HVAC or other trades work on the roof, before-and-after inspections show whether they caused damage.

Support insurance claims. A recent inspection report helps show that damage after a storm is new, not pre-existing.

Keep tenants informed. When repairs are scheduled, a short summary from the report helps tenants understand what to expect.

Warning Signs

What signs mean a commercial roof needs attention between inspections?

Facility staff can watch for a few warning signs.

01

Ceiling stains or drips

Even small stains mean water is getting through somewhere. The source may be far from the stain.

02

Standing water after 48 hours

Water that remains two days after a rain points to drainage problems.

03

Rust on the deck underside

Rust on steel decking seen from inside means moisture has been present for some time.

04

Higher energy bills

Wet insulation loses its insulating value, which can show up as higher heating and cooling costs.

Kansas Weather

How does Kansas weather wear down commercial roofs?

Wichita's climate puts every commercial roof through a demanding cycle each year.

Summer heat. Roof surfaces can get far hotter than the air on a July afternoon. Heat dries out asphalt-based roofs, softens sealants and stresses seams on single-ply membranes.

Rapid temperature swings. A sunny afternoon followed by a cool storm can drop the surface temperature quickly. That expansion and contraction loosens fasteners and opens laps over time.

Spring hail. Hail can bruise modified bitumen, fracture older TPO and crush insulation boards. Damage under the membrane is not always visible.

High wind. Strong gusts lift edges, peel coping caps and flutter mechanically fastened membranes.

Winter freeze-thaw. Water that gets into cracks freezes and expands, widening small openings into leaks by spring.

Scheduled inspections in spring and fall are timed to catch the effects of each season before the next one arrives.

Maintenance Value

Is commercial roof maintenance worth the cost?

For most buildings, the answer is yes.

01

Avoid emergency calls

Emergency leak calls during a storm cost more and disrupt operations. Routine maintenance reduces how often they happen.

02

Protect what is below

Inventory, equipment, finishes and tenant spaces can be worth far more than the roof repair that would have prevented the damage.

03

Extend service life

Clearing drains, sealing penetrations and fixing seams helps a roof reach its expected life instead of failing early.

04

Plan replacement on your terms

When replacement does come, maintenance records let you plan it in the right season and budget year.

Maintenance Calendar

What does a year of commercial roof maintenance look like in Wichita?

A simple calendar keeps small problems from turning into interior leaks during the busiest storm months.

01

Early spring, before storm season

Clear drains and scuppers of winter debris, check that rooftop unit panels are closed and that condensate lines discharge where they should. Reseal open coping joints before the April and May thunderstorms arrive.

02

After every major hail or wind event

Walk the roof or have us walk it. Look for torn flashing at parapets, displaced edge metal, punctures around rooftop units and dents in metal components that suggest hail size. Photograph everything with a date stamp.

03

Late summer

Long stretches of heat shrink sealants and stress seams. A late-summer check catches open laps and cracked pitch pan fill before fall rain.

04

Fall, after leaves drop

Trees near low-slope buildings can fill a drain in a single afternoon. A fall cleaning keeps water moving before freeze and thaw cycles begin, which is when ponding water does the most damage.

Commercial Inspections & Maintenance FAQ

When do I need commercial roof inspection?
You may need commercial roof inspection when you see leaks, missing materials, stains, loose flashing, storm damage, or repeated problems in the same roof area. A roof inspection helps separate a small repair from a larger issue before water reaches decking, insulation, or interior finishes.
Can commercial roof inspection be repaired instead of replaced?
Sometimes, yes. A newer roof with isolated damage may only need a focused repair. An older roof with widespread wear, repeated leaks, soft decking, or storm damage across several slopes may need replacement planning instead. The inspection should answer that question before you spend money.
Does Wichita weather affect commercial roof inspection?
Yes. Wichita roofs deal with hail, strong wind, sudden rain, heat, and fast temperature changes. Those conditions can lift shingles, stress flashing, loosen gutters, and open small leak paths. That is why we check the full area around the visible problem.
What other roof work might be connected?
This depends on what we find. Some commercial roof inspection calls connect to commercial roofing, TPO roofing, modified bitumen roofing. Others connect to gutters, siding, flashing, or ventilation. We keep those recommendations tied to the actual roof condition, not a pre-set package.

Call (316) 742-1900 to schedule an inspection with Wichita Roofing Pros. Tell us what you are seeing, when it started, and whether a recent storm came through. Those details help us look in the right places first.

What is the difference between an inspection and maintenance?
An inspection documents the roof's condition. Maintenance is the follow-up work, such as clearing drains, resealing penetrations and repairing small issues.
