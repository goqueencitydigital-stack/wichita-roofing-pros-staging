Common Problems

What goes wrong on flat and low-slope roofs?

Flat roofs are built to hold up under standing water for short periods, but they have weak points that need attention.

Ponding water

Water that stays in low spots long after a rain stresses the membrane, collects dirt and adds weight to the deck. NRCA defines ponding as water that remains more than 48 hours after rain under drying conditions.

Open seams

Seams join the sheets of membrane together. Heat, movement and age can open a seam, and an open seam lets water travel under the membrane.

Failing flashing

Flashing at walls, curbs, parapets and edges carries much of the load on a flat roof. Loose or cracked flashing is one of the most common leak sources.

Blisters and wrinkles

Trapped moisture or air under the membrane can form blisters. Wrinkles can open into cracks over time.

Punctures

Foot traffic, dropped tools, HVAC service work and hail can puncture the membrane.

Clogged drains and scuppers

Leaves, debris and silt can block drains and scuppers, which turns a normal rain into a ponding problem.

Finding the Leak

Why are flat roof leaks hard to find?

On a flat roof, the stain inside is often nowhere near the opening above.

  1. 01

    Water travels under the membrane

    Once water gets through a seam or puncture, it can run along the deck or through insulation before it finds a joint to drip from. The leak inside may be far from the actual entry point.

  2. 02

    Insulation hides moisture

    Many flat roofs have insulation boards under the membrane. Wet insulation can hold water for a long time and spread it across a wide area.

  3. 03

    Penetrations are common suspects

    HVAC units, pipes, vents, skylights and roof hatches all pass through the membrane. Each one has flashing that can fail.

  4. 04

    We work outward from the symptom

    We start with the interior stain, check the roof above it, then look at nearby seams, penetrations, drains and edges. We photograph what we find and explain how water is likely getting in.

Systems We Repair

Which flat roof systems do you repair?

We repair the low-slope systems we install, and we tell you plainly when a roof is outside what we service.

TPO

Thermoplastic polyolefin is a white single-ply membrane with heat-welded seams. TPO repairs usually involve cleaning the area and welding new membrane over the damage. See TPO roofing.

Modified bitumen

Modified bitumen is a multi-layer asphalt-based system, often with a granule surface. Repairs patch the damaged area with compatible material and seal the laps. See modified bitumen.

Low-slope sections on homes

Many Wichita houses have a flat or low-slope section over a porch, addition or garage. These small roofs leak for the same reasons as commercial roofs and need the same care.

Other systems

If your roof uses a membrane we do not work with, we will tell you during the inspection rather than guess at a repair.

Repair, Restore or Replace

Should you repair, restore or replace a flat roof?

The answer depends on how much of the roof is affected and what is happening under the membrane.

Localized damage

Repair

Best when damage is limited to specific spots, such as a punctured area, a few open seams or a failed flashing, and the rest of the roof is sound.

Sound roof, aging surface

Restore

When the membrane is aging but the insulation and deck are dry, a broader restoration of seams, flashings and problem areas may extend the life of the roof. We explain what is realistic for your system.

Widespread failure

Replace

When large areas are wet, the membrane has failed in many places, or the roof has reached the end of its life, replacement is usually the better long-term choice. See commercial roofing.

Drainage

How does drainage affect a flat roof?

Flat roofs are not truly flat. They need slope to move water to drains, scuppers or gutters.

  1. 01

    Building codes require slope

    Codes generally call for a minimum slope of one-quarter inch per foot for most membrane roofs, according to NRCA’s guidance on slope and drainage.

  2. 02

    Low spots grow over time

    Deck deflection, crushed insulation and settling can create low spots where water collects. Each time water sits there, it adds weight and wear.

  3. 03

    Drains need to stay open

    Clogged drains and scuppers are one of the easiest flat roof problems to prevent. Keeping them clear is a basic maintenance step.

  4. 04

    Tapered insulation can help

    During repairs or replacement, tapered insulation or crickets can be added to steer water toward drains and away from walls and curbs.

Kansas Weather

How does Kansas weather affect flat roofs?

Our climate is hard on low-slope roofs in several ways.

Hail

Large hail can puncture or bruise membranes, crack flashing and damage rooftop equipment. Check the roof after major hail.

Wind

High winds pull on roof edges and corners. Loose edge metal can let wind get under the membrane.

Sun and heat

Strong summer sun ages membranes and sealants. Reflective white roofs reduce heat on the surface.

Freeze and thaw

Water trapped in seams or cracks expands when it freezes and widens the opening.

Commercial Buildings

What should building owners know about flat roof repairs?

Commercial roof repairs need to work around tenants, equipment and business hours.

  1. 01

    Plan for access

    Our crews need safe access to the roof. Let us know about roof hatches, ladders, locked areas and tenant schedules.

  2. 02

    Coordinate with HVAC service

    Rooftop equipment service is a common cause of punctures. We can point out areas where walkway pads would protect the membrane.

  3. 03

    Keep a roof record

    A record of repairs, photos and inspection reports helps with budgeting, insurance and warranty questions.

  4. 04

    Schedule regular inspections

    Spring and fall inspections, plus checks after big storms, catch problems early. See commercial inspections and maintenance.

  5. 05

    Ask about the warranty

    Some membrane manufacturers require specific repair methods to keep a warranty in place. Bring your warranty paperwork to the inspection.

Rooftop Equipment

How do rooftop units and penetrations cause leaks?

Every pipe, curb and unit that passes through a flat roof is a place where the membrane has to be sealed around something.

HVAC curbs

Curbs that hold rooftop heating and cooling units need flashing that runs up the sides and is sealed at the top. Vibration and service traffic loosen it over time.

Pipe penetrations

Gas lines, plumbing vents and conduit need boots or flashing sleeves. Sealant around them dries out in the sun.

Pitch pans

Older roofs sometimes use pitch pans filled with sealant around odd-shaped penetrations. The filler shrinks and needs topping off or replacement.

Skylights and hatches

Curbed skylights and roof hatches need sound flashing on all four sides and a working seal on the lid.

Condensate lines

Air conditioning condensate that drains onto the roof can wear the membrane and collect algae. It should be routed to a drain.

Satellite and sign mounts

Mounts added after the roof was installed are a frequent source of leaks when they were fastened through the membrane without proper flashing.

Edges and Walls

Why do parapet walls and roof edges need special attention?

The edges of a flat roof take the most wind and often the most water.

  1. 01

    Parapet walls

    Many commercial buildings have short walls around the roof. The membrane turns up the inside of the wall and is capped or counter-flashed at the top. Cracked coping, open joints and failed counter flashing let water behind the membrane.

  2. 02

    Edge metal

    On roofs without parapets, edge metal holds the membrane at the perimeter. Loose or bent edge metal gives wind a place to grab, and once wind gets under the membrane at the edge, it can peel back a large area.

  3. 03

    Scuppers and gutters

    Water leaving the roof through scuppers or edge gutters passes through openings in the wall or edge. Those openings need sealed, well-fastened flashing to stay watertight.

  4. 04

    Masonry above the roof

    Brick or block walls above a low-slope roof can let water in through cracked mortar. That water may show up as a roof leak even though the roof itself is sound.

Moisture Under the Membrane

What happens when insulation under a flat roof gets wet?

Wet insulation changes the repair conversation, so we look for it before recommending a fix.

  1. 01

    Wet insulation loses performance

    Soaked insulation boards lose much of their insulating value and add weight to the deck.

  2. 02

    It can spread the leak

    Water moving through insulation can show up across a wide area of ceiling, even from a single small opening.

  3. 03

    It affects repair choices

    A patch over a dry area can work well. A patch over wet insulation traps the moisture and often fails. Wet sections usually need to be cut out and replaced with dry material.

  4. 04

    Soft spots are a clue

    Areas that feel soft or spongy underfoot, along with blisters, can mean water has reached the insulation. We note these during the inspection and explain what they mean for your roof.

More Questions About Flat Roof Repair

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

Is some standing water normal on a flat roof?
Small puddles that dry within about two days are generally considered normal. Water that stays longer points to a drainage problem worth looking at.
Can a flat roof leak be patched?
Often, yes. Many leaks come from a single seam, puncture or flashing. If the area around the leak is wet or the membrane is failing broadly, a patch may not last.
How do you find a leak on a flat roof?
We start at the interior symptom and work outward on the roof, checking seams, penetrations, flashing, drains and edges above and around the leak.
Do you repair flat roofs on homes?
Yes. Many homes have low-slope sections over porches, garages or additions. We repair those as well.
Can you repair a roof another company installed?
Yes, as long as it is a system we work with. If repairs could affect a manufacturer warranty, bring your paperwork so we can review it.
How long does a flat roof last?
It depends on the system, installation, drainage and maintenance. During the inspection, we give you an honest assessment of how much life your roof has left.
Should I go on the roof to look for the leak?
Only if you have safe, permitted access and the roof is dry. Avoid walking on blisters or soft spots, and leave repairs to trained crews.
Can hail damage a flat roof?
Yes. Large hail can puncture or bruise membranes and damage flashing and equipment. After a major storm, an inspection helps document any damage.
Do you repair roofs over occupied businesses?
Yes. We schedule work to limit disruption and let you know when crews will be on the roof and where they will need access.
What is a roof walkway pad?
Walkway pads are durable mats placed on the membrane along paths that service technicians use, protecting the roof from foot traffic and dropped tools.
Can a flat roof be coated instead of replaced?
Coatings can help some roofs with a sound, dry membrane and insulation. They are not a fix for wet insulation or widespread failure. We tell you honestly whether it fits your roof.
What should I do if my flat roof is leaking during a storm?
Protect inventory and equipment below, catch the water, take photos and call (316) 742-1900. We will temporarily seal the area when it is safe and plan the permanent repair.
