Overview

Wichita Roofing Pros checks membrane condition, seams, drains, scuppers, flashing, penetrations, edge metal, and roof access constraints before recommending work. Around Wichita, hail, high wind, sudden downpours, and hot summers can all shorten the life of roofing materials. We explain what we see, show you the areas that matter, and help you choose the next step without turning one roof concern into a bigger project than it needs to be.

Quick answerCommercial roofing covers low-slope and flat roofs on offices, retail, warehouses and multifamily buildings. Wichita Roofing Pros installs and repairs TPO and modified bitumen systems and provides commercial roof inspections and maintenance. Every recommendation starts with the roof surface, seams, drains and rooftop equipment.

Symptoms

What signs mean you should call?

These are the signs homeowners most often notice first. Any one of them is worth a free inspection.

  • Ponding water 48 hours after rain
  • Open or lifted seams
  • Blisters, cracks or punctures
  • Leaks at curbs, drains or equipment
  • Wet insulation under the membrane
Evaluation

How do we evaluate the problem?

Every inspection follows the same order so the recommendation is based on the whole roof, not the first symptom.

  1. Start with your concern. We inspect the spot that made you call.
  2. Inspect outward. Surrounding materials, flashing, valleys, vents, drainage and edges.
  3. Document. Photos when access is safe.
  4. Explain options. What needs attention now, what can be monitored, and why.
  5. Write it down. Scope and price in writing before work starts.

Commercial Roof Systems

Which commercial roof systems do Wichita buildings use?

Most commercial buildings in Wichita have flat or low-slope roofs. The membrane on top decides how the roof is repaired, maintained and eventually replaced.

01

TPO single-ply membrane

Thermoplastic polyolefin, or TPO, is a white or light-colored single-ply sheet that is mechanically fastened or fully adhered over insulation. Seams are joined with hot air, which fuses the sheets into one continuous surface.

TPO is common on warehouses, retail centers and newer offices across Wichita because the reflective surface helps with summer heat. See our TPO roofing page for details.

02

Modified bitumen

Modified bitumen is an asphalt-based roll roofing reinforced with polyester or fiberglass and modified with rubber or plastic polymers. It is installed in layers, often with a granulated cap sheet on top.

It holds up well to foot traffic and is common on older strip centers, churches and small commercial buildings. See modified bitumen roofing.

03

Built-up roofs on older buildings

Many older downtown and industrial buildings in Wichita still have built-up roofs, made of alternating layers of asphalt and felt with gravel on top. These roofs can last for decades but become hard to repair as they age.

When a built-up roof reaches the end of its life, we discuss whether recovering or tearing off makes sense based on the condition of the insulation and deck.

04

Metal and steep-slope sections

Some commercial buildings combine a low-slope main roof with metal or shingle sections over entries, offices or canopies. Those transitions are common leak points.

We inspect every section and the flashing that ties them together, not only the main field of the roof.

Commercial Leaks

Where do commercial roofs in Wichita usually leak?

Flat roofs rarely fail in the middle of the field. Most leaks start at details.

01

Drains, scuppers and gutters

Clogged drains and scuppers let water pond on the roof. Ponding water adds weight, speeds membrane aging and finds any weak seam. We clear and inspect drains on every visit.

02

Rooftop units and curbs

HVAC units sit on curbs that are flashed into the membrane. Service technicians walking to and from units can puncture the membrane or damage flashing. We check every curb and the paths around them.

03

Parapet walls and copings

Parapet walls and their metal caps take wind and water from every direction. Open coping joints and failed wall flashing let water run down behind the membrane.

04

Penetrations and pitch pans

Pipes, conduits and supports that pass through the roof need sealed boots or pitch pans. Sealant shrinks and cracks over time, especially in Kansas heat.

05

Seams and laps

Single-ply and modified bitumen roofs have seams that can separate under stress, heat or poor installation. We probe seams on every inspection.

06

Hail on the membrane

Large hail can bruise or puncture membranes and crack insulation boards below. Damage is not always visible, so we document hail impacts and test suspect areas.

Commercial Planning

How should building owners plan for commercial roof work?

A commercial roof is a major asset. Good planning keeps repairs predictable and avoids emergency replacements.

01

Start with a condition baseline

An inspection with photos, a roof plan and a list of issues gives you a starting point. It also documents the condition of the roof before the next storm, which helps if you need to file a claim.

02

Repair, restore or replace

Not every aging roof needs a full replacement. Some can be repaired and maintained for years, while others have wet insulation or deck damage that makes replacement the better investment. We explain the tradeoffs in writing.

03

Budget over several years

Owners and property managers often plan roof work over three to five years. Knowing which sections need attention first helps spread costs and avoid surprises.

04

Protect tenants and operations

Commercial work has to fit around business hours, deliveries and tenants. We plan staging, crane lifts and noisy work around your schedule.

Commercial Storm Claims

How do commercial hail claims work in Wichita?

Commercial claims follow the same basic steps as residential claims, but the roofs, policies and stakes are larger.

Document quickly. After a major storm, schedule an inspection as soon as it is safe. Fresh hail impacts on membranes, metal edge, rooftop units and skylights are easier to identify and photograph.

Look beyond the membrane. Hail can dent condenser fins, crack skylights, damage exhaust fans and crush insulation under the membrane. A full inspection checks all of it.

Know your policy. Commercial policies may have percentage deductibles, cosmetic damage exclusions or roof age schedules. Talk with your agent so you understand what is covered.

Meet the adjuster on the roof. We can walk the roof with your adjuster and point out the damage we documented. That keeps the conversation focused on facts.

Plan the work around operations. If a claim leads to replacement, we schedule the project around tenants, deliveries and business hours.

Energy and Comfort

Can a new commercial roof lower energy costs?

The roof is one of the largest surfaces on a commercial building, and it has a real effect on interior temperatures.

01

Reflective membranes

White and light-colored membranes reflect sunlight and stay cooler than dark roofs. On buildings with large roof areas and air-conditioned space below, that can reduce cooling loads. The ENERGY STAR cool roofs page explains how reflectivity is measured.

02

Insulation upgrades

A replacement is the best time to add insulation, since the old roof is already off. More insulation reduces heat gain in summer and heat loss in winter.

03

Tapered insulation for drainage

Tapered insulation creates slope toward drains, which reduces ponding and keeps the membrane drier.

04

Daylighting and skylights

Skylights can bring in daylight, but they need proper curbs and flashing. We check them on every inspection.

Comparing Bids

How should you compare commercial roofing bids?

Commercial bids can look very different from one another. A few questions make them easier to compare.

01

Same system, same thickness?

Check that each bid specifies the membrane type, thickness, insulation and attachment method. A lower price sometimes reflects a thinner membrane or less insulation.

02

Tear-off or recover?

Make sure each contractor is proposing the same approach and has checked the existing roof for moisture.

03

What happens at the edges?

Edge metal, copings, curbs and drains should be described clearly in every bid.

04

Who handles permits and coordination?

Ask who pulls the permit and who coordinates with HVAC contractors when units must be lifted.

Commercial Roofing FAQ

When do I need commercial roofing?
You may need commercial roofing when you see leaks, missing materials, stains, loose flashing, storm damage, or repeated problems in the same roof area. A roof inspection helps separate a small repair from a larger issue before water reaches decking, insulation, or interior finishes.
Can commercial roofing be repaired instead of replaced?
Sometimes, yes. A newer roof with isolated damage may only need a focused repair. An older roof with widespread wear, repeated leaks, soft decking, or storm damage across several slopes may need replacement planning instead. The inspection should answer that question before you spend money.
Does Wichita weather affect commercial roofing?
Yes. Wichita roofs deal with hail, strong wind, sudden rain, heat, and fast temperature changes. Those conditions can lift shingles, stress flashing, loosen gutters, and open small leak paths. That is why we check the full area around the visible problem.
What other roof work might be connected?
This depends on what we find. Some commercial roofing calls connect to TPO roofing, modified bitumen roofing, commercial inspections. Others connect to gutters, siding, flashing, or ventilation. We keep those recommendations tied to the actual roof condition, not a pre-set package.
