Overview

If you’re looking at TPO roofing in Wichita, the first question is usually simple: can the roof be repaired safely, or is the problem part of a bigger pattern? Wichita Roofing Pros checks welded seams, membrane field, drains, flashing, edge metal, roof curbs, and any areas where foot traffic has worn the surface before recommending work. Around Wichita, hail, high wind, sudden downpours, and hot summers can all shorten the life of roofing materials. We explain what we see, show you the areas that matter, and help you choose the next step without turning one roof concern into a bigger project than it needs to be.

Quick answerTPO is a single-ply white membrane with heat-welded seams, widely used on low-slope commercial roofs. It reflects sunlight, resists ponding when seams are welded correctly, and is repaired by welding new membrane over clean, prepared areas. NRCA recommends TPO membranes of at least 60 mils.

These are the signs homeowners most often notice first. Any one of them is worth a free inspection.

  • Leaks at curbs and penetrations

How TPO Works

How is a TPO roof built?

A TPO roof is more than the white sheet you see on top. It is an assembly of layers.

01

Deck

The structural deck is usually steel on commercial buildings, though wood and concrete decks are also common. The deck has to be sound and dry before anything goes on top.

02

Insulation

Rigid insulation boards, often polyiso, go over the deck. Tapered insulation can be used to create slope toward drains on roofs that are too flat.

03

Cover board

Some systems add a cover board over the insulation for extra durability and hail resistance.

04

TPO membrane

The membrane is mechanically fastened through the insulation into the deck or fully adhered with bonding adhesive. Seams are heat-welded into a single surface, as described in the NRCA TPO guideline.

Why TPO

Why do Wichita building owners choose TPO?

TPO has become one of the most popular low-slope roof systems in the country.

01

Reflective surface

White TPO reflects much of the sun's energy, which can reduce roof temperatures and cooling loads during Kansas summers. The Department of Energy cool roof guide explains how reflective roofs work.

02

Heat-welded seams

Hot-air welded seams create a continuous membrane that is often stronger than the sheet itself when done correctly.

03

Lightweight

TPO adds little weight compared to built-up or ballasted roofs, which helps on older buildings.

04

Cost-effective for large roofs

Wide sheets and efficient installation make TPO a cost-effective choice for warehouses and large retail buildings.

TPO Problems

What problems should owners watch for on a TPO roof?

TPO is durable, but it is not maintenance-free.

01

Seam failures

Poorly welded seams can separate over time. We probe seams with a tool on every inspection.

02

Punctures from foot traffic

Technicians servicing rooftop units can puncture the membrane with tools or debris. Walkway pads help.

03

Flashing at curbs and walls

Membrane flashing at curbs, walls and penetrations takes extra stress and can pull away.

04

Ponding water

Low areas that hold water can stress seams and collect dirt that reduces reflectivity.

05

Hail damage

Large hail can fracture TPO or crush the insulation beneath it, especially on older roofs.

06

Shrinkage and fastener issues

Older membranes can shrink or show fastener back-out, pulling at edges and flashings.

Replacement or Recover

Should an old roof be torn off or recovered with TPO?

Building codes and roof conditions decide whether a recover is possible.

Recover means installing new insulation and TPO over an existing roof. It can save tear-off cost and disposal, but it only works when the existing roof is dry, the deck is sound and codes allow another layer.

Tear-off removes the existing roof down to the deck. It is required when there are already multiple roofs, when insulation is wet or when the deck needs repair. Tear-off also gives the chance to add tapered insulation and correct drainage.

We check moisture and deck condition before recommending one path or the other, and we explain the cost and long-term tradeoffs in writing.

TPO Maintenance

How do you keep a TPO roof in good shape?

Simple maintenance goes a long way.

01

Inspect twice a year

Spring and fall inspections catch seam, flashing and drain issues early.

02

Keep it clean

Clean membranes reflect more heat. Clearing debris also prevents punctures and clogged drains.

03

Repair promptly

TPO patches must be welded with compatible material. Prompt repairs keep water out of the insulation.

04

Document after storms

A quick inspection after hail or high wind documents any new damage while it is fresh.

TPO Thickness

Does TPO thickness matter?

TPO membranes are sold in several thicknesses, and the choice affects durability.

TPO is commonly available in thicknesses measured in mils, or thousandths of an inch. Thicker membranes have more material above the reinforcing scrim, which can add durability and resistance to punctures and weathering.

Thinner membranes cost less up front and can work well on roofs with little traffic and good protection. Thicker membranes are often worth considering on roofs with heavy foot traffic, frequent hail exposure or long expected service life.

We discuss membrane thickness, insulation and attachment method together in the proposal, because they work as a system.

Attachment Methods

How is a TPO roof attached?

There are two main ways to hold a TPO membrane in place.

01

Mechanically fastened

The membrane is fastened through the insulation into the deck with plates and screws along the seams. This is common and cost-effective on steel decks.

02

Fully adhered

The membrane is glued to the insulation or cover board with bonding adhesive. Fully adhered roofs have no fastener rows under the seams and can resist wind flutter.

03

Induction welded

Some systems use plates bonded to the underside of the membrane with induction heat, combining fasteners with a smooth surface.

04

Choosing a method

Deck type, wind exposure, building height and budget all play a role. We recommend a method based on your building.

TPO Details

Which TPO details matter most?

The details at the edges decide how well a TPO roof performs.

01

Perimeter edge metal

Edge metal holds the membrane at the roof perimeter and resists wind uplift. It must be properly fastened and sealed.

02

Curb flashing

Rooftop unit curbs need membrane flashing that runs up the curb and is terminated correctly.

03

Drain sumps

Tapered insulation around drains helps water flow off the roof quickly.

04

Walkway pads

Welded walkway pads protect high-traffic paths around equipment.

Kansas Conditions

How does TPO perform in Wichita weather?

TPO's strengths and weaknesses both show up in Kansas.

Summer heat. TPO's reflective surface is its biggest advantage in Wichita. A white membrane stays much cooler than a dark roof in direct sun, which reduces heat flowing into the building and slows the aging of the roof assembly.

Hail. Hail resistance depends on membrane thickness, the insulation and cover board below and the age of the sheet. A cover board under the membrane can add protection. Older TPO tends to become more vulnerable to hail fractures.

Wind. Mechanically fastened TPO can flutter in high wind, which stresses fasteners and seams. Fully adhered systems and proper perimeter securement reduce that risk.

Dirt and reflectivity. Dust and debris dull the surface over time. Periodic cleaning restores reflectivity.

With good installation and regular inspections, TPO is a strong fit for many Wichita commercial buildings.

TPO Ownership

How do you protect a TPO roof after it is installed?

TPO is durable, but it is a single layer. The habits of everyone who goes on the roof make a real difference.

01

Control rooftop traffic

Walkway pads to each rooftop unit give service technicians a clear path and keep dropped tools and boots off the open membrane. Ask contractors to check in before going on the roof.

02

Keep sharp debris off the roof

Screws, sheet metal scraps and broken fan blades left behind after HVAC service are a common cause of punctures. A quick walk after any rooftop work is worth the time.

03

Watch the perimeter

Edge metal and termination bars hold the membrane down at the roof edge. Loose edge metal after a windstorm should be repaired quickly, before the next gust can peel the membrane back.

04

Keep records

Save the installation documentation, any manufacturer warranty paperwork and every inspection report. Those records help when a claim, sale or refinance comes up.

TPO Roofing FAQ

When do I need TPO roofing?
You may need TPO roofing when you see leaks, missing materials, stains, loose flashing, storm damage, or repeated problems in the same roof area. A roof inspection helps separate a small repair from a larger issue before water reaches decking, insulation, or interior finishes.
Can TPO roofing be repaired instead of replaced?
Sometimes, yes. A newer roof with isolated damage may only need a focused repair. An older roof with widespread wear, repeated leaks, soft decking, or storm damage across several slopes may need replacement planning instead. The inspection should answer that question before you spend money.
Does Wichita weather affect TPO roofing?
Yes. Wichita roofs deal with hail, strong wind, sudden rain, heat, and fast temperature changes. Those conditions can lift shingles, stress flashing, loosen gutters, and open small leak paths. That is why we check the full area around the visible problem.
What other roof work might be connected?
This depends on what we find. Some TPO roofing calls connect to commercial roofing, commercial inspections, modified bitumen roofing. Others connect to gutters, siding, flashing, or ventilation. We keep those recommendations tied to the actual roof condition, not a pre-set package.

Is TPO good for Wichita weather?
TPO handles heat well because of its reflective surface. Correct seams, drainage and maintenance matter more than the brand.
Can TPO be repaired?
Yes, by cleaning the membrane and heat-welding a compatible patch.
Is TPO good for Wichita's climate?
TPO's reflective surface helps in hot Kansas summers, and properly welded seams resist leaks. Like any membrane, it needs regular inspections, especially after hail.
Can TPO be installed over my existing roof?
Sometimes. A recover requires a dry existing roof, a sound deck and code allowance for another layer. We check before recommending it.
How are TPO seams sealed?
TPO seams are heat-welded with hot air, fusing the sheets into a continuous surface.
Can a TPO roof be repaired?
Yes. Clean, prepared TPO can be patched with compatible membrane welded in place. Older or dirty membranes need careful preparation for the weld to hold.
What thickness of TPO do you recommend?
It depends on the building, foot traffic, hail exposure and budget. We explain the options in your proposal so you can choose with the tradeoffs in mind.
Is TPO a good choice for a building with a lot of rooftop equipment?
It can be. The key is careful detailing at every curb and penetration, plus walkway pads where technicians travel. We plan the layout around your equipment during the proposal.
