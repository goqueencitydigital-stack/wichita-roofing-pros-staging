What Siding Does

What is siding really protecting on your home?

Siding is the part of your home people see first, but its main job is keeping water and wind out of the walls.

01

The first layer against rain

Siding sheds most of the rain that hits a wall. Behind it, a weather-resistant barrier and flashing handle the water that gets past. When siding cracks, loosens or is installed without the right clearances, more water reaches that second layer than it was designed for.

02

The wall framing and sheathing

Water that gets behind siding for months at a time can soften sheathing, stain interior drywall and lead to mold inside the wall cavity. Most of that damage stays hidden until a panel is removed.

03

Windows and doors

Openings are where most wall leaks start. Siding, trim and flashing around windows and doors work together to direct water out and away. A gap at a window head can let water run down inside the wall for years.

04

The roof-to-wall connection

Where a lower roof meets a second-story wall, the siding has to sit above step flashing and kick-out flashing so water goes into the gutter instead of behind the wall. This is one of the most common hidden leak points we find, and it is why a roofing company is a good fit for siding work.

Siding Options

Which siding options do we install in Wichita?

We install vinyl siding, LP SmartSide engineered wood and the soffit, fascia and trim that finish both. Each has a different look and maintenance profile.

01

Vinyl siding

Vinyl is the most budget-friendly option and needs no painting. Modern profiles come in many colors and textures. It is installed so each panel can expand and contract with temperature changes. See vinyl siding.

02

LP SmartSide engineered wood

LP SmartSide looks like painted wood siding and holds up well to impact. It is treated during manufacturing to resist fungal decay and termites, and it is painted to match any color. See LP SmartSide siding.

03

Soffit, fascia and trim

Soffit, fascia and trim tie the siding to the roofline, ventilate the attic and frame the windows and corners. Rotted fascia and damaged soffit are some of the most common repairs we make. See soffit, fascia and trim.

Repair or Replace

Should you repair siding or replace it?

Many siding problems can be repaired. Replacement makes sense when damage is widespread or the material is near the end of its life.

Repair is often enough when:

  • Damage is limited to a few panels from hail, a mower or a ball
  • You can match the existing profile and color closely
  • The wall behind the damaged panels is dry and solid

Replacement is usually the better value when:

  • Hail cracked or holed siding on several sides of the house
  • Panels are warped, faded or brittle across large areas
  • Water has damaged sheathing in several places
  • You want to change the look of the home or move from vinyl to engineered wood

When we recommend repair, we say how closely new pieces will match. Older vinyl fades, so a new panel can look slightly different from those around it. We explain that before any work begins.

Storm Damage

How does hail affect siding in south-central Kansas?

Hail that damages a roof often damages siding on the same side of the house.

01

Cracks and holes in vinyl

Vinyl becomes more brittle in cold weather and as it ages. Large hail can crack or punch holes through panels, especially on the side of the house facing the storm.

02

Dents and chips in engineered wood

Engineered wood resists impact better than vinyl, but large hail can still dent or chip the surface and crack the paint. Damaged paint should be touched up so the substrate stays protected.

03

Window wraps and trim

Aluminum window wraps and trim coil dent easily. Those dents are often the clearest evidence of hail size on the walls.

04

Documenting for a claim

We photograph each elevation of the house along with the roof so your insurer sees the full picture. Coverage depends on your policy. See storm and hail damage.

What does a siding project with us look like?

Siding work follows the same plain-language process as our roofing work.

Inspection. We walk every side of the home, check panels, trim, soffit and fascia, and look at the roof-to-wall transitions. We note any soft areas, stains or gaps that point to water behind the siding.

Comparing Materials

How do vinyl and LP SmartSide compare side by side?

Here is a quick comparison of the two siding options we install.

01

Up-front cost

Vinyl usually has the lower installed cost. LP SmartSide costs more for material and painting.

02

Appearance

Vinyl offers many factory colors and profiles. SmartSide looks like painted wood and can be painted any color.

03

Impact resistance

SmartSide resists cracking from impact better than vinyl, which can crack in large hail or cold weather.

04

Maintenance

Vinyl needs washing but no paint. SmartSide needs periodic repainting and caulk checks.

05

Warranty

Vinyl warranties vary by manufacturer. LP backs SmartSide with a 5/50-year limited warranty, subject to its terms.

06

Repairs

Vinyl repairs can show color differences on faded walls. SmartSide repairs can be repainted to match.

Hidden Water

How can you tell if water is getting behind your siding?

Water behind siding often shows up indoors or at the edges before it shows on the siding face.

Inside the home. Look for stains or bubbling paint on interior walls below windows, near corners and under roof-to-wall intersections. Musty smells in a room can also point to wet walls.

At the base of walls. Swollen or soft siding near the ground, especially below a downspout or a roof edge that ends against a wall, often means water is running down behind the siding.

Around windows. Gaps in caulk, cracked trim and stains under window sills are common signs.

Below roof-to-wall transitions. Where a lower roof meets a second-story wall, missing kick-out flashing sends water down the wall. Rot or staining below that corner is a classic sign.

If you notice any of these, a free inspection can find the source.

Siding and Roof Together

Why should siding and roofing be planned together?

Siding and roofing meet at several points on every house. Planning them together avoids gaps.

Step and kick-out flashing. Where a roof meets a wall, the flashing goes under the siding. Replacing siding later can mean disturbing a new roof edge, and replacing a roof later can mean pulling siding.

Fascia and soffit. The roof edge, fascia and soffit are shared by both systems. Repairing them once is cheaper than repairing them twice.

Storm claims. Hail often damages roofs and siding in the same storm. Documenting both at once gives your insurer the complete picture.

One schedule. Coordinating crews and materials under one company keeps the project simpler for you.

Siding & Trim FAQ

When do I need siding and trim?
You may need siding and trim when you see leaks, missing materials, stains, loose flashing, storm damage, or repeated problems in the same roof area. A roof inspection helps separate a small repair from a larger issue before water reaches decking, insulation, or interior finishes.
Can siding and trim be repaired instead of replaced?
Sometimes, yes. A newer roof with isolated damage may only need a focused repair. An older roof with widespread wear, repeated leaks, soft decking, or storm damage across several slopes may need replacement planning instead. The inspection should answer that question before you spend money.
Does Wichita weather affect siding and trim?
Yes. Wichita roofs deal with hail, strong wind, sudden rain, heat, and fast temperature changes. Those conditions can lift shingles, stress flashing, loosen gutters, and open small leak paths. That is why we check the full area around the visible problem.
What other roof work might be connected?
This depends on what we find. Some siding and trim calls connect to vinyl siding, LP SmartSide siding, soffit and fascia. Others connect to gutters, siding, flashing, or ventilation. We keep those recommendations tied to the actual roof condition, not a pre-set package.

More Questions About Siding & Trim

Can you replace siding and roof together?
Yes. Coordinating both lets flashing and roof-to-wall transitions be installed correctly in one plan.
Does hail damage siding?
Yes. Hail can crack vinyl and dent or puncture other products; document it with your roof damage.
Can you replace siding on just one side of the house?
Yes, if the remaining sides are in good condition and the new material can match closely. We will tell you if a color or profile match will be noticeable.
Is siding included in a hail claim?
Siding damage from hail is often included with roof damage in the same claim. Coverage depends on your policy and the adjuster’s findings. We document damage on every elevation.
What is the difference between soffit and fascia?
Fascia is the vertical board along the roof edge where gutters attach. Soffit is the panel under the roof overhang, often vented to let air into the attic.
Can siding and roof replacement be done at the same time?
Yes. Doing both together lets us coordinate flashing at roof-to-wall transitions and gives the home a consistent finished look.
Do you offer financing for siding?
Wichita Roofing Pros offers financing through Wisetack for qualifying projects. *All financing is subject to credit approval.
Do you repair siding after a storm even if the roof is fine?
Yes. We repair and replace siding whether or not the roof needs work.
How long does siding replacement take?
It depends on the size of the home, the material and any sheathing repairs. We give you the expected timeline before work begins.
