About Concrete Tile

What should Wichita homeowners know about concrete tile roofs?

Concrete tile is less common in Kansas than in the Southwest, but it appears on a number of Wichita-area homes, especially custom builds and Mediterranean or Spanish-style designs.

01

A heavy, durable material

Concrete tiles are made from cement, sand and water, molded into flat, low-profile or high-profile shapes. They are much heavier than asphalt shingles, so the roof framing must be designed to carry the weight.

That weight is one reason concrete tile is usually chosen during new construction rather than added to an existing home.

02

The tile is not the waterproof layer

On a tile roof, the underlayment below the tiles does most of the waterproofing. The tiles shed most of the water and protect the underlayment from sun and impact.

When a tile roof leaks, the cause is often aged or damaged underlayment, flashing or fasteners, not just a broken tile.

03

Color and finish

Concrete tiles come in many colors and textures. Some are colored through the body, while others have a surface coating that can fade over time.

04

Hail and concrete tile

Large hail can crack or chip concrete tiles. Damage is not always obvious from the ground, and walking a tile roof incorrectly can break more tiles. Inspection should be done carefully by someone who knows how to step on tile.

Tile Repairs

What repairs do concrete tile roofs need?

Most tile roof repairs fall into a few categories.

01

Cracked or broken tiles

Individual tiles can be removed and replaced. Matching color and profile is the main challenge on older roofs, since some tile lines are discontinued.

02

Slipped tiles

Tiles can slide out of position when fasteners fail or wind lifts them. Slipped tiles expose the underlayment to sun and water.

03

Ridge and hip issues

Ridge and hip tiles are set with mortar or mechanical systems. Cracked mortar or loose ridge tiles let water in at the top of the roof.

04

Underlayment replacement

When underlayment reaches the end of its life, tiles can often be carefully removed, the underlayment replaced and the tiles reinstalled.

05

Flashing at walls and valleys

Valleys, walls and chimneys need metal flashing that works with the tile profile. Failed or corroded flashing is a common leak source.

06

Penetrations

Pipe vents and other penetrations need lead or flexible flashing shaped to the tile.

Considering Tile

Is concrete tile the right choice for your Wichita home?

Concrete tile has real strengths and real tradeoffs.

01

Strengths

Concrete tile is long-lasting, fire-resistant and offers a distinctive look that suits Mediterranean, Spanish and some contemporary designs.

02

Weight

The structure must be able to carry the tile. Switching from shingles to tile usually requires an engineer's review and sometimes framing upgrades.

03

Hail exposure

Wichita sees frequent hail, and tile can crack under large stones. Some homeowners weigh that risk against the look and longevity of tile.

04

Alternatives

Synthetic slate and shake or metal roofing can offer a similar premium look with less weight. See synthetic slate and shake and metal roofing.

Our Approach

How do we inspect and work on concrete tile?

Tile roofs require care at every step.

Careful inspection. We walk tile roofs only where it is safe to step and use the right techniques to avoid breaking tiles. Where walking is not safe, we inspect from ladders and other vantage points.

Photo documentation. We photograph cracked, slipped and missing tiles, flashing issues and any signs of underlayment failure.

Matching tiles. For repairs, we look for matching tiles in color and profile. When an exact match is not available, we discuss options, such as using salvaged tiles from a less visible area.

Written recommendations. We explain whether the roof needs tile repairs, underlayment work or a broader solution, and we put it in writing.

Insurance support. If hail has damaged your tile roof, we document the damage and can meet your adjuster.

What styles of concrete tile are common?

Concrete tile comes in several profiles that change the look of a home.

01

Flat profile

Flat tiles create a clean, modern look and can mimic slate or wood shake.

02

Low profile

Low-profile tiles have a gentle curve and work with many traditional styles.

Tile Underlayment

Why is underlayment so important on a tile roof?

On a tile roof, the underlayment is the real waterproofing layer.

Concrete tiles shed most of the rain, but wind-driven water, melting snow and water that runs under tiles all end up on the underlayment. Over time, sun that reaches gaps between tiles and heat in the attic below wear the underlayment out.

When the underlayment fails, leaks appear even though the tiles look fine. The fix is usually to remove the tiles carefully, replace the underlayment and battens if used, update flashings, and reinstall the tiles with new fasteners.

That work can extend the life of a tile roof significantly, because the tiles themselves often outlast several generations of underlayment. We include an underlayment assessment in every tile roof inspection, looking from the attic where we can and lifting tiles where it is safe.

Tile Maintenance

How do you care for a concrete tile roof in Wichita?

Tile is low-maintenance, but not maintenance-free.

01

Check after hail

Have the roof inspected after large hail to catch cracked tiles before water reaches the underlayment.

02

Keep valleys clear

Leaves and debris in valleys slow water and can push it under tiles.

03

Limit foot traffic

Ask satellite installers and other trades to avoid walking on tile, or have a roofer guide them.

04

Watch the ridge

Cracked ridge mortar and loose ridge tiles should be repaired promptly.

Kansas Conditions

How does concrete tile perform in Kansas weather?

Concrete tile was popularized in warmer, drier climates. In Kansas, a few conditions deserve extra attention.

Hail. Wichita is one of the most hail-prone areas in the country. Large hail can crack concrete tiles, and cracked tiles expose the underlayment to sun and water. Some tile products carry impact ratings, and an inspection after major storms is important.

Freeze-thaw. Water absorbed into tile and mortar can freeze and expand in winter. Quality tiles are made to handle freeze-thaw cycles, but cracked ridge mortar and damaged tiles let water in where freezing makes the damage worse.

Wind. Kansas wind can lift tiles that are not properly fastened. Current installation methods use more fasteners or clips than many older roofs.

Heat. Tile's mass and the air space beneath it can help reduce heat transfer into the attic during summer.

If you own a tile roof in the Wichita area, regular inspections are the best way to keep it performing.

Insurance and Tile

How does homeowners insurance handle tile roof damage?

Tile claims raise a few special issues.

01

Matching discontinued tiles

When a tile line is discontinued, matching repairs can be difficult. Ask your adjuster how your policy handles matching.

02

Underlayment exposed by hail

Cracked tiles can expose underlayment, which may then need repair as part of the claim.

03

Careful inspection matters

Walking a tile roof carelessly can break tiles, so damage should be documented by someone experienced with tile.

04

Check the Kansas Insurance Department guide

The Kansas Insurance Department publishes after-the-storm tips for homeowners.

Living With Tile

How should Wichita owners care for a concrete tile roof?

Concrete tile is heavy and long-lived, but the parts around it still need regular attention.

01

Stay off the tiles

Walking on concrete tile can crack pieces, especially in cold weather. Leave roof access to a roofer who knows where to step and how to protect the surface.

02

Check after hail

Large hail can chip corners or crack tiles. Cracked tiles let water reach the underlayment below, which is the true waterproofing layer. A post-storm inspection documents any broken pieces.

03

Watch valleys and flashing

Leaves and grit collect where tiles meet in valleys and at walls. Keeping those areas clear helps water reach the gutters instead of backing up under the tile.

04

Plan underlayment care

The tile often outlasts the underlayment beneath it. When the underlayment ages, tiles can be lifted, the underlayment replaced and sound tiles reinstalled, which is often more economical than a full material change.

Concrete Tile FAQ

When do I need concrete tile roofing?
You may need concrete tile roofing when you see leaks, missing materials, stains, loose flashing, storm damage, or repeated problems in the same roof area. A roof inspection helps separate a small repair from a larger issue before water reaches decking, insulation, or interior finishes.
Can concrete tile roofing be repaired instead of replaced?
Sometimes, yes. A newer roof with isolated damage may only need a focused repair. An older roof with widespread wear, repeated leaks, soft decking, or storm damage across several slopes may need replacement planning instead. The inspection should answer that question before you spend money.
Does Wichita weather affect concrete tile roofing?
Yes. Wichita roofs deal with hail, strong wind, sudden rain, heat, and fast temperature changes. Those conditions can lift shingles, stress flashing, loosen gutters, and open small leak paths. That is why we check the full area around the visible problem.
What other roof work might be connected?
This depends on what we find. Some concrete tile roofing calls connect to residential roofing, roof repair, roof replacement. Others connect to gutters, siding, flashing, or ventilation. We keep those recommendations tied to the actual roof condition, not a pre-set package.

More Answers

More Questions About Concrete Tile

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

Can a concrete tile roof be repaired after hail?
Yes. Individual cracked or broken tiles can be replaced. On older roofs, matching tiles can be a challenge, and we discuss options during the inspection.
Is concrete tile too heavy for my house?
Concrete tile is much heavier than asphalt shingles. Homes not built for tile usually need an engineering review before switching.
Why does my tile roof leak if the tiles look fine?
The underlayment beneath the tiles does most of the waterproofing. Aged underlayment or failed flashing can leak even when the tiles look intact.
Can you walk on a concrete tile roof?
Tile can break under foot if walked incorrectly. We use careful techniques and avoid walking where it is not safe.
Can you replace the underlayment and reuse my tiles?
Often, yes. Tiles can be removed carefully, the underlayment and flashings replaced, and the tiles reinstalled. Some breakage is normal, so we plan for replacement tiles.
Can I add solar panels to a concrete tile roof?
It is possible, but the mounting has to be planned with the tile in mind. We recommend an inspection before solar work so cracked tiles and underlayment condition are addressed first, and the installer can flash each mount correctly.
Can broken concrete tiles be replaced one at a time?
Yes. Individual cracked tiles can usually be removed and replaced. The challenge is finding matching tiles for older roofs, and we tell you how close a match will be.
