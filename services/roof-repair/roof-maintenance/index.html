Why It Matters

Why does roof maintenance matter in Wichita?

A Kansas roof deals with hail, strong wind, hard summer sun and freezing winter nights. Each season wears on different parts.

  1. 01

    Small parts fail first

    Shingles get most of the attention, but the rubber pipe boots, sealant joints, exposed nail heads and metal flashings usually wear out before the field of shingles does. Those small parts cause a large share of leaks.

  2. 02

    Leaks hide in the attic

    Water can drip onto insulation and decking for months before a stain appears on the ceiling. By then, the decking may be soft and the insulation ruined. Regular checks catch the problem while the fix is still small.

  3. 03

    Storm damage adds up

    A storm that does not cause a leak can still loosen shingles or bruise them. The next storm finds that weak spot. Checking after major storms keeps small damage from stacking up.

  4. 04

    You can plan instead of react

    Knowing the condition of your roof lets you budget for a replacement on your timeline instead of during an emergency.

What We Check

What does a roof maintenance check cover?

We look at the parts of the roof that wear out first, then tell you what needs attention now and what can wait.

Pipe boots and vent collars

The rubber collars around plumbing pipes crack in the sun. A cracked boot lets water run straight down the pipe into the attic.

Flashing at walls and chimneys

We look for lifted, rusted or loose flashing and for dried-out sealant where flashing meets brick, siding or stucco.

Shingles and ridge caps

Missing, lifted, cracked or creased shingles, along with worn ridge caps, are marked and photographed.

Exposed nails and fasteners

Nail heads that have backed out can let water in and can puncture the shingle above.

Valleys

Valleys carry the most water on the roof. We check for debris, worn shingles and open joints.

Gutters and drip edge

Clogged or sagging gutters can push water back under the roof edge. We note any problems with the gutters and drip edge.

Attic signs

When we can reach the attic, we check for stains on the decking, damp insulation, daylight around penetrations and signs of poor ventilation.

Skylights and roof vents

Skylight flashing, box vents, turbines and power vents are checked for cracks, loose fasteners and gaps.

Overall roof life

We give you an honest estimate of how much useful life the roof has left and what would change that.

Common Repairs

What are the most common maintenance repairs?

Most maintenance repairs are small, quick and far cheaper than repairing water damage later.

Every repair comes with a written price before we start. If a repair is not worth doing because the roof is near the end of its life, we will tell you.

  1. 01

    Replacing pipe boots

    Swapping a cracked boot for a new one is one of the most effective leak-prevention repairs on a shingle roof.

  2. 02

    Resealing flashing

    Counter flashing and wall flashing joints are resealed or refastened. When flashing is rusted through or badly bent, we replace it.

  3. 03

    Replacing damaged shingles

    A few missing or cracked shingles can be replaced so the slope sheds water again. We match color and style as closely as available products allow.

  4. 04

    Securing exposed nails

    Popped nails are reset or replaced, and the shingles above are sealed down.

  5. 05

    Clearing valleys and roof debris

    Leaves, needles and branch debris hold water against the roof. Clearing them lets the roof drain.

  6. 06

    Fixing small vent problems

    Loose or cracked vent caps and failing seals around vents are repaired so wind-driven rain cannot get in.

Season by Season

What should a yearly roof maintenance plan look like?

Each season brings different risks. Here is a simple plan for a Wichita home.

Spring

before storm season

Check for winter damage, clear gutters and valleys, and replace any loose or cracked shingles before hail and wind season starts.

Summer

after storms

After any storm with large hail or high wind, look for shingles in the yard, dented gutters and damaged vents. Call for an inspection if you see damage.

Fall

before winter

Clear leaves from gutters and valleys, check that downspouts drain away from the house and make sure flashing is sealed before freezing weather.

Winter

watch from inside

Look for attic frost, drips or ceiling stains after snow or ice. Stay off the roof in icy weather and call if you see a problem.

Roof Age

How should maintenance change as your roof ages?

A new roof and a 20-year-old roof need different attention.

  1. The first few years

    A new roof needs little more than gutter cleaning and a look after big storms. Keep your installation paperwork and warranty documents somewhere safe.

  2. The middle years

    As sealants age and pipe boots weather, small repairs become more common. A check every spring and fall and after major storms keeps the roof on track.

  3. The later years

    On an older roof, shingles lose granules, become brittle and may curl at the edges. Maintenance can extend the roof’s life for a while, but at some point repairs stop being worth the money. We will tell you when you reach that point and explain what a roof replacement would cost, using our planning price of $505 per roofing square.

  4. After any major storm

    Regardless of age, schedule a free roof inspection after a hailstorm or high winds. Damage that qualifies for an insurance claim is easiest to document soon after the storm.

Records

Why should you keep records of roof maintenance?

A simple folder of roof records helps in several ways.

Warranty questions

Records show when the roof was installed and what was done to it since.

Insurance claims

Photos of a well-maintained roof before a storm help show what the storm changed.

Selling your home

Buyers and inspectors ask about roof age and repairs. Records answer those questions quickly. Our lifetime transferable warranty can also transfer to the next owner.

Planning a replacement

Seeing repairs become more frequent helps you decide when to budget for a new roof.

Repair or Replace

When is maintenance no longer enough?

Maintenance keeps a healthy roof healthy. It cannot make an old or badly damaged roof new again.

These signs usually mean it is time to think about replacement instead of more repairs:

  • Shingles are curling, cracking or losing granules across large areas of the roof
  • Repairs are needed every year in different places
  • The decking feels soft or spongy in several spots
  • Hail damage is spread across many slopes
  • The roof is near the end of the life you would expect for its material

We explain the trade-off honestly. Read our guide on whether to repair or replace a roof for more detail.

    Trees and Roofs

    How do trees affect roof maintenance?

    Shade trees are a big part of many Wichita neighborhoods, and they change how a roof wears.

    We point out branches that should be trimmed and note debris-prone areas that need regular clearing.

    1. 01

      Leaves and needles collect

      Debris settles in valleys, behind chimneys and in gutters, where it holds moisture against the roof.

    2. 02

      Branches rub

      Limbs that touch the roof wear away granules and can lift shingles in the wind.

    3. 03

      Shade slows drying

      Shaded slopes stay damp longer, which encourages moss and algae.

    4. 04

      Animals use branches

      Squirrels and raccoons use overhanging limbs to reach the roof, where they can chew vents and soffits.

    Roof Features

    Which roof features need the most maintenance?

    Some parts of the roof need extra attention because of how water flows around them.

    Chimneys

    Chimney flashing and the brick or crown above it wear over time. A cricket behind wide chimneys helps send water around it.

    Skylights

    Skylights need sound flashing and seals. Older skylights may need resealing or replacement.

    Dormers and walls

    Step flashing along dormer walls and roof-to-wall joints is a common leak point.

    Low-slope sections

    Porch or addition roofs with very little slope drain more slowly and need closer checks.

    Hidden Damage

    What roof damage is easy to miss from the ground?

    Some of the most important problems are invisible unless someone looks closely.

    That is why a close-up check, with photos, tells you far more than a look from the yard.

    1. 01

      Bruised shingles

      Hail can soften the mat of a shingle without breaking the surface. Bruises often look like dark spots and can lead to cracks later.

    2. 02

      Broken seal strips

      Wind can break the adhesive that holds shingle tabs down, even if the shingles settle back into place. Unsealed tabs lift in the next storm.

    3. 03

      Cracked flashing sealant

      Hairline cracks in sealant at walls and chimneys are hard to see but let water in during wind-driven rain.

    4. 04

      Soft decking

      Rot under the shingles may only show up as a slight dip or a spongy feel underfoot.

    More Answers

    More Questions About Roof Maintenance

    Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

    How often should a roof be checked?
    We suggest a check in spring and fall and after any major hail or wind storm. Older roofs benefit from more frequent attention.
    Do you clean gutters during roof maintenance?
    We look at gutters and drip edge as part of the check and note any problems. Ask us about gutter work when you schedule, and we will explain what we can include.
    Is roof maintenance worth it on an older roof?
    Often, yes, if the roof still has useful life. A few small repairs can buy time to plan a replacement. If repairs no longer make sense, we will say so.
    How much does a maintenance repair cost?
    It depends on what needs fixing. Replacing a pipe boot is a small job, while rebuilding chimney flashing takes more time. You get the price in writing before any work starts.
    Can maintenance help my roof warranty?
    Keeping records of inspections and repairs makes warranty questions easier to answer. Read your manufacturer warranty for specific maintenance terms.
    Do you do maintenance on commercial roofs?
    Yes. See [commercial inspections and maintenance](/services/commercial-roofing/commercial-inspections-maintenance/) for flat and low-slope roofs.
    What is the most common maintenance problem you find?
    Cracked pipe boots and dried-out sealant around flashing are among the most common. Both are inexpensive to fix early and expensive to ignore.
    Can I pressure wash my roof?
    We do not recommend pressure washing shingles. High pressure can strip granules and lift tabs. Ask us before using any cleaning method on your roof.
    Does moss or algae hurt my roof?
    Dark streaks are often algae, which is mostly cosmetic. Moss holds moisture and can lift shingles over time. We can look at it and recommend the right approach.
    Should I have my roof checked before selling my home?
    It is a good idea. A pre-listing check lets you fix small problems and answer buyer questions with confidence.
    What if you find storm damage during maintenance?
    We document it with photos and explain whether it may be worth contacting your insurer about a claim.
    Can you inspect my roof without walking on it?
    When walking is unsafe, we use other methods and explain any limits of what we could see.
