Is It an Emergency?

What counts as a roofing emergency?

Some roof problems can wait for a scheduled visit. These cannot, because every hour lets more water into the house.

Water coming through the ceiling

Dripping from a light fixture, a spreading stain or water running down a wall means the roof is open somewhere above. Water follows rafters and pipes, so the opening may be several feet from the drip.

A sagging or bulging ceiling

Drywall that holds water can give way without warning. Keep everyone out of the room beneath it and call right away.

Shingles torn off by wind

When wind strips shingles from a slope, the underlayment or bare decking is exposed. The next rain can soak straight through.

A tree or limb on the roof

Branches can puncture decking, crack rafters and tear flashing. Do not climb up to remove a limb. Weight and broken framing make that dangerous.

Hail punctures or broken skylights

Very large hail can crack shingles through, split vents and break skylight glazing. Any opening you can see from the ground needs protection before the next storm.

Flashing ripped loose

Wind can peel metal flashing away from a chimney, wall or roof edge. That leaves a joint wide open to wind-driven rain.

First Steps

What should you do in the first hour?

Your safety comes first, then protecting the inside of the house. Leave the roof itself to a crew with the right equipment.

  1. 01

    Stay safe

    Do not go on the roof during or right after a storm. Wet shingles, wind and hidden damage make falls likely. If water is near light fixtures, outlets or the electrical panel, keep clear and turn off power to that area only if you can do so safely.

  2. 02

    Protect what is inside

    Move furniture, electronics and rugs away from the leak. Put down towels and catch drips in buckets or bins. Keep people and pets out of any room with a sagging ceiling.

  3. 03

    Take photos

    Photograph water stains, the drip, damaged items and anything you can see on the roof from the ground. Note the date and time of the storm. These photos help with both the repair and any insurance claim.

  4. 04

    Call us

    Call (316) 742-1900 and tell us what you see. We will ask a few questions, tell you how soon a crew can come out, and explain what to do until we arrive.

  5. 05

    Contact your insurer if the damage came from a storm

    Most homeowners policies expect you to take reasonable steps to keep damage from getting worse. Keep every receipt for emergency work. The Kansas Insurance Department has storm resources for policyholders.

How We Respond

How does an emergency roof repair visit work?

Emergency work happens in two parts: stop the water now, then fix the roof properly.

  1. 01

    We talk through it on the phone

    We ask where the water is, what the storm did and whether anyone is at risk. That tells us what to bring and how urgent the visit is.

  2. 02

    We look at the damage

    A crew member checks the roof from the ground and, when it is safe, from the roof. We also look at the attic or ceiling where water is coming in.

  3. 03

    We protect the opening

    Depending on the damage, we tarp the area, seal a temporary patch, or replace a few shingles right away. The goal is to keep the next rain out.

  4. 04

    We document everything

    We photograph the damage before and after the temporary work. You get those photos for your records and your insurer.

  5. 05

    We explain the permanent repair

    Once the roof is protected, we explain what the permanent fix involves and send the scope and price in writing.

  6. 06

    We schedule the real repair

    Permanent repairs happen when the weather allows and, for insurance claims, after you have talked with your adjuster.

Temporary or Permanent

What is the difference between tarping, a temporary patch and a permanent repair?

Each one has a place. Knowing the difference helps you understand what you are paying for.

Fastest cover · large openings

Emergency tarping

A heavy tarp is secured over the damaged area and fastened so wind does not lift it. Tarping is the fastest way to cover a large opening, such as a section stripped by wind or a hole from a fallen limb. It is meant to hold for a short time, not for months. Read more about emergency roof tarping.

Small openings

Temporary patch

For a small opening, such as a few missing shingles or a lifted flashing, a crew can seal the spot with membrane, sealant or replacement shingles. This keeps water out until the full repair is done.

Long-term fix · warranty

Permanent repair

The permanent repair replaces damaged decking, underlayment, shingles and flashing so the area matches the rest of the roof and carries the workmanship warranty. On a badly damaged roof, the permanent fix may be a roof replacement.

Common Causes

What usually causes roof emergencies in Wichita?

Our weather is the reason. Spring and summer storms cause most emergency calls, but winter has its own risks.

  1. 01

    Straight-line winds

    Thunderstorm winds can lift shingle tabs, break the seal strips and peel whole sections away, especially on older roofs where the sealant has aged. Ridge caps and roof edges go first.

  2. 02

    Hail

    South-central Kansas sees frequent hail. Small hail bruises shingles. Very large hail can fracture shingles, dent vents and break skylights, which can turn into an active leak during the same storm. The National Weather Service hail size reference shows how hail sizes compare.

  3. 03

    Falling trees and limbs

    Mature trees in older Wichita neighborhoods are beautiful, but heavy limbs over a roof are a risk in high wind and during ice storms.

  4. 04

    Ice and snow

    A winter storm can leave ice along the eaves. Water backing up behind that ice can find its way under shingles and into the walls.

  5. 05

    Aging flashing and pipe boots

    Not every emergency is caused by a storm. A cracked pipe boot or failed chimney flashing can leak for weeks inside the attic before it shows up on a ceiling during a heavy rain.

Insurance

How do emergency repairs fit into an insurance claim?

Emergency work and the insurance claim happen side by side. A few habits make that process easier.

  1. 01

    Protecting the home is expected

    Policies generally ask homeowners to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Tarping or a temporary patch is that kind of step.

  2. 02

    Keep the receipts and photos

    Save the invoice for emergency work and the photos taken before and after. Your insurer may reimburse reasonable emergency costs under the claim, depending on your policy.

  3. 03

    Ask before permanent work

    Talk with your insurer before the permanent repair or replacement, so the adjuster can see the damage. Temporary protection does not stop an adjuster from evaluating the roof.

  4. 04

    Read your policy for specifics

    Coverage, deductibles and timelines depend on your policy. If you have questions about how your claim works, ask your insurer or agent. We can explain what we found on the roof and share photos. See how we help with insurance claim roof replacement.

Storm Chasers

How do you avoid emergency-repair pressure after a storm?

After big storms, out-of-town crews knock on doors across Wichita. Emergencies make it easy to rush. A few checks protect you.

Check the Kansas registration

Kansas requires roofing contractors to register with the Attorney General. You can look up any company in the roofing registration directory.

Get the scope in writing

Even emergency work should come with a written description of what was done and what it cost.

Do not sign away your claim

Be careful with paperwork that hands control of your insurance claim to a contractor you just met. Read everything before you sign.

Look for a local address

A real local office and phone number mean someone will still be here when you need warranty work. Our office is at 1809 S Hoover Rd Suite 101, Wichita, KS 67209.

After the Emergency

What happens after the roof is protected?

Stopping the water is only the first step. The rest of the house and roof still need attention.

  1. 01

    Dry the inside quickly

    Wet drywall, insulation and carpet should be dried as soon as possible. Materials that stay wet for more than a day or two are at risk of mold. For large areas, a water mitigation company can help.

  2. 02

    Check the whole roof, not just the hole

    A storm strong enough to cause one opening often causes damage elsewhere. We look at every slope, the flashing, the vents, the gutters and the ridge so nothing is missed. That full look is part of our free roof inspection.

  3. 03

    Look in the attic

    Wet insulation loses much of its value and can hold moisture against the decking. Damaged insulation may need to be removed and replaced.

  4. 04

    Plan the permanent repair

    We will explain whether a repair, a section replacement or a full replacement makes sense, and why. Every option comes with a written price.

Prevention

How can you lower the chance of another roof emergency?

No roof is storm-proof, but these steps reduce the odds and the damage.

Trim limbs over the roof

Have heavy branches over the house cut back before storm season.

Replace aging pipe boots and sealant

Small parts wear out before shingles do. Replacing them early prevents many slow leaks.

Choose impact-rated shingles at replacement

Class 4 impact-rated shingles hold up better to hail. Ask about them when it is time for a new roof. See asphalt shingles.

Keep gutters clear

Clogged gutters overflow at the roof edge and can push water under shingles during heavy rain. See gutter repair.

Schedule regular checks

A check in spring and fall catches loose shingles and failing flashing before storms find them. See roof maintenance.

More Questions About Emergency Roof Repair

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

Do you offer emergency roof tarping?
Yes. When a storm or fallen limb leaves part of the roof open, we can tarp the area to keep water out until the permanent repair is done. Call (316) 742-1900.
How fast can you get to my house?
It depends on the weather and how many homes were hit by the same storm. When you call, we will tell you honestly how soon a crew can come out and what to do until then.
What are your hours?
Our office is open Monday through Friday from 8 AM to 7 PM and Saturday from 10 AM to 6 PM. Call (316) 742-1900 to reach us.
Should I get on the roof to put a tarp down myself?
We do not recommend it. Wet, damaged roofs are slippery and may have weak spots. Focus on protecting the inside of the house and let a trained crew handle the roof.
Is emergency repair covered by insurance?
Storm-related emergency work is often part of a homeowners claim, but coverage depends on your policy. Keep the receipts and photos and ask your insurer how they handle emergency costs.
Will a tarp damage my shingles?
A properly installed tarp is fastened to protect as much of the roof as possible. Any fastener holes are in an area that will be repaired or replaced during the permanent fix.
How long can a tarp stay on the roof?
A tarp is a short-term measure. Sun and wind wear it down, so the permanent repair should be scheduled as soon as weather and your insurance process allow.
Can you help if water is coming in around a chimney?
Yes. Chimney flashing is a common leak point after wind storms. We can seal the area temporarily and then rebuild the flashing properly.
Do you handle emergency repairs on commercial roofs?
Yes. We repair leaks on TPO and modified bitumen roofs as well. See our [flat roof repair](/services/commercial-roofing/flat-roof-repair/) page for details.
