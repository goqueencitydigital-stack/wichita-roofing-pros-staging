Overview

Severe weather can affect different parts of a roof in different ways. Wichita Roofing Pros provides roofing storm damage repair by evaluating affected slopes, ridge caps, flashing, vents, exposed metal components, gutters, and visible impact or wind-related conditions before recommending work.

Wichita hail, straight-line winds, heavy rain, and sudden storms can loosen materials, damage vulnerable roof details, or expose openings that were not obvious beforehand. We document visible conditions when access allows, explain what appears damaged, and help homeowners determine the appropriate next step without assuming every storm requires a larger roofing project.

Quick answerAfter hail or high wind, a storm damage inspection documents what the storm did to your shingles, flashing, vents, gutters and soft metals. Wichita Roofing Pros photographs the damage, explains repair or replacement options and can meet your insurance adjuster. Your insurer decides what the policy covers.

Symptoms

What signs mean you should call?

These are the signs homeowners most often notice first. Any one of them is worth a free inspection.

  • Dents in gutters, downspouts or vents
  • Bruised or cracked shingles
  • Granules piling at downspouts
  • Missing or creased shingles after wind
  • Damaged window screens or siding
Evaluation

How do we evaluate the problem?

Every inspection follows the same order so the recommendation is based on the whole roof, not the first symptom.

  1. Start with your concern. We inspect the spot that made you call.
  2. Inspect outward. Surrounding materials, flashing, valleys, vents, drainage and edges.
  3. Document. Photos when access is safe.
  4. Explain options. What needs attention now, what can be monitored, and why.
  5. Write it down. Scope and price in writing before work starts.

Recognizing Damage

What does hail damage on a roof look like?

Hail damage is not always obvious from the ground. These are the signs we look for up close.

01

Bruises and soft spots

Hail can crush the mat under the granules, leaving a soft spot that feels like a bruise on an apple.

02

Granule loss in round patterns

Round dark spots where granules were knocked off expose the asphalt to sunlight.

03

Cracked shingles

Larger hail can crack shingles, especially older or brittle ones.

04

Dents on metal

Dents on vents, gutters, downspouts and window wraps help confirm hail size and direction.

05

Split wood shakes

Fresh splits with sharp edges and light-colored wood inside point to recent hail.

06

Damage on soft surfaces

Spatter marks on decks, fences and siding can show where hail hit hardest.

After the Storm

What should you do in the first days after a hailstorm?

A few steps protect your home and your claim.

Stay safe. Do not climb onto a wet or damaged roof. Look from the ground and photograph what you can see.

Prevent further damage. If water is coming in, call us to tarp the roof. Your policy may require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.

Get an inspection. We document damage on every slope with photos, along with gutters, siding and other parts of the house.

Contact your insurer. Report the claim to your insurance company and share the storm date. The Kansas Insurance Department recommends documenting damage and keeping receipts for emergency repairs.

Be careful with storm chasers. After big storms, out-of-town contractors often go door to door. In Kansas, roofing contractors must register with the Attorney General’s office. Check registration and local references before signing anything.

What Should Wichita Homeowners Know About Storm Damage?

Roof damage after severe weather is not always obvious from the ground. Hail can leave impact marks or bruising, while strong winds may lift materials, loosen flashing, damage ridge areas, or affect vents and other exposed components.

A proper hail damage roof repair evaluation should look beyond one visible mark and consider how the surrounding roof system performed during the storm. Similarly, wind damage roof repair should focus on actual movement, displacement, exposed edges, and connected components rather than assuming damage simply because high winds occurred.

  • We check affected roof slopes, ridge caps, flashing, vents, exposed metals, gutters, and other safely visible conditions.
  • Hail impacts and wind effects can vary between different sides of the same roof.
  • Recent damage should be distinguished from normal aging or older wear whenever possible.
  • Photos help homeowners understand visible conditions without relying on vague descriptions.
  • Isolated damage may require focused work, while broader deterioration should be explained separately.

Experienced storm damage roofers should be able to show what they found and explain why the recommended scope addresses those conditions.

What other roofing work might need attention?

A severe-weather concern can sometimes connect to another roofing service, but each issue should be routed to the page that best matches the homeowner’s actual need.

For broader non-storm damage, homeowners can visit Roof Repair. If water is entering the home, the Roof Leak Repair page focuses on tracing the likely water-entry point. Homeowners who are unsure whether roofing work is needed can use the Free Roof Inspection service. Insurance-related replacement questions should remain with the dedicated insurance replacement resource.

  • A roof storm repair should remain focused when weather damage is limited to a serviceable area.
  • Active water intrusion may need separate leak-source evaluation.
  • A broader roof condition should not automatically be classified as storm damage.
  • Insurance decisions belong to the homeowner and insurer; our role is to explain observable roofing conditions and the proposed scope.

This keeps weather-related repairs separate from generic repair, leak detection, inspection, and replacement intent.

What Details Matter Most After Hail or High Winds?

01

Hail Impact and Material Condition

Hail size, wind speed, roof material, age, slope, orientation, and existing condition can influence how a roof responds. Visible dents on vents, flashing, or other exposed components can provide useful context, but soft metal hail damage repair should never be treated as automatic proof that every surrounding roofing material has failed. The roof covering itself still needs to be evaluated.
02

Wind-Related Damage

Strong or straight-line winds can lift edges, displace materials, loosen ridge components, move flashing, or expose vulnerable attachment points. Roof wind damage repair services should therefore evaluate both the visibly affected area and nearby components that may have experienced movement during the same weather event.
03

Severe Weather Exposure

Heavy rain can expose openings created by wind or impact, while repeated weather cycles may reveal conditions that were previously difficult to notice. A severe weather roof repair recommendation should be based on visible roofing evidence, not simply the fact that a storm passed through Wichita.
04

Cost and Repair Scope

Storm damage roof repair cost depends on the material, amount of damage, roof slope, accessibility, affected components, flashing work, surrounding condition, and the final approved scope. A property-specific evaluation is more useful than a generic online price because one home may require a localized correction while another has several affected roof details.

Common Questions

Storm & Hail Damage FAQ

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

How can I tell if hail damaged my roof?
Hail damage can appear as impact marks, bruising, damaged ridge areas, dents on exposed roof components, or changes to the roofing surface. Some conditions are difficult to confirm safely from the ground, so the roof should be evaluated before conclusions are made.
Can wind damage a roof without removing shingles?
Yes. Wind can lift edges, loosen attachment points, move flashing, or affect ridge components without leaving an obvious missing section. The affected areas and surrounding roof details should be evaluated for visible movement or damage.
Can storm damage be repaired without replacing the entire roof?
Often, yes. Storm damage roof repairs may remain localized when the affected area is limited, and the surrounding system is still serviceable. Broader deterioration is a separate issue and should be evaluated on its own.
What should I do after a severe storm in Wichita?
From the ground, look for missing or displaced materials, debris, damaged gutters, visible roof-edge changes, or new interior moisture. Avoid climbing onto the roof yourself. If something looks different or you are concerned about recent weather exposure, arrange a professional evaluation.
Does every hailstorm mean my roof needs work?
No. A hailstorm does not automatically mean a roof has sustained functional damage. Hail size, wind, material type, roof age, orientation, and existing condition all matter. Recommendations should be based on visible findings.
More Answers

More Questions About Storm & Hail Damage

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

Should I call my insurer or a roofer first?
Many homeowners get a free inspection first to learn whether there is damage worth claiming, then contact their insurer. Review your policy for reporting deadlines.
Will you meet my insurance adjuster?
Yes. We can meet the adjuster to review documented damage. The coverage decision belongs to the insurer.
How long do I have to file a hail claim?
Deadlines depend on your policy. Check your policy or ask your agent, and report damage as soon as practical after the storm.
Can hail damage be hidden?
Yes. Bruising and granule loss can be hard to see from the ground but can shorten roof life. An inspection up close is the best way to know.
Do you meet with insurance adjusters?
We can meet your adjuster on the roof and point out the damage we documented.
Should I wait for the adjuster before tarping my roof?
No. If water is getting in, take reasonable steps to prevent more damage, take photos and keep receipts. Then share them with your insurer.
Does wind damage look different from hail damage?
Yes. Wind usually lifts, creases or removes shingles along edges and ridges, while hail leaves round bruises and granule loss across the roof.
