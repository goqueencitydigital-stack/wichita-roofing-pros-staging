Overview

Your attic needs to breathe. Air should come in low at the eaves and leave high near the ridge, carrying heat out in summer and moisture out in winter. When that flow is blocked or unbalanced, shingles age faster, insulation gets damp and upstairs rooms stay hot. Wichita Roofing Pros checks your intake and exhaust and corrects the ventilation, often as part of a roof replacement.

How It Works

How does attic ventilation work?

A vented attic is designed to let outdoor air move through it in a steady path.

Air comes in low. Intake vents at the soffits or eaves let cooler outside air into the attic at its lowest point.

Air leaves high. Exhaust vents near the ridge let warm, moist air out at the top of the attic. The U.S. Department of Energy’s guide to attic air sealing describes this low-in, high-out pattern and notes that continuous soffit vents work best.

The air path must stay open. Insulation pushed into the eaves can block intake vents. Baffles keep a channel open between the soffit and the attic.

Balance matters. Intake and exhaust need to work together. Too much exhaust and too little intake can pull conditioned air out of the house through ceiling gaps.

Warning Signs

What are the signs of poor attic ventilation?

Ventilation problems show up in the attic, on the roof and inside the house.

01

Hot upstairs rooms

A very hot attic radiates heat into upper rooms and makes the air conditioner work harder in summer.

02

Frost on nails in winter

Frost or water droplets on roofing nails and the underside of the decking mean moist indoor air is reaching the attic and not getting out.

03

Damp or matted insulation

Moisture reduces how well insulation works and can lead to mold.

04

Shingles aging early

Excess attic heat can contribute to curling, blistering and premature aging of shingles.

05

Ice along the eaves

Warm attic air can melt snow on the roof. That water refreezes at the colder eaves and can back up under shingles.

06

Musty smells or mold

A musty attic or dark staining on the decking points to trapped moisture.

Vent Types

What types of roof vents are there?

Each type has a role. The right combination depends on your roof shape and attic.

01

Soffit vents

Intake vents in the underside of the eaves. Continuous or evenly spaced soffit vents supply most of the intake air on many homes.

02

Ridge vents

A continuous exhaust vent along the peak of the roof, covered by ridge caps. It lets warm air out along the full length of the ridge.

03

Box or static vents

Low-profile exhaust vents set near the ridge. They are common on hip roofs or roofs with short ridges.

04

Turbine vents

Exhaust vents with spinning heads that use wind to pull air from the attic.

05

Power attic fans

Electric fans that push air out of the attic. They need enough intake to work without pulling air from the living space.

06

Gable vents

Vents in the gable walls. They can help on some homes but can disrupt airflow when combined with other exhaust types.

Mixing Vents

Why should you avoid mixing exhaust vent types?

More vents are not always better. Different exhaust vents can work against each other.

Short-circuiting. When two kinds of exhaust vents sit on the same attic, such as a ridge vent and a gable vent or a power fan and a ridge vent, one can pull air in through the other. Air takes the shortest path and skips the lower attic, which leaves heat and moisture behind.

Power fans need intake. A power fan with too little intake can pull air from the house through light fixtures, attic hatches and other gaps. That wastes cooled or heated air and can draw moisture into the attic.

One exhaust system per attic space is the usual goal. We look at the vents you have now and recommend a single, balanced approach.

How Much Ventilation

How much attic ventilation does a home need?

Building codes set a minimum. Your attic layout decides how to reach it.

The code starting point. The International Residential Code sets the minimum net free ventilating area at 1/150 of the attic floor area. That can drop to 1/300 when the ventilation is balanced, with roughly 40 to 50 percent of it provided by exhaust vents high on the roof and the rest by intake vents below. Your local building department decides which code edition applies.

A simple example. A 1,500-square-foot attic at 1/150 needs about 10 square feet of net free area. At 1/300, with balanced intake and exhaust, it needs about 5 square feet, split between soffit intake and ridge or roof exhaust.

Net free area is not the vent size. Screens and louvers reduce how much air a vent actually passes. Vent manufacturers list net free area for each product, which is the number that counts.

Intake is often the weak link. Many older homes have plenty of exhaust and very little working intake, especially when soffits are solid or blocked by insulation.

Moisture

Where does attic moisture come from?

Most attic moisture comes from inside the house, not from the roof.

01

Bathroom fans

Bathroom fans should vent outside. The Department of Energy’s ventilation guide says kitchen and bathroom vents should never vent into the attic.

02

Kitchen and dryer vents

Dryer and kitchen exhaust carry a lot of moisture. They need to exhaust outdoors through properly sealed vents.

03

Air leaks from below

Gaps around light fixtures, wiring, plumbing and the attic hatch let warm, moist air rise into the attic.

04

Roof leaks

A small roof leak adds moisture too. Stains on the decking around vents or pipes can point to a leak rather than ventilation.

Air Sealing

How do air sealing and insulation relate to ventilation?

Ventilation works best as part of a system with air sealing and insulation.

Seal first, then insulate. The Department of Energy recommends sealing air leaks between the living space and the attic before adding insulation, according to its Consumer Guide to Durable Attics.

Keep vents clear. When insulation is added, baffles should keep soffit vents open.

Know what we do. Our work focuses on the roof side: intake and exhaust vents, ridge vent installation, soffit vent openings and the roof penetrations themselves. For whole-house air sealing or insulation upgrades, we can point out what we see and suggest you talk with an insulation or energy professional.

Roof Replacement

Why is roof replacement the best time to fix ventilation?

With the old shingles off, the roof is open and ventilation changes are simpler.

Ridge vents can be cut in. When the ridge is exposed, the decking can be cut back for a continuous ridge vent.

Old vents can be removed. Extra exhaust vents that are causing short-circuiting can be removed and the decking patched.

Intake can be added. Soffit vents can be added or opened at the same time if the soffits allow it. See soffit, fascia and trim.

Shingle warranties expect proper ventilation. Shingle manufacturers’ installation instructions call for adequate attic ventilation, and poor ventilation can affect warranty coverage. Getting it right protects your investment. Learn more about roof replacement.

Wichita Climate

Why does attic ventilation matter so much in Kansas?

Our weather swings from hot, humid summers to cold winters, and the attic feels both extremes.

Summer heat builds up fast. On a sunny Wichita afternoon, an unvented or poorly vented attic can get far hotter than the outdoors. That heat bakes the shingles from below and pushes into the rooms underneath.

Winter moisture has to go somewhere. Cooking, showers and everyday living add moisture to indoor air. When that air leaks into a cold attic, it can condense on the underside of the decking.

Storm season adds stress. A roof that already runs hot ages faster, and aged shingles hold up worse in hail and wind. Good ventilation is one of the ways to help a roof last.

Older Homes

What ventilation problems are common in older Wichita homes?

Older houses were often built with different standards, and many have been changed over the years.

01

Solid soffits

Many older homes have solid wood soffits with few or no vents, which leaves the attic with almost no intake.

02

Painted-over vents

Years of paint can close the openings in soffit vents.

03

Insulation blocking the eaves

Blown-in insulation added later often fills the eaves and covers the intake vents unless baffles were installed.

04

A patchwork of exhaust vents

Some roofs have gable vents, box vents, a turbine and a ridge vent all on the same attic, added at different times.

More Answers

More Questions About Attic Ventilation

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

Do I need a ridge vent?
Not every roof can use one. Ridge vents work well on roofs with long ridges and good soffit intake. Hip roofs with short ridges may need box vents instead.
Will more vents make my house cooler?
Balanced ventilation can lower attic temperatures, but adding the wrong vents can backfire. Air sealing and insulation also affect how cool upstairs rooms stay.
Can attic ventilation stop ice dams?
Good ventilation and air sealing help keep the roof deck cold, which reduces ice dams. Insulation and sealing leaks from the house below matter just as much.
Should my bathroom fan vent into the attic?
No. Bathroom fans should vent to the outdoors. Venting moist air into the attic can cause mold and damage the decking.
Is a power attic fan a good idea?
It can be on some homes, but only with enough intake. Without it, a power fan can pull conditioned air from the house. We will tell you whether it fits your attic.
Can you add soffit vents to my home?
Often, yes. It depends on how the soffits and eaves are built. We check during the inspection and explain the options.
Does attic ventilation matter in winter?
Yes. In winter, ventilation helps remove moisture that rises from the house. Without it, moisture can condense on the cold decking.
How much does ventilation work cost?
It depends on the changes needed. Adding a ridge vent during a replacement is simpler than retrofitting vents on an existing roof. You get the price in writing first.
How do I know if my attic has enough intake?
Look under the eaves for vents, then check from inside the attic whether light or air comes through at the eaves. We can measure both intake and exhaust during an inspection.
Does a metal roof need attic ventilation?
Yes. A vented attic under a metal roof still needs balanced intake and exhaust. Specific details depend on the roof system.
Can poor ventilation void my shingle warranty?
Shingle manufacturers call for proper ventilation in their installation instructions, and inadequate ventilation can limit coverage. Read your specific warranty for details.
Do you install baffles at the eaves?
When we open or add soffit intake, we make sure there is a clear air path from the soffit into the attic, which may include baffles where insulation blocks it.
