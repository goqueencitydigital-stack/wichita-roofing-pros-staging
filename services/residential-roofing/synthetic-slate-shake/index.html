Synthetic vs. Natural

How does synthetic slate or shake compare with the real thing?

Synthetic products are designed to look like natural slate or cedar shake while solving their biggest drawbacks.

01

Weight

Natural slate is very heavy and can require extra framing. Synthetic slate weighs much less, so most homes can carry it without structural changes.

02

Maintenance

Cedar shake needs ongoing care to resist rot, splitting and moss. Synthetic shake does not absorb water the same way and needs far less maintenance.

03

Breakage

Natural slate can crack when walked on or hit by hail. Many synthetic products are rated for impact and are easier to repair.

04

Appearance

Quality synthetic products use molded textures and varied colors to look natural from the street. We bring samples so you can compare in daylight at your home.

Installation

What should you know about installing synthetic slate and shake?

Synthetic roofing has its own installation rules, and they differ by manufacturer.

Follow the manufacturer exactly. Each synthetic product has its own exposure, nailing pattern and accessory pieces. Warranties depend on following those instructions, so our crews work from the product’s installation guide.

Plan the transitions. Hips, ridges, valleys and walls need matching accessories. These details are where synthetic roofs look finished or look patched.

Underlayment and flashing. The same full-system approach applies: new underlayment, ice and water protection where needed and new flashing at walls, chimneys and penetrations.

Color blending. Many products are installed from several color blends. We mix bundles as the manufacturer recommends so the roof does not show stripes or patches.

Ventilation. Good attic ventilation protects synthetic products just as it does shingles.

Repairs

Can you repair an existing natural slate or wood shake roof?

Sometimes. It depends on the age and condition of the existing roof.

01

Spot repairs

Small leaks on a slate or shake roof can often be repaired by replacing broken pieces and fixing flashing. Finding matching material is the main challenge.

02

Emergency tarping

When a shake roof is leaking after a storm, we can tarp it to protect the home until a permanent fix is planned.

03

When to switch

When a shake or slate roof needs widespread repair, replacing it with synthetic slate or shake can keep the look while reducing maintenance.

A synthetic slate roof should be evaluated as a complete roofing assembly rather than by looking at one visible piece. Homeowners comparing synthetic slate roof tiles should consider roof slope, existing decking, underlayment, flashing details, ventilation, weather exposure, product requirements, and installation method. The material may create a slate-inspired appearance, but its long-term performance also depends on the components beneath and around it.

Synthetic Slate & Shake FAQ

Do insurance companies like slate roofs?
Insurance companies look at several factors, including the roof’s age, condition, material, installation quality, replacement cost, and the weather risks in the area. Some roofing materials may qualify for different underwriting considerations or discounts, but this varies by insurer. The best approach is to check directly with your insurance provider before making a roofing decision.
What is the cheapest roof to put on a house?
Asphalt shingles are usually one of the more affordable roofing choices for homeowners because the material and installation costs are generally lower than many premium roofing systems. The final price still depends on roof size, slope, removal needs, decking condition, and other project details.
How much does synthetic slate cost per square foot?
There is no single price that applies to every home. Cost can vary based on the product selected, roof size, pitch, tear-off requirements, decking condition, flashing, underlayment, labor, and overall project complexity. A property-specific estimate is the most accurate way to understand the expected cost.
What time of year is the cheapest to replace a roof?
There is no guaranteed cheapest time of year because pricing can change with contractor schedules, material availability, weather conditions, and project demand. Homeowners should focus on completing necessary work when conditions are suitable rather than delaying a needed project only to wait for a possible seasonal discount.
What is the longest-lasting roof material?
Natural slate is known for having one of the longest potential service lives among residential roofing materials when it is properly installed and maintained. Other roofing systems can also last for many years, but actual lifespan depends on the product, installation quality, ventilation, maintenance, and local weather exposure.
