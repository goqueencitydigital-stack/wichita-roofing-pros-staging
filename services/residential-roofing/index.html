Choosing a Material

Which roofing materials work best on Wichita homes?

Most homes here use asphalt shingles, but metal and synthetic slate or shake are good fits for some houses and budgets.

01

Architectural asphalt shingles

Architectural shingles are the most common roof in Wichita. They are thicker and more dimensional than older three-tab shingles and come in impact-rated versions. They balance cost, appearance and storm performance for most homes. See asphalt shingles.

02

Metal roofing

Standing seam and exposed-fastener metal roofs last a long time, shed water quickly and reflect summer heat. They cost more up front and need careful detailing at penetrations. See metal roofing.

03

Synthetic slate and shake

Composite slate and shake products give a premium look without the weight of natural slate or the upkeep of wood shake. Many are rated for impact. See synthetic slate and shake.

04

Concrete tile

Concrete tile is heavy and long-lived and appears on some Spanish and Mediterranean-style homes. It needs framing that can carry the weight. See concrete tile.

The Roof System

What parts make up a residential roof besides the shingles?

Shingles are the part you see. The parts underneath decide whether the roof stays dry.

01

Decking

Plywood or OSB sheathing nailed to the rafters. Soft or delaminated decking is replaced so new shingles have a solid base.

02

Underlayment and ice barrier

A secondary water-resistant layer under the shingles, with self-adhering membrane in valleys, at eaves and around penetrations where water concentrates.

03

Flashing

Metal pieces at walls, chimneys, skylights and valleys that direct water away from joints. Most leaks start at flashing, not in the open field of shingles.

04

Ventilation

Intake at the soffits and exhaust near the ridge keep the attic cooler in summer and drier in winter, which protects the shingles and the decking.

05

Drip edge

Metal at the roof edges that keeps water off the fascia and in the gutter.

06

Ridge and hip caps

Special shingles that cap the peaks of the roof and cover the ridge vent.

Roof Life

How long do residential roofs last in south-central Kansas?

Material ratings are a starting point. Weather, ventilation and installation decide the real number.

Hail shortens roof life more than age. Wichita sits in one of the most active hail regions in the country. A single storm can bruise shingles, knock off granules or crack them, even on a roof that is only a few years old.

Heat matters too. Long summer stretches above 100 degrees bake roofs with poor attic ventilation. Shingles over a hot attic often curl and lose granules sooner than shingles over a well-ventilated one.

Wind tests the edges. Strong straight-line winds lift shingles at rakes, ridges and eaves. Shingles that were not fastened or sealed correctly are the first to go.

Installation details add years. Proper nailing, sealed starter strips, ice and water protection in valleys and correct flashing all help a roof reach the life its material is designed for.

The best way to know where your roof stands is a free roof inspection. We tell you whether the roof has years left, needs a repair or is ready for replacement.

What Should Wichita Homeowners Know About Their Roof?

A proper evaluation begins on the roof, not with a quick estimate from the driveway. We inspect the parts of a residential roof that commonly allow water entry, including shingles, valleys, penetrations, flashing, vents, edges, and drainage components. A ceiling stain may originate from a cracked pipe boot, lifted flashing, a nail pop, or a damaged valley several feet from the visible mark. That is why we assess the surrounding system before explaining whether a focused repair should hold or whether widespread deterioration makes replacement the more practical option. When comparing licensed residential roofing providers, homeowners should expect clear findings, documented recommendations, and proof of insurance.

  • We inspect shingles, valleys, pipe boots, flashing, roof edges, gutters, and safely accessible decking conditions.
  • We evaluate residential roof ventilation because trapped heat and moisture can shorten material life and affect attic conditions.
  • Wichita hail, wind, sudden rain, and temperature changes can turn minor wear into an active leak.

What Other Roofing Work Might Need Attention?

A roof concern may involve the covering material, flashing, drainage, ventilation, or another connected exterior component. Our residential roofing materials comparison helps homeowners understand how asphalt shingles, metal panels, and synthetic slate or shake differ in appearance, durability, installation requirements, and long-term value.

  • Asphalt shingles are widely used and offer several styles, impact ratings, and warranty options.
  • Metal systems may provide long service life but require careful attention to seams, fasteners, flashing, and expansion.
  • Synthetic slate and shake can deliver a premium appearance without the weight of natural materials.
  • If a focused repair solves the problem, the recommendation should remain focused on that repair.

What Details Matter Most for a Home Roof?

01

Material Condition

Material age, installation quality, impact damage, roof pitch, and exposure all influence performance. Different residential roof types also have different maintenance needs, repair methods, and expected service lives.
02

Storm Exposure

Hail, straight-line winds, heavy rain, and flying debris can affect shingles, vents, flashing, and gutters during the same weather event. Damage should be evaluated based on actual roof conditions rather than assumptions made from the ground.
03

Flashing, Valleys, and Ventilation

The areas around walls, chimneys, roof edges, valleys, pipe penetrations, and attic airflow frequently determine whether a repair remains watertight. These details require careful inspection and correct installation.
04

Connected Exterior Work

Water spilling behind gutters, reaching siding, or deteriorating fascia may indicate a wider drainage problem. Timely residential roof maintenance can help identify these connections before moisture reaches decking, insulation, or interior finishes.

Common Questions

Residential Roofing FAQ

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900.

When should I schedule a professional roof evaluation?
Schedule an evaluation when you notice active leaks, ceiling stains, missing or lifted materials, loose flashing, storm damage, sagging areas, or repeated problems in the same location. An inspection can determine whether the concern is isolated or part of a wider issue.
Can a damaged roof be repaired instead of replaced?
Yes, many isolated leaks, flashing failures, damaged shingles, and pipe-boot problems can be repaired. Replacement may be more appropriate when damage is widespread, materials are heavily aged, or repairs are unlikely to provide dependable long-term protection.
Does Wichita weather affect roof performance?
Yes. Hail, strong winds, rapid temperature changes, heavy rain, and prolonged heat can loosen materials, reduce granule protection, damage flashing, and accelerate normal wear.
How long does a residential roof last?
Service life depends on the material, installation quality, ventilation, maintenance, storm exposure, and roof design. Asphalt shingles commonly have a shorter expected lifespan than many metal or premium synthetic systems, but actual condition should guide repair and replacement decisions.
How do I schedule an inspection in Wichita?
