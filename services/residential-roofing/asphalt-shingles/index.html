Impact Ratings

What do Class 3 and Class 4 shingle ratings mean?

Impact ratings come from a standard lab test, and the class tells you how large an impact the shingle withstood without cracking.

01

How the test works

Under the UL 2218 test, steel balls of increasing size are dropped onto a shingle sample from set heights. The sample is checked for cracks after each impact. The largest ball the shingle survives sets its class, from Class 1 to Class 4.

02

Class 3

Class 3 shingles passed impacts from a 1.75-inch steel ball. They offer better impact resistance than standard shingles at a moderate cost.

03

Class 4

Class 4 shingles passed impacts from a 2-inch steel ball, the highest rating in the test. Many Kansas homeowners choose Class 4 because of the area’s hail history.

04

What ratings do not promise

A rating does not make a roof hailproof. Real hail varies in size, density and angle. Independent research from IBHS found that impact performance varies between products, even within the same class. See our guide to Class 3 vs. Class 4 shingles.

Installation Details

Which installation details make an asphalt shingle roof last?

The same shingle can last a long time or fail early, depending on how it is installed.

Nailing. Each shingle has a nailing zone that the manufacturer specifies. Nails placed high, low or at an angle reduce wind resistance and can lead to blow-offs. Our crews follow the manufacturer’s nailing pattern for each product.

Starter strips. Starter shingles at the eaves and rakes give the first row of shingles a sealed edge. Missing starter at the rakes is a common reason shingles peel off in high wind.

Ice and water protection. Self-adhering membrane goes in valleys, along eaves and around penetrations where water concentrates or backs up.

Valleys. Valleys carry the most water on the roof. They are lined and shingled carefully, with no nails near the center where water flows.

Flashing. New step flashing at walls, pipe boots at plumbing vents and proper chimney flashing close the joints where most leaks begin.

Ventilation. Balanced intake and exhaust protects shingles from attic heat. We review ventilation during every replacement.

Warning Signs

How can you tell when asphalt shingles are wearing out?

These signs are often visible from the ground or a second-story window.

01

Granules in the gutters

Some granule loss is normal on a new roof. Heavy granules in gutters and at downspouts on an older roof mean the shingles are wearing through their protective layer.

02

Curling and cupping

Shingle edges that turn up or centers that sink often point to age, heat or poor ventilation.

03

Cracks and missing tabs

Cracked or missing pieces leave the underlayment exposed. Wind can lift weakened shingles in the next storm.

04

Dark streaks and moss

Dark streaks are usually algae, which is mostly cosmetic. Moss holds moisture against the roof and should be addressed.

05

Bald spots after hail

Round dark spots where granules were knocked off can mean hail bruising. They are worth an inspection, even if the roof does not leak yet.

06

Nail pops

Nails that back out lift the shingle above them and can create a small leak through the nail hole.

What should Wichita homeowners know about asphalt shingles?

A proper asphalt-shingle evaluation should start with the roof system, not just one damaged tab. We look for granule loss, lifted or brittle material, exposed fasteners, nail pops, impact marks, flashing gaps, damaged ridge details, and signs that water may be reaching the layers beneath the visible surface.

Homeowners comparing the different types of asphalt shingles should also consider roof pitch, exposure, material construction, impact resistance, appearance, ventilation, and the existing roof assembly. Product selection matters, but correct installation and the condition of the supporting system matter just as much.

  • Isolated damage on a newer roof may be limited to a small area.
  • Widespread granule loss, brittleness, recurring leaks, or deterioration across several slopes can indicate a broader condition.
  • A ceiling stain does not always identify the exact entry point because water can travel before becoming visible indoors.
  • Wichita weather exposure should be considered together with roof age, installation quality, and maintenance history.

Homeowners researching asphalt fiberglass shingles should understand that the visible shingle is only one layer of the roofing assembly. Underlying protection, flashing, ventilation, ridge details, and drainage all contribute to long-term performance.

What Other Roofing Work Might Need Attention?

Asphalt-shingle concerns can overlap with other parts of the exterior. A leak may begin at flashing, a wind event may affect vents or gutters, or aging materials may expose weaknesses around valleys and roof penetrations.

If the condition extends beyond the shingle system, homeowners can review our residential roofing, roof repair, and roof replacement pages for the appropriate next step.

  • Related services are used to clarify the correct scope rather than automatically expand a project.
  • Gutters, fascia, siding, flashing, and roof ventilation may need attention when they affect water movement.
  • A focused correction should remain focused when the surrounding roofing system is still performing properly.
  • Wider deterioration should be explained before a larger project is recommended.

For homeowners comparing architectural asphalt shingle options, the goal should be to match the product to the roof and Wichita exposure rather than selecting a material based only on appearance.

Asphalt Shingles FAQ

When should I have an asphalt-shingle roof checked?
Schedule an evaluation when you notice missing or lifted shingles, unusual granule loss, ceiling stains, loose flashing, exposed fasteners, storm-related changes, or recurring moisture problems. Early evaluation can help identify whether the concern is isolated or connected to a larger roof condition.
What is the average life of asphalt shingle roof systems?
The average life of asphalt shingle roof materials depends on product type, installation quality, ventilation, roof pitch, maintenance, sun exposure, and severe weather. Because Wichita roofs can experience hail, wind, heat, and rapid weather changes, actual condition is more useful than age alone when deciding what the roof needs.
Which asphalt shingle is best for my home?
The asphalt shingle depends on the property, roof design, weather exposure, appearance preferences, budget, manufacturer specifications, and installation requirements. A product that makes sense for one Wichita home may not be the best choice for another.
Are impact-resistant shingles worth considering in Wichita?
They can be worth evaluating because hail is an important local exposure. Product testing, manufacturer specifications, warranty language, cost, appearance, and the complete roof system should all be compared before selecting an impact-rated option.
How should I choose an asphalt-shingle contractor?
When comparing asphalt shingles contractors, look for clear inspection findings, defined scope, product information, installation details, and explanations of related roof conditions. The recommendation should be based on what is actually found on the property.
