Do You Need a Permit to Replace a Roof in Wichita?

Published 2026-11-20By the Wichita Roofing Pros teamHomeowner Guide
In this guideWho issues roofing permits?What does a roofing permit cost?How does Kansas roofing registration fit in?Frequently asked questions

Quick answerRoofing permits in Wichita and unincorporated Sedgwick County are handled by the Metropolitan Area Building and Construction Department (MABCD). MABCD publishes a roofing permit fee of $0.05 per square foot, with a $50 minimum and a $1,500 maximum. Ask your contractor to confirm in writing who pulls the permit.

Who issues roofing permits?

MABCD is the joint Wichita–Sedgwick County building department, and roofing is one of the trade permits it issues online through the MABCD portal or in person. Cities outside its jurisdiction have their own offices.

What does a roofing permit cost?

MABCD’s published roofing fee is $0.05 per square foot (minimum $50, maximum $1,500).

MABCD roofing permit fee examples
Roof surfaceCalculated fee
1,000 sq ft$50 (minimum)
2,500 sq ft$125
4,000 sq ft$200
30,000+ sq ft$1,500 (maximum)

Fee examples calculated from the published MABCD rate. Confirm current fees with MABCD before relying on them.

How does Kansas roofing registration fit in?

Separately from permits, every roofing contractor providing roofing services for a fee in Kansas must hold a registration certificate from the Kansas Attorney General. You can check a contractor’s status in the Attorney General’s directory.

Frequently asked questions

Does a roof repair need a permit?

Small repairs may not, while replacements usually do. Rules depend on the jurisdiction and scope, so confirm with MABCD or your city.

Who is responsible for pulling the permit?

Typically the contractor. Make sure the written estimate says so.

Why It Matters

Why does a roofing permit matter to a homeowner?

A permit is not just paperwork. It creates a record that the work was done and, where an inspection is required, that it was checked against local code. That record can help when you sell the home, file an insurance claim or answer a buyer’s questions.

Unpermitted work can create problems later: a buyer’s inspector may ask about it, an insurer may ask about it, and the city can require corrections. Ask your contractor to confirm in writing who pulls the permit and whether the fee is included in the estimate.

Codes also set minimum roofing details, like underlayment and edge requirements. The International Residential Code is the base for many local roofing rules; local amendments can add to it.

Registration and Permits

How are Kansas contractor registration and local permits different?

These are two separate requirements. The Kansas Attorney General registers roofing contractors statewide under the Kansas Roofing Registration Act, including proof of liability insurance. Local permit offices like MABCD issue permits for individual jobs and may have their own contractor licensing rules.

A contractor should meet both. You can check state registration in the Attorney General’s directory and ask the contractor about local licensing. Read How to choose a roofing contractor in Kansas for the full checklist.

Insurance and Resale

How do permits help with insurance claims and selling your home?

When you file a future claim, an insurer may ask how old the roof is. A permit record and dated invoice give a clear answer. When you sell, the buyer’s agent or inspector may ask the same question, and some buyers check permit history.

Keep your permit number with your contract, invoice and warranty paperwork. If you are planning to sell, our guide Does a transferable roof warranty help when selling? explains what paperwork buyers usually ask for.

02

Address, not ZIP code, decides the jurisdiction. Some addresses near city lines are in a different jurisdiction.

03

Starting before approval

Work should start after the permit is issued.

04

Losing the record

Save the permit number and any inspection result with your roof paperwork.

Code Basics

Which code requirements affect a roof replacement?

Local codes are based on national model codes, with local amendments. For residential roofs, the International Residential Code (IRC) sets minimum requirements for roof coverings, including underlayment, ice barrier areas in cold climates, drip edge, flashing and the number of roof layers allowed. Wichita and Sedgwick County use the Unified Building and Trade Code, which adopts and amends national codes for local use.

What this means for you:

  • There are limits on how many layers of roofing can be on a roof, which affects whether a tear-off is required
  • Damaged decking needs to be replaced so the new roof has a solid base
  • Flashing at walls, chimneys and valleys must be installed correctly
  • Manufacturers’ installation instructions also apply, and following them protects your warranty

We build every roof to meet local code and the manufacturer’s instructions, so you do not have to sort through codebooks yourself.

Commercial

Do commercial roofs need permits too?

Yes. Commercial reroofing generally requires permits as well, and commercial projects can involve more review, especially if insulation, drainage or rooftop equipment changes. Building owners should confirm who pulls the permit and whether inspections apply. Our commercial roofing team handles permit coordination as part of the project scope.
