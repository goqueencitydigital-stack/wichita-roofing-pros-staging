How Do You Read a Written Roofing Estimate?

Published 2026-11-20By the Wichita Roofing Pros teamHomeowner Guide
In this guideWhat should every roofing estimate include?What red flags should you watch for?How do you verify a Kansas roofing contractor?How do you compare two estimates fairly?Frequently asked questions

Quick answerA good written roofing estimate lists the measured roof size, what is being torn off, the exact materials, flashing and ventilation work, how decking repairs are priced, cleanup, permits and the warranty. Compare estimates line by line instead of by total price. If something is not written down, assume it is not included.

What should every roofing estimate include?

The estimate should describe the job clearly enough that two contractors could price the same work. These are the lines to look for.

Written roofing estimate checklist
Line itemWhat it should sayWhy it matters
Roof sizeMeasured squares, including wasteSets the base price
Tear-offNumber of layers removedExtra layers add labor and disposal
DeckingInspection plus a per-sheet price for replacementsRot is often hidden until tear-off
UnderlaymentType of underlayment and ice/water protection areasProtects the deck behind the shingles
ShinglesManufacturer, product line and colorPrevents a cheaper substitute
Flashing & bootsReplace or reuse, by locationMost leaks start at details
VentilationRidge, box or turbine vents and intakeAffects shingle life and attic heat
CleanupMagnet sweep, debris haul-offProtects tires, lawns and pets
PermitWho pulls it and whether the fee is includedLocal code requirement
WarrantyManufacturer tier and workmanship warrantyWho stands behind what
PaymentDeposit, schedule and accepted methodsAvoid paying in full upfront

What red flags should you watch for?

The Federal Trade Commission warns homeowners about contractors who pressure for quick decisions or full payment upfront. Storm season brings out-of-town crews, so slow down before signing.

  • Large cash-only deposits or full payment before work begins
  • No physical address or Kansas roofing registration
  • Vague “replace roof” scope with no materials listed
  • Offers to “cover” your deductible
  • Pressure to sign the same day

How do you verify a Kansas roofing contractor?

Every roofing contractor providing residential or commercial roofing for a fee in Kansas must hold a registration certificate from the Kansas Attorney General. You can look up a contractor in the Attorney General’s roofing registration directory.

Kansas law also requires registered roofers to file proof of liability insurance of not less than $500,000 (K.S.A. 50-6,125).

How do you compare two estimates fairly?

Put the estimates side by side and match each line. Differences in squares, decking allowances, materials and warranty usually explain most of the price gap.

  1. Match roof size. If one says 24 squares and another 30, ask how each was measured.
  2. Match materials. Same manufacturer, line and impact rating?
  3. Match details. Flashing, boots, ventilation, drip edge.
  4. Match warranty. Manufacturer tier plus workmanship coverage.
  5. Then compare total. Only now is price an apples-to-apples comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Should a roofing estimate be free?

Yes. Wichita Roofing Pros provides free inspections and written estimates with no obligation.

How long is a roofing estimate valid?

Material prices change, so estimates usually carry an expiration date. Ask what it is and what happens if the job is scheduled after it.

What if the crew finds rotted decking?

A good estimate explains how decking is priced before work starts, so you are not surprised. Decking condition can only be fully confirmed after tear-off.
