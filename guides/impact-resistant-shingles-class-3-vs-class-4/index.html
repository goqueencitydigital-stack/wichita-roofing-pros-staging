Class 3 or Class 4: Are Impact-Resistant Shingles Worth It in Wichita?

Published 2026-11-20By the Wichita Roofing Pros teamHomeowner Guide
In this guideHow are impact ratings tested?What can impact-resistant shingles not do?Do impact-rated shingles lower insurance premiums?Frequently asked questions

Quick answerImpact-resistant shingles are rated by dropping steel balls on them; Class 4 passes the largest (2-inch) ball and Class 3 the next size down. They reduce damage from small and medium hail but are not hail-proof. Some insurers offer premium credits for impact-rated roofs, so ask your agent what applies to your policy.

How are impact ratings tested?

Under the UL 2218 standard, steel balls from 1.25 to 2 inches are dropped on the shingle. Class 3 withstands a 1.75-inch ball and Class 4 a 2-inch ball without cracking.

UL 2218 impact classes
RatingSteel ball sizeRoughly like hail size of
Class 11.25 in.Half dollar
Class 21.5 in.Walnut / ping pong ball
Class 31.75 in.Golf ball
Class 42 in.Hen egg

Hail size comparisons from the National Weather Service hail size reference.

What can impact-resistant shingles not do?

Very large hail can still damage any roof, and real hail is often harder and faster than lab tests. As our walkthrough video puts it, with 2-inch hail you can see damage on Class 3 or Class 4.

Do impact-rated shingles lower insurance premiums?

Some Kansas insurers offer discounts for impact-rated roofs, but the amount depends on your insurer and policy. Ask your agent before choosing, and we can provide the product documentation they request.

Wichita Hail

Why do impact ratings matter so much in Wichita?

South-central Kansas sees frequent severe thunderstorms in spring and early summer, and hail is one of the main reasons Wichita homeowners replace roofs. The National Weather Service office in Wichita keeps records of major local hail events, including the April 24, 2006 hailstorm that damaged roofs across the city.

Impact-rated shingles do not stop hail, but they are made to resist cracking from hailstones up to the size they were tested for. For homeowners who have filed more than one hail claim, that can mean fewer claims, fewer repairs and fewer roof replacements over time.

Side by Side

How do Class 3 and Class 4 shingles compare for a homeowner?

Both are a step up from standard shingles. The differences are mainly the test level, insurance treatment and price.

01

Test level

Class 4 passes the largest steel ball in the UL 2218 test (2 inches). Class 3 passes a 1.75-inch ball.

02

Insurance credits

Some insurers offer premium credits for Class 4 roofs. Credits for Class 3 are less common. Ask your agent before choosing.

03

Availability

Class 4 versions are available from major manufacturers in many colors. Class 3 options are less common on the market.

04

Price

Impact-rated shingles cost more than standard shingles. The written estimate shows the difference for your roof.

Is It Worth It

When are Class 4 shingles worth the extra cost?

Class 4 shingles tend to make the most sense when:

  • You plan to stay in the home for many years
  • Your home has a history of hail claims
  • Your insurer offers a meaningful premium credit
  • You want fewer interruptions from storm repairs

They may matter less if you plan to sell soon, if your insurer offers no credit, or if budget is the main concern. In that case, a quality architectural shingle installed correctly, with good flashing and edge details, is still a strong roof.

Installation still matters. An impact-rated shingle that is not nailed and sealed correctly can still blow off in wind. Our crews follow the manufacturer’s installation instructions, which also protects your manufacturer warranty. Compare options on our asphalt shingles page or in the materials comparison.

After a Storm

What should you do if hail hits an impact-rated roof?

Impact-rated does not mean inspection-free.

01

Look from the ground

Check gutters, downspouts, window screens and vents for dents. Soft metals show hail size quickly.

02

Document the storm

Write down the date, take photos of hail on the ground and any visible damage.

03

Schedule an inspection

A free inspection confirms whether the shingles were damaged or only the soft metals.

04

Talk to your insurer

If there is damage, start the claim with your insurer. Our storm and hail resource center explains the steps.

The Test

How does the UL 2218 test actually work?

UL 2218 is a laboratory test. A sample of the roofing product is installed on a test deck, and steel balls are dropped from set heights onto the same spots more than once. After each drop, the material is checked for cracks or tears on the top surface and the back side. A product earns the highest class it passes without visible damage.

Steel balls are harder than real hailstones, and they fall straight down. Real hail can be softer or harder, hit at an angle in wind and strike a roof that has aged in the sun for years. That is why research groups like the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety (IBHS) have also tested shingles with ice-based hail to compare how products perform in conditions closer to real storms.

The takeaway for homeowners: the rating is a useful way to compare products, but installation, roof age and storm conditions all affect how a roof performs in a real hailstorm.

Beyond Shingles

Which other parts of the roof matter for hail?

01

Vents and roof jacks

Soft metal vents dent easily and can crack around fasteners. Hail-resistant vent options exist.

02

Gutters and downspouts

Dented gutters can still drain, but crushed downspouts block water. See our gutter services.

03

Skylights

Skylight lenses can crack in hail. Impact-rated skylights are available.

04

Siding and trim

Hail often damages siding on the storm-facing side. Our siding and trim team can repair or replace it.

Choosing a Product

How do you choose an impact-resistant shingle?

Start with the products your installer is certified to install with system warranties. As a TAMKO Pro Platinum contractor and CertainTeed ShingleMaster, we can walk you through impact-rated lines from those manufacturers and what their warranties cover.

Next, look at color and profile options, and check any HOA rules. Then compare the written price difference against your insurer’s premium credit, if one is offered. Many homeowners find the decision easier once they see both numbers side by side in writing.

Ready to compare? Book a free roof inspection and ask for both a standard and an impact-rated option on your estimate.

Insurance

What should you ask your insurance agent about impact-rated roofs?

Before you choose, call your agent and ask:

  • Do you offer a premium credit for a Class 4 roof, and how much is it?
  • What documentation do you need, such as the invoice, product name or a certificate?
  • Does the credit apply to the whole policy or only the wind and hail portion?
  • Does my policy pay replacement cost or actual cash value for the roof?
  • Does my policy treat cosmetic hail damage differently from functional damage?

Write down the answers. Then compare the credit to the written price difference between standard and impact-rated shingles. The Kansas Insurance Department also publishes consumer information about storm coverage.

Myths

What are common myths about impact-resistant shingles?

01

“They are hail-proof.”

No roof is hail-proof. Impact-rated shingles resist cracking from hail up to the tested size.

02

“They always lower insurance.”

Only some insurers offer credits. Ask your agent.

03

“They look plain.”

Most impact-rated architectural shingles look like other architectural shingles and come in many colors.

04

“Any roofer can install them the same way.”

Installation still matters. Manufacturer instructions must be followed to protect performance and warranty.

Long Term

How do impact-resistant shingles age over time?

Like any shingle, impact-rated products age with sun, heat and weather. Over the years, any asphalt shingle can become less flexible. Good attic ventilation, regular inspections and prompt repairs after storms help any roof perform as long as possible. Ask about the manufacturer warranty for the specific product and how it treats hail.

More Answers

More impact-rating questions

Still have a question? Call (316) 742-1900. Hablamos Español.

What does UL 2218 mean?

UL 2218 is the standard impact test for roofing. Steel balls of increasing size are dropped on the product, and the rating shows the largest ball it withstood without cracking.

Will Class 4 shingles lower my insurance?

Some insurers offer premium credits for Class 4 roofs. It depends on your company and policy, so ask your agent before you choose.

Do Class 4 shingles look different?

No. Most Class 4 architectural shingles look like standard architectural shingles and come in similar colors.

Can hail still damage Class 4 shingles?

Yes. Very large or wind-driven hail can still damage any roof. Class 4 shingles reduce damage from small and medium hail.

Can I upgrade to impact-resistant shingles on an insurance claim?

Often you can pay the difference to upgrade. Ask your insurer how upgrades are handled under your policy.

Are metal roofs Class 4?

Many metal roofing products carry a Class 4 rating. Metal can still dent cosmetically in hail, so ask how your insurer treats cosmetic damage.

Do impact-resistant shingles cost much more?

They cost more than standard shingles. Your written estimate can show both options so you can see the exact difference for your roof.

Can impact-resistant shingles be repaired?

Yes. Damaged shingles can be replaced with matching product, like any other shingle.
