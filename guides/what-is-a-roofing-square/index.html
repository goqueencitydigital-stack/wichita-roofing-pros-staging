What Is a Roofing Square?

Published 2026-11-20By the Wichita Roofing Pros teamHomeowner Guide
In this guideHow do you estimate squares from your footprint?Why do measured squares differ from my estimate?How do squares turn into a price?Frequently asked questions

Quick answerA roofing square is 100 square feet of roof surface. Roofers measure and price roofs in squares, so a roof with 2,500 square feet of surface is 25 squares. Because pitch and overhangs add area, your roof is usually larger than your home’s footprint.

How do you estimate squares from your footprint?

Multiply the home’s footprint (including the garage) by a pitch multiplier, then divide by 100. For an 1,800 square-foot footprint with a medium 6/12 pitch: 1,800 × 1.118 ÷ 100 ≈ 20.1 squares.

Why do measured squares differ from my estimate?

Overhangs, dormers, valleys and cut-up roof planes add surface and waste. During the free inspection we measure the actual roof, which is why calculator results are a planning range.

How do squares turn into a price?

Multiply squares by the per-square price. At our basic planning price of $505 per square, 20 squares is about $10,100.

Frequently asked questions

How many bundles are in a square?

Most architectural shingles come three bundles to a square, but it varies by product.

Is a square the same as square feet of my house?

No. A square is roof surface, not living space.

Measuring

How do roofers measure your roof?

There are a few accurate ways to measure, and they are often used together.

01

On-roof measurement

Measuring each roof plane by hand, when it is safe to walk the roof.

02

Aerial or satellite reports

Measurement reports built from imagery that calculate area and pitch for each plane.

03

Pitch gauge

A tool or app that reads the roof slope to apply the right multiplier.

04

Footprint estimate

A planning method using the home’s footprint and pitch, like our calculator. Useful for budgeting, not for ordering material.

Pitch

How do you find your roof’s pitch?

Roof pitch is written as rise over run: a 6/12 roof rises 6 inches for every 12 inches of horizontal distance. You can estimate it from the ground by comparing the roof to common shapes:

  • Low (around 3/12 to 4/12): a gentle slope, common on ranch homes
  • Medium (around 5/12 to 7/12): the most common slope on Wichita homes
  • Steep (around 8/12 to 10/12): you would not want to walk on it
  • Very steep (12/12 or more): a 45-degree angle or steeper

Steeper roofs have more surface area for the same footprint, which is why the multiplier increases. They also take more time and safety equipment to install.

Material and Waste

What is waste, and why is it added to roof squares?

Waste is the extra material needed for cuts at valleys, hips, rakes and around chimneys and skylights. A simple gable roof needs less waste than a roof with many angles. Waste is added to the measured squares so the crew does not run short mid-job.

Ridge caps, starter shingles and hip shingles are often listed separately. Your estimate should show the measured roof size and how waste is handled. Read How to read a written roofing estimate for the full list of line items.

Worked Examples

How do you calculate roofing squares step by step?

Here are three worked examples using the pitch multipliers in the table above.

Example 1: Ranch home, low pitch. Footprint including garage: 2,000 sq ft. Pitch: about 4/12, multiplier 1.054. Roof surface: 2,000 × 1.054 = 2,108 sq ft. Squares: 2,108 ÷ 100 ≈ 21.1 squares. At $505 per square: about $10,650.

Example 2: Two-story home, medium pitch. Living space: 2,600 sq ft over two floors, so the footprint is about 1,500 sq ft including the garage. Pitch: about 6/12, multiplier 1.118. Roof surface: 1,500 × 1.118 = 1,677 sq ft. Squares: about 16.8. At $505 per square: about $8,480.

Example 3: Steep, complex roof. Footprint: 2,400 sq ft. Pitch: about 9/12, multiplier 1.25. Roof surface: 2,400 × 1.25 = 3,000 sq ft, or 30 squares. At $505 per square: about $15,150, before any extra flashing, decking or material upgrades.

Notice how the two-story home has fewer squares than the ranch, even with more living space. Roof size follows the footprint and pitch, not the square footage of the house.

Terms

What other roof measurement terms will you see?

01

Facet or plane

Each flat section of the roof. More facets usually mean more cuts and waste.

02

Eave

The lower, horizontal edge of the roof where gutters attach.

03

Rake

The sloped edge at the end of a gable.

04

Ridge and hip

The peak where two planes meet at the top (ridge) or at an outside corner (hip).

05

Valley

The inside angle where two roof planes meet and water collects.

06

Bundle

A package of shingles. Many architectural shingles come about three bundles to a square.

Common Mistakes

What mistakes do people make when estimating roof size?

The most common mistakes are using living space instead of footprint, forgetting the garage or porch roofs, and assuming a flat roof multiplier for a sloped roof. Another is comparing two estimates that measured the roof differently. If one bid lists 22 squares and another lists 28 for the same house, ask each contractor how they measured.

Our roofing glossary explains other terms you may see, and the roof replacement cost guide shows what changes the final price.

Beyond Shingles

How are squares used for metal and flat roofs?

Squares are used across nearly every roofing material. Metal panels are ordered by length and coverage width, but the job is still measured and priced by roof area. Low-slope commercial membranes like TPO and modified bitumen are measured by square too, and insulation, edge metal and flashing are listed separately.

On commercial buildings, you may also see measurements in total square feet instead of squares. The idea is the same: 100 square feet equals one square. Read TPO vs. modified bitumen for more on low-slope roofs.

Bundles

How do squares translate into bundles and materials?

Shingles are sold in bundles, and many architectural shingles cover one square with about three bundles. Some heavier or premium products use more bundles per square. Other materials are ordered by the roll or piece:

  • Underlayment comes in rolls that cover a set number of squares
  • Ice and water protection is ordered by the linear foot of eaves and valleys
  • Starter strip and ridge cap are ordered by linear foot of edges, ridges and hips
  • Drip edge is ordered in pieces by the length of eaves and rakes
  • Vents and pipe boots are counted individually

That is why a good estimate lists more than squares. Each of these pieces affects how well the roof keeps water out.

More Answers

More measuring questions

How many squares is a typical Wichita roof?

Many single-family homes fall somewhere around 15 to 35 squares, depending on footprint, pitch and roof shape.

Does the garage count toward roof squares?

Yes. If the garage is under the same roof, its roof area is included.

Why does my estimate list more squares than the calculator?

Measured squares include every plane, overhang and waste factor, which a footprint estimate cannot capture exactly.

Are roofing squares used for metal and flat roofs too?

Yes. Squares are used across roofing materials, including metal and low-slope membranes.

What does $505 per square mean for my home?

It is our basic planning price. Multiply your roof squares by $505 for a planning number, then confirm the final price in writing after inspection.

Can I measure my own roof?

You can estimate it from the ground using the footprint and pitch method. For ordering materials and pricing, a measured inspection is more accurate and safer than climbing the roof yourself.

Is a roofing square the same for every roofer?

Yes. A square is always 100 square feet of roof surface. Differences between bids come from how the roof was measured and how waste is added.

How much does one square of roofing cost?

Our basic planning price for roof replacement is $505 per square. Your final price is confirmed in writing after a free inspection.

How many squares can a crew install in a day?

It depends on crew size, pitch, roof complexity and weather. Many average homes can be completed in one to a few days.

What is the difference between a square and a roofing bundle?

A square is a measurement of roof area (100 square feet). A bundle is a package of shingles; several bundles usually cover one square.

Why do porch and garage roofs matter for roof size?

Every roof plane counts toward total squares, including garage, porch and addition roofs. Leaving them out is one of the most common reasons an estimate looks lower than the real job.

Does roof pitch change the price per square?

Steeper roofs take more time and safety equipment, so very steep roofs can cost more to install. Your written estimate shows how pitch affects your price.

Is a bigger roof always more expensive?

Usually, since price follows squares. A smaller roof with many valleys, dormers or steep planes can still cost more per square than a larger, simple roof.
