What You Get Right After a Free Roof Inspection

A typical inspection takes around 30–60 minutes, depending on the size and condition of the roof. When access is safe, the inspector photographs what they find. Those photos are the starting point for the conversation that follows.

  • Photos of any damaged or worn areas
  • A plain-English explanation of each finding
  • Notes on flashing, vents, pipe boots and valleys
  • Gutter, soffit and fascia observations
  • Signs of hail or wind damage, if any
  • A recommended next step

In many cases the exterior inspection can be done without you at home, and the results are discussed afterward. For a full list of what is checked, see what a free roof inspection includes.

How Roof Inspection Findings Are Explained

Good findings are sorted by urgency, not just listed. We group what we see into three simple buckets so you can decide what to do and when.

How inspection findings are grouped
GroupWhat it usually meansTypical next step
Fix nowActive leaks, missing shingles, open flashingRepair or temporary protection soon
Plan forWear that will become a problemSchedule a repair or budget for replacement
MonitorNormal aging with no active damageCheck again after storms or next year

Repair, Replacement or No Work at All

If the problem is limited to one area and the rest of the roof is sound, a repair is often the right call. Common repairs include replacing a pipe boot, resealing or replacing flashing, or swapping out a few damaged shingles. If damage is spread across several roof planes, the shingles are near the end of their life, or the decking is soft, replacement may make more sense.

We explain both options and why one fits better, without turning one roof concern into a bigger project than it needs to be. If you are weighing the two, our guide on whether to repair or replace your roof in Wichita walks through the factors. For repairs, see our roof repair services; for a full new roof, see roof replacement in Wichita.

When a Free Roof Inspection Finds Storm Damage

Hail and wind are common in Wichita, so many inspections turn up storm damage the homeowner did not know about. When that happens, the order of steps matters. Damage should be documented before anything is repaired so your insurer can see it as it was.

A regular inspection looks at overall roof condition, while an insurance-focused inspection documents storm damage in a way that supports a claim conversation. Your insurer’s adjuster makes the coverage decision. You can read how they differ in what makes a roof insurance inspection different, and see our storm and hail damage repair and insurance claim roof replacement pages for next steps.

Getting the Scope and Price in Writing

If work is recommended, you get the scope and price in writing before anything starts. A clear written scope lists the roof size, what is being removed, materials, flashing and ventilation work, cleanup and warranty. That makes it easy to understand what you are paying for and to compare it with any other estimate.

For replacements, our basic price is $505 per roofing square, confirmed in writing after the inspection. Financing is available through Wisetack for qualifying projects, subject to credit approval; see roof financing options for the current terms.

No Pressure: What a Free Inspection Should Not Include

There is no obligation to buy anything after a free inspection. You can take the photos and written findings, think it over, and call back when you are ready. Be cautious of anyone who asks you to sign on the spot, offers to cover your deductible or asks for full payment upfront; the FTC guidance on home improvement scams lists these as warning signs.

How to Prepare for Your Free Roof Inspection

You do not need to do much before a free roof inspection, but a little preparation makes the visit more useful and the results easier to act on.

  • Write down any leaks, stains, drafts or noises you have noticed, and roughly when they started.
  • Note recent storms, especially hail or high wind, and the dates if you know them.
  • Gather any paperwork you have on the roof, such as past repair invoices or warranty documents.
  • Make sure the inspector can reach the attic access if you want the underside of the decking checked.
  • Move vehicles or items that block the driveway or the side of the house where a ladder will go.

You are welcome to be home and walk around the house with the inspector, but it is not required. Either way, you will receive photos and an explanation of what was found. Having your notes ready helps connect what you have seen inside the home with what the inspector finds on the roof, which leads to a clearer recommendation.

Booking Your Next Roof Inspection

Once a year and after any major hail or wind storm is a practical inspection schedule for Wichita homes, and our warranty maintenance terms also call for an annual inspection. Late fall is a good time to book one, before freeze-thaw weather and winter wind find weak spots. See how often to schedule a roof inspection in Wichita for more on timing.

Your Report

What does the inspection report include?

After the inspection, you receive a clear picture of your roof’s condition:

  • Photos of the roof surface, flashing, vents, gutters and any damage found
  • A plain-English explanation of what each photo shows
  • The approximate roof size and pitch
  • Whether the roof needs no work, a repair or a replacement, and why
  • For storm damage, documentation you can share with your insurer
  • If work is needed, a written scope and price

Keep the report with your home records. It is useful for insurance, maintenance planning and selling your home later.

Decision Time

How long do you have to decide after the inspection?

There is no deadline from us. You can take the time you need to compare estimates, talk with family or check with your insurance company. If there is an active leak or storm damage that could get worse, we will tell you and explain the risk of waiting. For insurance claims, your policy may have its own deadlines, so check with your agent.

Scheduling

How soon can work start after you approve the estimate?

Once you approve the written estimate, the office schedules your project and orders materials. Timing depends on the season, weather and material availability. Repairs are often scheduled sooner than full replacements. If permits are needed, we apply for them and build that time into the schedule. You get a confirmed date and a call ahead of the crew’s arrival.
