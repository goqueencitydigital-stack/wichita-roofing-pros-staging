What Makes Metal Roofing Different From Asphalt in Wichita?

Metal roofing and asphalt shingles differ in four core areas: lifespan, weight, installation method, and performance under hail. A standard three-tab or architectural asphalt shingle roof in Wichita typically lasts 20 to 30 years depending on installation quality and storm history. A properly installed metal roof carries a manufacturer lifespan of 40 to 70 years for standing seam systems, and 30 to 45 years for exposed-fastener steel panels.

Weight is another contrast. Metal roofing generally runs 1 to 3 pounds per square foot, compared to 2 to 4 pounds per square foot for architectural asphalt shingles. That lighter weight matters on older Wichita homes where adding a second layer of shingles is already at the structural limit. Metal is often a viable option when a re-roof would otherwise require a full tear-off due to weight concerns.

From a performance standpoint, metal roofing handles high-wind events well. Most metal roofing systems are rated for sustained winds above 120 mph, which is relevant for a region that sees supercell thunderstorms, straight-line wind events, and occasional tornado activity. IBHS hail and high-wind roof research has documented that metal roofing systems with concealed fasteners perform well in both hail and high-wind scenarios when properly installed.

What Are the Main Metal Roofing Panel Types for Wichita Homes?

Three panel configurations dominate the residential metal roofing market in Wichita: standing seam, exposed-fastener steel, and steel shingles. Each has distinct advantages and trade-offs.

Standing seam panels interlock at raised seams, hiding all fasteners beneath the metal surface. Because no fasteners are exposed to the elements, there are no penetrations that can back out, corrode, or allow water entry. This system costs more, typically 30 to 50 percent above exposed-fastener steel per square installed, but it carries the longest warranties and the best long-term watertight performance. It is the preferred choice for Wichita homeowners who plan to stay in the home long-term or who want a product that performs through multiple severe storm seasons.

Exposed-fastener corrugated or ribbed steel panels are the more affordable metal option. Fasteners are driven directly through the panel into the decking with rubber-gasketed screws. Over time those gaskets compress, degrade, or can back out slightly, creating potential leak points. Annual inspection of fasteners is advisable. These systems are common on outbuildings and some residential applications where budget is the primary driver.

Steel shingles replicate the appearance of asphalt shingles, cedar shake, or slate while delivering the durability of metal. They are a middle-ground option that blends into neighborhood aesthetics more easily than corrugated panels while still offering 40-plus-year service life. Many Wichita homeowners in traditional neighborhoods choose steel shingles specifically because HOA guidelines or aesthetic preferences require a shingle profile.

Does Metal Roofing Actually Hold Up to Wichita Hailstorms?

Hail resistance is one of the most-cited reasons Wichita homeowners consider metal roofing, and the answer is nuanced. Metal roofing rated Class 4 impact resistance under UL 2218 is the highest available rating and is tested by dropping 2-inch steel balls onto panels at simulated hail velocities. Most standing seam and steel shingle products from established manufacturers achieve Class 4 ratings, meaning they resist functional damage from large hail stones that would crack or puncture asphalt shingles.

However, “functionally undamaged” does not mean “cosmetically undamaged.” Hail stones 1.5 inches or larger can leave visible dents in softer aluminum and steel panels. The roof continues to perform, but the appearance changes. Harder steel alloys and thicker gauges reduce visible denting. If cosmetic damage is a concern, gauge selection and panel material matter. Twenty-four-gauge steel resists denting better than 26-gauge, and Galvalume-coated steel holds up better than bare aluminum in the Wichita climate.

From an insurance standpoint, Class 4 impact-resistant roofing may qualify for a discount on homeowner’s insurance premiums in Kansas. Some carriers reduce premiums by 10 to 30 percent for verified Class 4 roofing products. Before purchasing metal roofing, confirm with your insurer whether they recognize the specific product and what documentation they require. The Kansas Department of Insurance storm claim data shows that hail claims continue to be the leading driver of residential roofing replacements across south-central Kansas, which makes this insurance angle particularly relevant for Wichita homeowners.

Top 5 Reasons Wichita Homeowners Choose Metal Roofing

  1. Longevity beyond two or three asphalt roofing cycles. A standing seam metal roof installed today may outlast two or three asphalt replacements, potentially eliminating one or two future $15,000 to $25,000 roofing projects over a 50-year ownership period.
  2. Class 4 hail impact rating and insurance premium reductions. Verified Class 4 products reduce the likelihood of functional hail damage and may lower annual insurance costs, offsetting some of the higher upfront price.
  3. Wind resistance rated above 120 mph. Standing seam systems handle straight-line wind events better than asphalt, where shingles can lift at lower wind speeds from compromised sealant strips.
  4. Energy efficiency from reflective coatings. Light-colored or Kynar-coated metal panels reflect solar heat more effectively than dark asphalt, which can reduce attic temperatures and cooling loads during Wichita’s hot July and August months.
  5. Low maintenance profile. Metal roofing does not granule-shed, crack from UV, or require periodic sealing the way tile and modified bitumen surfaces do. Inspection is still advisable after major hail events, but routine maintenance needs are minimal.

Is Metal Roofing Loud During Rain and Hail in Wichita?

The noise concern is one of the most persistent myths about metal roofing in Wichita, and it is worth addressing directly. Noise level on a metal roof depends almost entirely on the installation substrate and insulation beneath the panels. Metal roofing installed over solid OSB or plywood decking with a layer of underlayment, or over an existing insulated attic assembly, is not meaningfully louder than asphalt shingles during rain. The hollow, drumming sound associated with metal roofs comes from open-frame installations on agricultural buildings where there is no solid sheathing to dampen vibration.

Residential metal roofing over solid decking with proper underlayment performs within normal ambient noise levels during rain events. During a heavy hailstorm with large stones, any roof type makes noise, and metal is louder than asphalt in that specific scenario. But for 99 percent of the rain events Wichita receives throughout the year, a well-installed residential metal roof is not noticeably louder than asphalt inside the home.

What Does Metal Roofing Cost in Wichita Compared to Asphalt?

Metal roofing in Wichita carries a higher installed cost than asphalt shingles. Architectural asphalt shingle replacement on a typical 1,500 to 2,000 square foot Wichita home generally runs in the range of $8,000 to $15,000 depending on pitch, complexity, and decking condition. Exposed-fastener steel panels run roughly 20 to 40 percent more. Standing seam metal roofing on the same home typically runs from $18,000 to $35,000 or more, again depending on complexity, panel profile, and coating selection.

The cost comparison only tells part of the story. When you factor in that a standing seam metal roof may last 50 or more years compared to 20 to 25 years for architectural asphalt shingles, and that Class 4 products may reduce insurance premiums, the per-year cost of ownership narrows considerably. For homeowners who plan to stay in their Wichita home for 15 years or more, metal roofing often compares favorably on a lifetime-cost basis. For those planning to sell within five years, the upfront premium is harder to recoup through sale price alone, though a new metal roof can be a positive factor in negotiations.

Choosing a roof material for Wichita weather

Hail resistance, wind ratings and summer heat should drive the choice as much as looks. Impact-rated products, proper ventilation and a correct installation matter more in Kansas than in milder climates.

Frequently Asked Questions About Metal Roofing in Wichita

Does metal roofing attract lightning more than asphalt in Kansas?

No. Metal roofing does not attract lightning. Lightning strikes the highest point in an area regardless of roofing material. Metal roofing is actually a safer option in a lightning strike because it is non-combustible. Unlike wood or organic-content roofing, metal does not ignite if struck. Wichita’s flat terrain and open landscape make lightning behavior primarily a function of building height, not roofing material.

Can metal roofing be installed over existing asphalt shingles in Wichita?

In many cases, yes. Metal roofing can be installed over one existing layer of asphalt shingles when the decking is structurally sound. This approach avoids tear-off disposal costs and adds an insulating air gap. However, any pre-existing moisture damage, soft spots, or decking rot must be addressed first. A professional inspection determines whether an overlay is appropriate before metal installation begins.

How do I know if my Wichita home’s structure can support metal roofing?

Most residential structures in Wichita can support metal roofing because it is lighter than the asphalt shingles it replaces, not heavier. The structural concern is more relevant when adding materials rather than replacing with comparable or lighter options. A roofer will evaluate decking condition, rafter spacing, and current load during the pre-installation inspection phase to confirm suitability.

Does standing seam metal roofing void my homeowner’s warranty if I add solar panels later?

Standing seam metal roofing accommodates solar panel clamps that attach to the seams without penetrating the panels, which preserves the roof’s watertight integrity. This is actually an advantage over asphalt shingles, where solar installation requires penetration fasteners through the shingle surface. Confirm with your metal roofing manufacturer that clamp-based solar attachments are compatible with the specific panel profile you select.

What maintenance does a metal roof in Wichita actually need?

Annual inspection is advisable after hail seasons. Check for dents that may indicate stone size, look for any sealant at flashings or penetrations that has cracked, and clear debris from valleys and gutters. Exposed-fastener systems warrant fastener inspection every few years. Standing seam systems require minimal maintenance beyond keeping debris cleared and confirming flashing integrity at chimneys, skylights, and roof edges.

What is the best roofing material for Wichita?

For most homes, impact-rated architectural shingles offer the best balance. Metal and synthetic slate are strong premium options.

Are Class 4 shingles worth it?

In hail country they can be. Ask your insurer about premium impacts before you decide.

How long do architectural shingles last?

Many last 20 years or more depending on ventilation, installation and storm exposure.
